P101 · Unit 6 · Lesson 1

Momentum and a chosen system

A trolley may feel harder to stop because it is heavier, because it is moving faster, or both. But “harder to stop” is not yet a quantity: stopping over a longer time can require a smaller average force. We need to separate the motion change from the interaction that produces it.

Allow three hours: 60 minutes for the unit introduction and explanation, 90 minutes for Activities 6.1–6.5, and 30 minutes for feedback and correction. Use a pencil and calculator; no Python is needed.

6.1 Signed momentum

Choose a straight horizontal track and call right positive. For an object of constant mass m, the horizontal momentum is px=mvx. The velocity component carries the direction. A mass is positive, so momentum has the same sign as velocity. In three dimensions the corresponding statement is p=mv: momentum is a vector, not another name for speed.

The SI unit is kilogram metre per second, written kg m/s. Do not use kg m/s²: that is a unit of force. Momentum describes the object's motion at an instant. It need not be changing, and a nonzero momentum does not require a nonzero resultant force. A trolley moving steadily on an ideal horizontal track has momentum even though its horizontal forces balance.

Example 6.1 · Same momentum, different motion

A 0.40 kg trolley A travels right at 3.0 m/s. A 0.60 kg trolley B travels left at 2.0 m/s. In our chosen axis their momenta are

pAx=(0.40)(+3.0)=+1.2 kgm/s.
pBx=(0.60)(−2.0)=−1.2 kgm/s.

The magnitudes are equal, but the vectors are opposite. Their total horizontal momentum is zero. Neither trolley is at rest. A zero total is a cancellation in a signed sum, not a claim that every part has zero motion.

For a system containing several objects, add their momenta using one observation frame and one set of axes. We use capital Px for the system total. Adding speeds first and multiplying by total mass generally fails because the objects may have different velocities. Only when all parts share a velocity can that shortcut represent the sum.

Activity 6.1 · Build the total (15 minutes)

A 0.50 kg trolley moves at +1.6 m/s and a 0.80 kg trolley at −0.50 m/s. Draw their velocity and momentum arrows, calculate each momentum and the total, and explain what the total's sign means. Reverse the coordinate axis and repeat. Does the physical situation change?

Discussion of Activity 6.1

The momenta are +0.80 and −0.40 kg m/s, so Px=+0.40 kg m/s. The resultant momentum points right, although one trolley moves left. Reversing the axis gives −0.80, +0.40 and −0.40 kg m/s. The signs change with the coordinate description; the actual directions do not. Arrow lengths for momentum should use a common momentum scale, distinct from any velocity scale.

6.2 Change over an interval

Label the initial and final instants before subtracting anything. For one constant-mass object,

Δpx=pfx−pix=m(vfx−vix).

The order “final minus initial” matters most when motion reverses. A ball arriving from the left and rebounding towards the left has a positive initial velocity and a negative final velocity in our right-positive axis. Both slowing the incoming motion and establishing the outgoing motion contribute to a negative momentum change.

Example 6.2 · A rebound is not a small slowdown

A 0.20 kg ball approaches a wall at +4.0 m/s and leaves at −3.0 m/s. Thus pix=+0.80 and pfx=−0.60 kg m/s, giving

Δpx=−0.60−(+0.80)=−1.40 kgm/s.

Subtracting the speeds would give a change of −1.0 m/s and the wrong momentum change, −0.20 kg m/s. Speeds hide the reversal. The magnitude of the correct change is larger than the magnitude of either endpoint momentum because the two momentum arrows point in opposite directions.

Momentum depends on the observation frame, just as velocity does. We keep a laboratory frame that can be treated as inertial: Newton’s laws apply without additional frame forces. Do not combine the initial velocity measured from the floor with a final velocity measured from a moving trolley. An apparent imbalance can be a mismatch of descriptions rather than new physics.

Activity 6.2 · Compare three endings (20 minutes)

A 0.30 kg object initially moves at −2.0 m/s. Find its momentum change if its final velocity is (a) zero, (b) −1.0 m/s and (c) +1.0 m/s. Sketch initial and final arrows and explain why all three changes are positive. Which changes involve reversal?

Discussion of Activity 6.2

Initial momentum is −0.60 kg m/s. Final momenta are 0, −0.30 and +0.30 kg m/s, so the changes are +0.60, +0.30 and +0.90 kg m/s. All changes point right because the final velocity is more positive. Only (c) reverses direction; a positive change does not by itself imply a positive final velocity.

6.3 Impulse and average force

For constant mass and a constant resultant force, Newton’s second law gives Fx=max. Multiply by an interval Δt and use axΔt=vfx−vix. The result connects force acting over time with the change we have just calculated:

FxΔt=Δpx.

The force–time product is an impulse. Real impacts rarely have constant force. Here we use the average resultant force over the stated interval, defined as the constant force that would produce the same momentum change in the same time. It is a time average, not automatically the mean of the first and last force readings. Write Jx=F―xΔt=Δpx. A newton second is equivalent to a kilogram metre per second.

Key idea 6.1 · State the interval and the force

For a fixed collection of matter in an inertial frame, the change of total momentum equals the external impulse. In one component, ΔPx=Jext,x=F―ext,xΔt. The average is over that same interval. A short, strong force and a longer, weaker force can have the same impulse; their peak forces need not be equal.

Example 6.3 · Two stopping times

A 1.5 kg model carriage slows from +2.0 m/s to rest. Its momentum change is −3.0 kg m/s. Over 0.10 s, the average resultant force is −30 N; over 0.30 s, it is −10 N. The impulse is −3.0 N s in either case.

These averages alone do not tell us the largest force, the deformation, or the force on an individual part of the carriage. They establish a useful design direction—extend the stopping time for a given momentum change—but are not a complete safety assessment.

The airframe in Figure 6.5 is part of an instrumented test, not an ideal rigid particle. Different parts can stop at different times and deform. Our impulse account describes a chosen total momentum change; understanding the load on a seat or attachment needs additional measurements and a more detailed model. The distinction between a useful average and a complete force history matters in interpreting the apparatus.

Activity 6.3 · An average is not a peak (20 minutes)

A horizontal resultant force on a test object is +12 N for 0.10 s, then +4 N for 0.30 s. Find the impulse in each interval, the total impulse and the average over the full 0.40 s. If the initial momentum is −1.0 kg m/s, find the final momentum. Explain why averaging 12 and 4 gives the wrong full-interval force.

Discussion of Activity 6.3

The impulses are +1.2 and +1.2 N s, giving +2.4 N s in total. The full-interval average is 2.4/0.40=6.0 N. The final momentum is −1.0+2.4=+1.4 kg m/s. The larger force acts for less time; the arithmetic mean 8 N would give equal weight to unequal intervals. This piecewise-constant calculation uses ordinary products and sums, without a continuous-force integral.

6.4 Moving the system boundary

Suppose trolley A pushes trolley B. With A alone as the system, B's force on A crosses the boundary and contributes external impulse. With A and B together, both members of their Newton’s-third-law pair are internal. At each instant those two forces are equal and opposite. Over the same interval their impulses cancel in the sum for A plus B.

This cancellation concerns the total momentum. It does not remove the interaction from either trolley's individual motion. A can lose exactly the momentum B gains while the pair's total stays fixed. Nor does drawing a larger boundary automatically remove every external force: track friction and a hand pushing one trolley remain external unless their agents are included too.

Example 6.4 · An outside push survives the sum

During an encounter, B gives A an impulse of −0.60 N s, and A gives B +0.60 N s. A hand also gives B +0.10 N s. Neglect other horizontal impulses. Then ΔpAx=−0.60 and ΔpBx=+0.70 kg m/s. For A plus B, the changes sum to +0.10 kg m/s: precisely the hand's external impulse.

It would be incorrect to declare momentum conserved just because two objects collide. The system and its surroundings decide which terms cancel.

Activity 6.4 · Draw two boundaries (20 minutes)

Two trolleys interact on a level track. List forces for A alone and for A plus B; include gravity, track support and a small leftward track force on each. Indicate which pairs cancel inside the larger system and which do not. If each track force averages −0.05 N over 0.20 s, find the pair's horizontal momentum change.

Discussion of Activity 6.4

A alone has B's force, weight, support and track resistance. A plus B contains both mutual interaction forces; these cancel internally. Earth and track are outside both chosen boundaries. Vertical support and weight can balance as external forces for each trolley, but they are not a third-law pair. The horizontal external resultant averages −0.10 N, giving ΔPx=−0.020 kg m/s. The mutual contact force need not be known to find this total change.

6.5 When can an impulse be neglected?

“Negligible” means small enough for the question's accuracy, not exactly zero. If a resistive force is bounded in magnitude by 0.05 N over a 0.04 s encounter, its impulse magnitude is at most 0.002 N s. That may be unimportant compared with an individual momentum change of 0.8 kg m/s, but important if the effect we want to resolve is only 0.001 kg m/s.

Choose the interval carefully. An external force that has little effect during a brief collision can have a substantial effect during the two seconds before the next photograph. We should compare states just before and just after the interaction when that is what our approximation describes. Also avoid dividing by a nearly zero total momentum to judge a percentage error: oppositely moving objects can have a tiny total despite substantial individual momenta.

Activity 6.5 · Audit the approximation (15 minutes)

The external horizontal force on two trolleys has magnitude at most 0.08 N. Estimate the maximum impulse over (a) 0.025 s and (b) 1.5 s. The investigation seeks changes of 0.01 kg m/s. Which interval supports neglecting the force at that resolution? Write a qualified conclusion, then explain why “there are no external forces” overstates it.

Discussion of Activity 6.5

The bounds are 0.002 and 0.12 N s. The first is below the stated 0.01 kg m/s resolution; the second is twelve times that scale. Neglecting the impulse during the short interval may be adequate for this purpose, provided the bound is credible. It is not adequate over the longer interval. External forces may still act; their accumulated effect is what the approximation neglects.

We now have the condition needed for the next lesson. When external impulse is negligible, total momentum is approximately unchanged. We can use this to connect the states on either side of a collision, even if the detailed contact force is unknown.
