P101 · Block 2 · Unit 6

Momentum, work and energy balances

Two trolleys meet on a straight track. For a brief moment they push strongly on one another, then separate or travel together. A camera can measure their velocities before and after the encounter even when it cannot resolve the rapidly changing contact force. What can those measurements tell us?

In Units 4 and 5 we followed motion by identifying forces and finding acceleration. Here we reorganise the same mechanics around changes between two states. A force acting over a time interval changes momentum. A force acting through a displacement can change kinetic energy. These two accounts answer different questions, and they do not have the same conservation conditions.

The first two lessons develop a signed momentum account. Choosing both trolleys as one system makes their mutual pushes internal, but it does not make an outside push disappear. We will learn to justify neglecting external impulse, and to say what a collision prediction can and cannot establish. A short Python comparison will check several proposed outcomes against one transparent model.

The next two lessons connect work with kinetic energy, then include gravitational potential energy and dissipative transfers. The central question becomes where energy is stored and how it crosses a chosen boundary. The closing lesson brings the methods together: a useful calculation starts by deciding which balance has the information needed to answer the question.

The visible spray in Figure 6.1 reminds us that the surroundings can move too. Treating the capsule alone, capsule plus water, or a pair of laboratory trolleys means drawing different boundaries. We begin with small, controlled models so that every contribution can be identified before returning to more complicated settings.

Before you begin

You need signed velocity, force diagrams, Newton’s laws and constant-acceleration relations from Unit 4, and the distinction between speed and changing velocity from Unit 5. Retrieve vector components and the dot product from M101 Unit 2. Python work uses the functions, tables and plotting developed in P101 Units 1–3; no calculus or numerical motion solver is required.

Allow 18 hours, including two hours of Python. The five lessons take 3, 4, 4, 4 and 3 hours respectively. Those estimates include this introduction, attempts, feedback, correction, cumulative exercises and reference use. Work across several sittings. The notebook and companion script are alternative ways to do each investigation, not additional assignments.

By the end, you should be able to choose a system and interval, calculate signed momentum and external impulse, justify a collision approximation, calculate constant-force work, construct an energy ledger and test a proposed result by hand and code. You should also be able to explain why agreement with a balance is a necessary check rather than proof that a model captures every feature of an experiment.
