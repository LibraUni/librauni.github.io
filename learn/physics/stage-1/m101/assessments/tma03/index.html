TMA 03: Accumulation, methods and numerical evidence

Integration connects a local description with a total: the area of a cross-section with a volume, a velocity with a change of position, or a sequence of sampled rates with an estimated accumulation. A useful answer also explains the signs, conditions and choices that make the calculation meaningful. This assessment brings those strands together and asks you to examine the evidence behind a numerical result.

Units 8–11 · 100 marks · Six hours. Complete all five questions. The six hours are the separate assessment allocation after Block 3, beyond its 78 study hours.

Before you begin

Complete Units 8–11 and their exercises, including the Block 3 formative checkpoint, iCMA 03. The questions also retrieve algebra, domains, differentiation, one-sided derivatives and sign reasoning from earlier M101 units. Question 5 uses the functions, conditions and loops practised in Unit 11, building on the shared Python foundation introduced in P101. No plotting package is required.

This is open-book work. You may consult the teaching, your notes, a calculator and Python documentation. Write your own reasoning and code. Clarification of wording and installation help are permitted; asking another person or a generative tool to solve, write, rewrite or debug assessed answers is not. Ordinary editor completion of names and syntax is acceptable. Declare any assistance and its extent. If substantive help has already been used, disclose it so a suitable independent follow-up can be agreed.

Label each answer by question and part. Show intermediate reasoning and include units where needed. Typed mathematics and legible handwriting are equally acceptable. Give exact answers where possible; retain full precision in numerical calculations and round only for display. All trigonometric arguments are in radians. Valid alternative methods and equivalent forms receive credit unless a particular method is requested.

Plan the six hours

Allow 15 minutes to read and organise; about 40, 65, 50, 50 and 100 minutes for Questions 1–5; and 40 minutes to check, rerun and prepare your files. These are planning guides, not timed sections. You may work in several sittings. If the budget proves insufficient, retain your partial work and report the time used and unfinished parts.

Start Question 5 in a blank notebook or script. All required inputs are given here. You can download a self-contained copy of these questions for offline reading, or print this page. On narrow screens, long equations and diagrams scroll sideways; focus them and use the arrow keys when reading by keyboard.

Question 1 · An accumulation with a changing rate (16 marks)

Allow about 40 minutes.

The dimensionless function f and its accumulation A are defined by

f(t)={2−t,0≤t≤2,t−3,2<t≤4,
A(x)=∫0xf(t)dt.

The domain of A is [0,4].

  1. Obtain a piecewise formula for A(x) on the whole interval. Show how the contributions on either side of 2 are combined. Use your formula to evaluate ∫40f(t)dt, explaining the effect of reversing the limits. [6]

  2. Determine the global minimum and global maximum of A on [0,4]. Give each value and every point at which it is attained. Justify your answer using the sign of the rate on the relevant intervals and appropriate endpoint comparisons. [5]

  3. Decide whether A is continuous and whether it is differentiable at x=2. Justify both conclusions, using one-sided derivatives or difference quotients for the second. Explain why substituting f(2) into the fundamental theorem alone would not settle the derivative at that point. [5]

Question 2 · Select, calculate and check (24 marks)

Allow about 65 minutes.

For each calculation, make your choice of method and its main intermediate steps visible.

  1. Find the family of antiderivatives

    ∫3x2−2x+1xdx,x>0.

    Show how rewriting the integrand makes the calculation possible. [5]

  2. Evaluate

    ∫016x1+3x2dx

    by substitution. State the new variable, differential and transformed limits, and carry out the evaluation consistently in that variable. [7]

  3. Use integration by parts to find ∫xlnxdx for x>0. Explain your choice of the factor to differentiate, then check your completed antiderivative by differentiating it. [8]

  4. A proposed calculation reads

    ∫−111x2dx=[−1x]−11=−2.

    Identify the domain and theorem-condition failure. Explain why the displayed endpoint subtraction is not justified by the finite-interval integration taught in this block. You are not asked to evaluate an improper integral. [4]

Question 3 · Motion and averages (18 marks)

Allow about 50 minutes.

A particle moves along a straight line. For 0≤t≤5, its velocity is

v(t)=(t−1)(t−4),

where t is the numerical value of time in seconds and v(t) is the numerical value of velocity in metres per second. Its position at t=0 is 3m. Positive velocity means motion in the direction of increasing position.

  1. Calculate the displacement during the five seconds and the final position. Explain how these two quantities are related. [6]

  2. Find the total distance travelled and the average speed over the same interval. Show where you split the calculation and justify the signs used. [7]

  3. Find the mean velocity on [0,5] and every time in (0,5) at which the instantaneous velocity equals that mean. State the condition that guarantees at least one such time and check it for this model. [5]

Question 4 · Capacity and inflow (18 marks)

Allow about 50 minutes.

An ideal vessel has horizontal circular cross-sections. At height z metres above its base, the internal radius in metres is

r(z)=1+z2,0≤z≤2.

The vessel is initially empty. Assume rigid walls, no outlet, no leakage and negligible evaporation.

  1. Starting with the volume of a thin horizontal slice, construct an integral for the vessel’s capacity and evaluate it exactly. Explain the dimensions of the integrand and the resulting units. [6]

  2. The following illustrative inflow readings are recorded during filling. Treat the rate as varying linearly between adjacent readings.

    Table M101-TMA03-1 · Supplied inflow readings
    Time / minInflow rate / m³ min⁻¹
    00.5
    20.8
    50.6
    90.2

    Use the trapezoidal rule with the actual interval widths to estimate the volume admitted by 9min. Show each interval’s contribution. Decide whether the vessel would overflow under this interpolation model, and explain why the tabulated rates alone would not certify the behaviour of every possible flow between the readings. [6]

  3. Calculate the mean inflow rate for the nine-minute interval under the same model. Explain why the unweighted arithmetic mean of the four readings is not the required mean rate. [3]

  4. The instrument is subsequently found to read 0.05m3min−1 too high at every reading. Determine the correction to the estimated admitted volume. Explain whether adding linearly interpolated rows to the uncorrected table would remove this bias. [3]

Question 5 · When numerical agreement misleads (24 marks)

Allow about 100 minutes.

Write an independent Python investigation of composite midpoint and trapezoidal quadrature. Use ordinary Python functions and loops, with the standard math module where useful. Do not copy a complete quadrature implementation from the teaching or use a symbolic-integration or library quadrature routine. You may consult the taught formulas. A labelled plain-text table is sufficient; plots are optional and carry no extra marks.

Throughout this question x and the function values are dimensionless. First consider f(x)=1/(2+x) on [0,1].

  1. Before running the program, evaluate the exact integral of f on [0,1] and calculate the midpoint and trapezoidal estimates with n=2 equal panels by hand. Show the sample positions and weights. Keep these predictions alongside the later outputs. [5]

  2. Starting in a blank file, write reusable midpoint and trapezoidal functions, each taking a function, two bounds and a panel count. Require a positive integer panel count and report an invalid count clearly. Support reversed bounds and equal bounds. You may assume finite bounds and finite function values for valid calls in this investigation; a general-purpose validation library is not required. [5]

  3. Run both rules for f on [0,1] with n=2,4,8,16,32. Tabulate the rule, panel count, estimate and absolute error against your exact integral. Compare with your hand predictions and explain the observed change in error when the panel count doubles. Also test both rules on the constant function c(x)=3 with n=4 over [0,2], from 2 to 0, and from 1 to 1; state the expected results before running those checks. Demonstrate one invalid panel-count call without stopping the rest of the investigation. [5]

  4. Now consider

    g(x)=2+cos(16πx),0≤x≤1.

    Evaluate its integral analytically. Run both rules with n=4,8,16,32 and report estimates and absolute errors. Explain the trapezoidal results for n=4 and n=8 by examining the actual endpoint sample positions in the cosine. Would a stopping rule based only on agreement between these two successive estimates be reliable here? Use the analytical result and your remaining computations to support your conclusion, and identify a useful additional check when an exact integral is unavailable. [6]

  5. Run the whole investigation in a fresh process, or restart the notebook kernel and run all cells in order. Save the outputs, give brief run instructions and the Python version, and conclude by distinguishing implementation checks, observed refinement and a justified claim about accuracy. [3]

How your work will be marked

Marks beside the parts total 100. A private worked marking guide supports the following public criteria.

  • Question 1 (16): correct accumulation across a change of formula, orientation, complete extrema reasoning, and distinction between continuity and differentiability.
  • Question 2 (24): appropriate transformations, correct substitution and limits, a reasoned parts calculation and derivative check, and attention to the conditions of the fundamental theorem.
  • Question 3 (18): distinction between displacement, position and distance; justified signs; meaningful averages and their conditions.
  • Question 4 (18): construction of a slice model, units, use of unequal sampling intervals, interpretation of interpolation, and reasoning about a common instrument bias.
  • Question 5 (24): independently written reusable code, hand and edge-case checks, reproducible output, and an explanation of how sample positions can undermine apparent agreement.

Valid method and reasoning receive credit even when an arithmetic slip affects a later result. A consistent carried-forward error is not repeatedly penalised where the later work remains meaningful. Correct-looking numbers do not replace required explanations or repair an invalid domain. Alternative correct methods receive the same credit except where a specific method is requested. Small floating-point differences between environments are acceptable: interpret your actual outputs rather than an expected last digit.

Feedback will identify secure reasoning, corrections and a focused next step. This assessment introduces no new pass threshold and changes no module weighting. Presentation is assessed for readability, not decoration.

Prepare one legible answer document and your independently written notebook or script with saved outputs and run instructions. PDF is suitable for written work; an executed notebook containing all the mathematics and explanation can serve as the complete answer document if it is readable. Include M101 TMA 03, version 1.0, question labels, an attempt identifier such as attempt-01, references, an assistance declaration and an honest note of time used. Keep an unchanged copy of the files you hand over.

Reading or downloading the assessment does not submit an attempt or change your academic record. No calendar deadline is set by this release; use a separately agreed personal date if you have one.

Before handing over, check that all parts are labelled, signs and units are explained, and the saved numerical outputs come from the final code. Include partial work if a difficulty remains so the feedback can address your reasoning.
