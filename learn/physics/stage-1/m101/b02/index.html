M101 · Block 2

Change and local behaviour

A position tells us where something is. A rate tells us how its position is changing. Moving between those descriptions requires a new idea: the behaviour of a function over intervals that become smaller and smaller.

Block 1 developed vectors and matrices as ways to organise direction and simultaneous relationships. Its central habit was to connect a calculation with what the entries represent. Here we keep that habit while shifting attention from whole transformations to the neighbourhood of a particular input. How much does the output change when the input changes a little? Is there a predictable local pattern, and what might prevent one?

Imagine a position recorded at two nearby times. Their difference, divided by the elapsed time, gives an average velocity. To describe velocity at one instant, we cannot simply set the elapsed time to zero: that would divide by zero. We need to ask what happens to the quotient as the interval shrinks. Unit 4 develops the language that makes this question meaningful. Unit 5 then builds the derivative and practical differentiation methods. Unit 6 uses them to examine rates, turning points and curve shape. Unit 7 studies local linear approximations and numerical derivative checks.

The block contains 72 study hours, including eight hours of Python already within that total. Unit 4 contributes 16 hours and one Python hour. TMA 02 has a separate six-hour allocation after the block. The mathematical work can begin with algebra, elementary functions and the habits developed in Block 1; each computing activity states its P101 preparation. We will not assume that an available programming lesson has already been studied.

Limits, derivatives and approximations are mathematical claims with conditions. A graph can suggest a pattern, and a calculation can test a case, but neither automatically establishes the behaviour at every nearby input. The examples in this block will include failures as well as successes, so that choosing a method includes recognising when its conditions do not hold.
