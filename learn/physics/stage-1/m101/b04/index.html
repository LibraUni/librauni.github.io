M101 · Block 4 · 36 hours, including 8 hours Python

Approximation, validation and mathematical judgement

A calculation can be correct and still answer the wrong question. A graph can look persuasive while hiding the part of a function that matters. Two programs can agree because they share the same mistake. How, then, do we build a reasoned case for trusting an answer?

Throughout M101 you have already made such judgements. You checked a vector against its direction, substituted a solution into simultaneous equations, examined the domain before differentiating and compared numerical integrals with exact ones. This final block makes those habits the centre of the work. The challenge is to choose checks that reveal different aspects of a problem and to explain what their evidence supports.

Unit 12 develops a method for resolving competing answers. You will establish a common question, compare analytical, graphical and numerical evidence, vary an input and repair a flawed check. Unit 13 then brings the module's representations together in a supplied setting involving direction, local change and accumulation. It asks you to transfer the methods and explain your choices.

Bring your earlier work on equations, derivatives, integrals and numerical checks. The block uses these tools in combination; it introduces no new catalogue of integration techniques or differential-equation solvers. Its 36 hours include eight hours of Python and formative practice. The separately allocated revision and final examination follow the block. Your written explanations here will help you identify what you can reconstruct and where further practice is useful.
