M101 · Block 3

Accumulation and integral models

A local rate describes what is happening now. An accumulated quantity records what has happened over an interval.

In Block 2, differentiation let us extract a rate from a changing quantity. We now turn that question around. Given a changing rate, how can we calculate its total contribution? A flow rate can build a delivered volume, a velocity can build a displacement, and contributions distributed along a length can build a total mass. The physical meaning changes, but the mathematical construction has a common form: identify a small contribution, add the contributions, and examine a limit.

Unit 8 develops that construction and connects it to differentiation through the fundamental theorem. Unit 9 then asks how to recognise and calculate suitable antiderivatives. Unit 10 concentrates on translating a question into an integral, with attention to signs, units and assumptions. Unit 11 returns to sums as practical calculations and investigates when their numerical evidence deserves confidence.

The block's 78 hours include 10 hours of Python. TMA 03 has a separate six-hour allocation after the block. The formative checkpoint belongs within Unit 11. Each unit includes its own cumulative practice and reference resources; the later units remain outlines until their teaching is released.

Bring forward the habits developed in Block 2: specify a domain, state the conditions of a theorem, distinguish a numerical observation from a proof, and check the scale and units of an answer. P101 provides the physical theory of its applications. When we use a physical setting here, the required relationship will be supplied. These integral foundations will also support M102's later work on differential equations and probability.
