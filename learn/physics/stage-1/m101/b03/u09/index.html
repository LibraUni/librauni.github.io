M101 · Block 3 · Unit 9

Choosing an integration method

An antiderivative turns an accumulated total into an endpoint calculation. The next question is how to find one—and how to know that the choice is sound.

In Unit 8, the fundamental theorem connected a rate with its accumulated change. Once a function with the right derivative had been found, the calculation became short: evaluate it at two endpoints and subtract. Yet that useful instruction leaves an important task unfinished. A rate may contain a power of another expression, a product, or a quotient. How do we recognise the function whose derivative produced it?

Compare the expressions e2x, xex2 and xex. All contain an exponential, and two contain a factor of x. Their appearance alone does not tell us how to integrate them. We need to look at relationships within each expression. Is a factor the derivative of something inside another function? Would differentiating one factor simplify the problem? Could ordinary algebra reveal a familiar integral before we introduce a new method?

This unit develops that judgement. We begin by strengthening the elementary pairs from Unit 8 and learning to rewrite expressions without losing their domains. Substitution then gives a systematic way to reverse the chain rule. It changes the variable used to describe an accumulation; keeping the differential and the endpoints consistent is part of the method. Integration by parts reverses the product rule and exchanges one integral for another that we can manage more easily.

Finding an answer and checking it are separate parts of the work. Differentiation tests whether a proposed antiderivative has the required rate throughout an interval. Endpoint evaluation then gives the accumulated change. A short Python investigation will help you locate errors and compare expressions, while keeping the distinction between an algebraic identity and agreement at a few inputs clear.

A wider view · Choosing a tool for accumulation

Cartographers have used instruments such as this to obtain areas from maps. An analytical integral starts from a different kind of information: an expression for the quantity being accumulated. Choosing a method depends partly on what information is available. In this unit we exploit the structure of expressions; in Unit 11 we will return to numerical accumulation. The ICE Scotland Museum describes how a polar planimeter is used.

Preparation and study route

You need Unit 8's definite and indefinite integrals, constants, continuity conditions and endpoint evaluation, together with Unit 5's product and chain rules. Keep powers, logarithms and radian trigonometry available. The first lesson retrieves these ideas through calculations; it does not assume that new integration methods are already familiar.

The six lessons each take four hours, for 24 hours including two hours of Python. Lessons 1–5 each reserve 20 minutes for the cumulative exercises in Lesson 6. Together with its own 65-minute allocation, this provides 165 minutes of closing attempts, followed by 65 minutes of feedback and correction. Earlier activity feedback and worked-example reconstruction are budgeted separately. These are study estimates: use the time to explain decisions on paper and repair mistakes, not simply to read displayed answers.

Lessons 5 and 6 each include one hour of computing. Before undertaking that work independently, you should be able to define and call a returning function, loop over a list, import elementary functions from math, and restart and run a notebook using the P101 foundation. The written lessons can be studied first if that preparation is still needed.

By the end, you should be able to justify an elementary method choice, carry a substitution through its limits, use parts purposefully, and reject an answer that fails a derivative or domain check. Unit 10 will use these tools in integral models. Further rational and trigonometric techniques and improper integrals belong to M102; Unit 11 will develop numerical integration. Here we concentrate on making the methods already within reach reliable.
