M101 · Unit 9 · Lesson 5

Selecting and checking a method

Choose a method from the structure you can explain, then test the resulting claim.

We now have several routes to an antiderivative. The challenge is deciding which route makes progress. A good first line does more than name “substitution” or “parts”: it identifies the relationship that will simplify the integral. A good final line shows that the proposed function really has the required derivative.

Four hours: 55 minutes reading, 60 minutes written activities, 45 minutes feedback, 60 minutes Python, and 20 minutes reserved for the closing exercises.

9.17 Compare structure before calculating

Start by simplifying the expression and checking its domain. Then look for an elementary pair or a chain-rule match. If a product remains, ask whether differentiating one factor and integrating the other will reduce it. These questions form a useful order of inspection, but they do not guarantee that every integral will yield to our current methods.

Example 9.16 · Three products, three decisions

Choose and carry out an efficient first method for (a) ∫x(x2+2)dx, (b) ∫xex2+2dx, and (c) ∫xe2xdx.

In (a), expanding gives x3+2x, so the answer is x4/4+x2+C. A substitution u=x2+2 also works, giving (x2+2)2/4+C. Expanding that result shows the two expressions differ only by a constant.

In (b), the inner derivative is 2x. Set u=x2+2, so xdx=du/2, giving ex2+2/2+C. Expanding an exponential would not be an elementary algebraic simplification.

In (c), the exponent's derivative is 2, so substitution does not absorb the outside x. Parts with u=x, dv=e2xdx gives xe2x/2−e2x/4+C, as in Lesson 4. Differentiation verifies all three answers.

More than one valid method can exist. Prefer a route that reduces algebra and makes its checks easy to read. If two answers look different, compare their derivatives and simplify their difference on the same interval before deciding that one is wrong.

Activity 9.17 · Write the reason first

Choose a method and evaluate: (a) ∫(x2+1)/xdx, x>0; (b) ∫x/(x2+1)dx; (c) ∫xlnxdx, x>0; (d) ∫lnx/xdx, x>0. Explain why the location of the factor x changes the decision in (c) and (d).

Discussion of Activity 9.17

(a) Divide to get x+1/x, giving x2/2+lnx+C. (b) Use u=x2+1, giving ln(x2+1)/2+C. (c) Parts differentiates lnx and integrates x, giving x2lnx/2−x2/4+C. (d) Substitution u=lnx absorbs dx/x, leaving ∫udu; the answer is (lnx)2/2+C. Multiplication by x suits the parts reduction in (c); division by x supplies the inner derivative in (d).

9.18 What does an agreement establish?

To verify an antiderivative, establish F′=f throughout the relevant interval. To verify an endpoint calculation, establish the validity of the interval and then check F(b)−F(a). These are different claims. A single correct endpoint difference need not come from a correct antiderivative.

Example 9.17 · Correct endpoints, incorrect rate

The function F(x)=x3 is an antiderivative of f(x)=3x2. A second proposal is G(x)=x3+x(1−x). Compare them on [0,1].

The extra term vanishes at 0 and 1. Both differences therefore equal 1:

F(1)−F(0)=G(1)−G(0)=1.

But G′(x)=3x2+1−2x. The difference between this derivative and the required rate is 1−2x. It vanishes only at x=1/2, rather than throughout the interval. Thus G is not an antiderivative of 3x2, despite giving the correct difference at these endpoints.

This example shows how a check can be too narrowly chosen. Endpoint agreement checks those two values. A derivative check at just the midpoint would also miss the defect. Testing additional points makes that defect visible, while the algebra explains it for every input.

Activity 9.18 · Construct a misleading check

(a) For the same F(x)=x3, explain why H(x)=F(x)+5 is a valid antiderivative of 3x2. (b) Let J(x)=F(x)+x(x−2). Which interval with endpoints 0 and a positive integer gives the same endpoint difference for J and F? (c) Calculate J′−F′, and choose one point at which a derivative comparison exposes the defect.

Discussion of Activity 9.18

(a) A constant has derivative zero. (b) The extra term vanishes at 0 and 2, so use [0,2]; both differences are 8. (c) The derivative discrepancy is 2x−2. At zero it is −2, at 2 it is 2, while at 1 it vanishes. A test only at 1 would fail to detect the error. Unlike a constant shift, this difference changes across the interval.

9.19 A computational audit of endpoint claims

Python is useful for organising several checks consistently. It also makes it easy to repeat a weak check many times. Our investigation begins with a correct analytical baseline and a deliberately incorrect candidate. You will predict what each comparison can reveal, run it, and explain the result.

Download the two-part notebook or the plain Python companion. Complete Part A here; Part B belongs to Lesson 6. The files run with standard Python and math. Before working independently, retrieve P101's returning functions, lists, loops and notebook restart/run-all practice. No numerical integration library is used.

def F(x):
    return x**3

def G(x):
    return x**3 + x*(1-x)

def change(candidate, a, b):
    return candidate(b) - candidate(a)

for a, b in [(0.0, 1.0), (0.0, 0.5), (0.25, 0.75)]:
    print(a, b, change(F, a, b), change(G, a, b))

The function change receives another function as its first argument, calls it at two inputs, and subtracts. You have already used this pattern with numerical derivatives in Unit 7. The loop repeats the comparison for three intervals. Make the predictions by hand before reading the output.

Activity 9.19 · Part A: choose checks that can fail 60 minutes, including feedback

  1. Spend 10 minutes predicting the three endpoint differences for both functions.
  2. Run Part A in a fresh kernel. Compare the output with your predictions. Spend 15 minutes investigating why two different intervals hide the defect.
  3. Spend 15 minutes adding the interval [0.2,0.6] and reversed interval [1,0]. Explain which exposes the defect and which merely checks orientation.
  4. For 10 minutes, compare the supplied analytical derivative functions at 0, 0.5 and 1. Explain the zero discrepancy at the midpoint.
  5. Use the final 10 minutes to write a conclusion: which claims are algebraically established, which cases were tested, and what information the endpoint checks failed to establish. Restart and run all cells to verify reproducibility.
Discussion of Activity 9.19

For [0,1], both differences are 1. For [0,0.5], F gives 0.125 and G gives 0.375. For [0.25,0.75], both give 0.40625 because the added term has value 0.1875 at both ends. On [0.2,0.6], the values are 0.208 and 0.288, exposing the difference. Both give −1 on the reversed full interval; this checks subtraction order but still hides the defective derivative.

The analytical derivative discrepancies at 0, 0.5 and 1 are 1, 0 and −1. These functions encode derivatives you first calculate on paper; Python has not symbolically differentiated anything. The algebra G′−3x2=1−2x establishes that G fails as an antiderivative on the whole interval. Decimal output may contain small binary floating-point rounding differences.

9.20 Finish with a justified claim

A complete integration solution states the domain, gives the reason for its method, carries the calculation through, and checks the result. For a definite integral it also states the bounds and the resulting number; for an indefinite integral it includes the constant. These habits let another reader follow the argument and locate a disagreement.

Sometimes the immediate methods do not simplify an integral. That is a useful diagnosis if it is stated accurately. For ∫ex2dx, the substitution u=x2 does not supply the missing factor 2x. This shows that the simple chain-rule match fails. It does not show that the continuous function lacks an accumulation function: Unit 8 constructs one as ∫0xet2dt. Our finite collection of formula methods is not the definition of integration.

Activity 9.20 · Review a proposed solution

A learner writes: “For ∫01xex2dx, choose parts because there are two factors. Let u=ex2, dv=xdx. This gives a more complicated integral, so the original integral cannot be evaluated.” Explain the two errors of reasoning, then give a complete solution.

Discussion of Activity 9.20

The presence of a product does not determine the method; a failed choice does not establish impossibility. The inner derivative of x2 is proportional to the factor x, so substitution is efficient. With u=x2, xdx=du/2, and bounds 0 and 1, the result is 12[eu]01=(e−1)/2. The antiderivative ex2/2 differentiates to xex2, and the integrand is continuous and nonnegative on the interval.

You now have a method-selection argument and several ways to test it. The final lesson combines them in independent mixed practice and a second computational audit, then collects the results worth keeping to hand.
