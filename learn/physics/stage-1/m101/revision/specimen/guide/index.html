How to use the guide

Keep this separate from the question paper during your attempt. Each solution below explains the mathematical route and the meaning of the result. Open one question at a time, mark the evidence you actually wrote, and annotate a correction in a different colour. A correct solution read after the sitting is useful learning, but it is not evidence that you produced that reasoning independently.

Allocate the 100 whole marks by the numbered criteria below. Each criterion is worth one mark; there are no discretionary presentation marks, negative marks or half marks. Award a criterion once wherever the relevant work appears, and cross-reference it if it is in another part. A correct unrequested method earns the mathematical criteria it actually demonstrates, subject to explicit method requirements below. The worked solution is one route, not a required wording.

Accept exact equivalent forms and sensible rounding. Unless the question specifies accuracy, accept at least three significant figures for approximate results when that supports the conclusion. A correct answer alone earns only result criteria; do not infer unseen derivations, checks or explanations. Legible diagrams may communicate reasoning. Ignore crossed-out work unless clearly reinstated; where two uncancelled contradictory answers remain, award only criteria unambiguously supported and record the conflict.

Follow-through (FT) preserves a subsequent valid method after a local arithmetic slip only in the cases described below. Record the original error and each FT criterion. Do not carry through an invalid domain, zero division, wrong operation, missing sign interpretation or an alleged verification that fails. Do not subtract an additional penalty after withholding the affected criteria. Criterion totals and outcome judgements are recorded separately.

Practice evidence. These marks help diagnose revision needs and do not count towards M101 completion. Use the worked solutions with the marking clarifications below.

The O1–O6 labels identify each mark’s primary outcome. Fluency, connected reasoning and transfer describe the demand of the question.

Q1. Vectors and geometric meaning · 12 marks

Q1a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Subtract the starting position from the finishing position:
d=b−a=(−3,4,0)m,|d|=(−3)2+42+02=5m.
The magnitude is the straight-line separation of the endpoints. A route between them could be longer; no route has been specified.
  1. Q1a.1 · 1 mark · O3 · fluency: Uses B minus A
  2. Q1a.2 · 1 mark · O3 · fluency: Displacement (−3,4,0) m
  3. Q1a.3 · 1 mark · O3 · fluency: Euclidean norm evaluated as 5 m
  4. Q1a.4 · 1 mark · O3 · connected: Identifies straight-line endpoint separation

Marking decisions and alternatives. A reversed displacement loses criterion 2, but its correctly calculated norm and endpoint-separation interpretation can earn 3–4. A bare correct displacement earns 2 only unless the subtraction is shown or unambiguously indicated. Units are required on the final displacement and magnitude; do not penalise the same omitted length unit twice: withhold criterion 3 only.

u·v|v|=−13.
The component points opposite to v. Both vectors are nonzero, so their negative scalar product also establishes an obtuse angle. A projection magnitude alone would discard the requested sign.

Q2. Transformations and linear systems · 12 marks

Q2a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
First Sp=(1,−1)T, then RSp=(1,1)T. In the other order Rp=(1,2)T and SRp=(3,2)T. The two outputs for the same input differ, so these transformations do not commute; the rightmost matrix acts first.
  1. Q2a.1 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: Sp=(1,−1)
  2. Q2a.2 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: RSp=(1,1)
  3. Q2a.3 · 1 mark · O4 · connected: SRp=(3,2)
  4. Q2a.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Uses unequal outputs to explain order

Marking decisions and alternatives. Criterion 2 follows through a single arithmetic slip in Sp if R is then correctly applied. Computing SRp in place of RSp cannot earn 2; it can earn 3 if explicitly identified. Criterion 4 requires a correct ordering argument, not merely the word “different”.

Q2b · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Adding the equations eliminates y and gives 3x=6. Hence x=2 and y=1. Substitution gives 2(2)+1=5 and 2−1=1, so the pair satisfies both requirements.
  1. Q2b.1 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: Valid elimination giving 3x=6 or equivalent
  2. Q2b.2 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: x=2
  3. Q2b.3 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: y=1
  4. Q2b.4 · 1 mark · O4 · connected: Checks both original equations

Marking decisions and alternatives. Elimination is requested: a solver-only answer earns the accurate x and y marks, but not criterion 1 or an absent check. A correct equivalent elimination earns 1. Follow through one arithmetic slip into a correctly recovered second variable (criterion 3); criterion 4 requires a truthful check of both originals, which may identify and correct the slip. Do not award a false claim that a wrong pair satisfies them.

Q2c · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
If λ≠0, the second equation fixes y=μ/λ, and the first fixes x=3−2μ/λ. If λ=0, μ≠0, it instead asserts 0=μ, an impossibility. If both are zero, the second equation adds no restriction:
(x,y)=(3−2t,t),t∈R.
A zero coefficient alone does not distinguish the last two cases; the right-hand side decides whether the row is a contradiction or an identity.
  1. Q2c.1 · 1 mark · O4 · fluency: Unique if λ≠0
  2. Q2c.2 · 1 mark · O4 · connected: None if λ=0 and μ≠0
  3. Q2c.3 · 1 mark · O4 · connected: Infinite if λ=μ=0 with family (3−2t,t)
  4. Q2c.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Justifies fixed variable / contradiction / free variable distinctions

Marking decisions and alternatives. Criterion 3 requires the parameter family as well as the zero/zero condition. A statement that determinant zero always means no solution loses the relevant classification/reasoning marks. Equivalent parametrisations are valid. Criterion 4 requires reasons for all three cases.

Q3. Limits and differentiation · 14 marks

Q3a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The original denominator excludes 2. For x≠2, cancellation gives f(x)=x+2, so the limit is 4. The original function is not continuous at 2 because it is undefined there. Defining the extended function's value to be 4 makes it agree with the limit.
  1. Q3a.1 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Domain all real x except 2
  2. Q3a.2 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Cancellation valid for x≠2 and limit 4
  3. Q3a.3 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Not continuous at 2 because undefined
  4. Q3a.4 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Extension value 4

Marking decisions and alternatives. Domain and continuity are separate demands. A numerical limit 4 alone earns criterion 2 only if cancellation or equivalent valid limiting reasoning is supplied. An assertion that cancellation defines the original function at 2 loses criterion 3 and flags O1a for review; it need not erase valid algebra elsewhere.

Q3b · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Fix x≠0. During cancellation take h≠0 and x+h≠0. Then
g(x+h)−g(x)h=1x+h−1xh=−1x(x+h).
For fixed nonzero x, sufficiently small h keeps the denominator nonzero. Taking the limit gives g′(x)=−1/x2. Setting h to zero before cancellation would leave the original quotient undefined.
  1. Q3b.1 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Correct difference quotient
  2. Q3b.2 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Simplifies to −1/[x(x+h)]
  3. Q3b.3 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Limit derivative −1/x²
  4. Q3b.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Explains nonzero x,h,x+h and limit after simplification

Marking decisions and alternatives. A recalled derivative without a difference quotient earns criterion 3 only. Criterion 2 requires valid quotient simplification. Criterion 4 requires x≠0, h≠0, x+h≠0 and taking the limit after cancellation; equivalent “sufficiently small nonzero h” reasoning is accepted. Do not follow through division by zero.

Q4. Using a derivative · 12 marks

Open worked solutions and complete marking criteria for Q4

Q4a · 7 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The derivative 3x2−3 vanishes at −1 and 1. Its signs are positive, negative, positive across these points, so −1 is a local maximum and 1 a local minimum. Alternatively f″(x)=6x gives the same classifications. The values are
f(−2)=−2,f(−1)=2,f(1)=−2,f(3)=18.
The global maximum is 18 at x=3, and the global minimum is −2 at both x=−2 and x=1. Continuity on the closed interval ensures extrema exist; differentiability in its interior means any interior extremum is stationary. Comparing both endpoints with both stationary values is therefore complete.
  1. Q4a.1 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Derivative 3x²−3
  2. Q4a.2 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Interior roots −1 and 1
  3. Q4a.3 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Correct local classifications with derivative evidence
  4. Q4a.4 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Both endpoint values −2 and 18
  5. Q4a.5 · 1 mark · O1 · fluency: Both stationary values 2 and −2
  6. Q4a.6 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Global maximum/minimum with all locations
  7. Q4a.7 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Continuity/closed interval and interior differentiability justify candidate list

Marking decisions and alternatives. Criterion 3 accepts a sign table or second-derivative test with evaluated signs. Criterion 6 needs both extrema and every attainment point. A local classification alone cannot earn the global mark. For a single arithmetic value slip, criterion 6 can follow through a consistent comparison of all four candidates; annotate FT and do not accept a comparison that contradicts their displayed values. Criterion 7 remains independent.

Q4b · 5 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
At zero, g(0)=2 and g′(0)=1/4. The line is L(x)=2+x/4, giving g(0.1)≈2.025. The real domain is x≥−4, so −3.9 is allowed, but is far from the base point and close to the domain boundary. The exact-function calculation gives
g(−3.9)=0.1≈0.316228
. The error is about 0.709, far larger than 0.01. Membership of the domain does not ensure that a local approximation is accurate.
  1. Q4b.1 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Base value 2 and slope 1/4
  2. Q4b.2 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Tangent line 2+x/4
  3. Q4b.3 · 1 mark · O1 · connected: Estimate 2.025
  4. Q4b.4 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Domain x≥−4 and explains large step/local limitation
  5. Q4b.5 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Calculator comparison ≈0.316228 rejects error claim

Marking decisions and alternatives. One arithmetic slope slip may carry into the constructed line and its 0.1 evaluation (criteria 2–3), provided the tangent method is valid. Criteria 4–5 assess the original function and stated proposal, not the candidate’s altered line. “Outside the domain” at −3.9 is false. Accept reasonable calculator rounding (e.g. 0.316 and error 0.709).

Q5. Integration and the fundamental theorem · 16 marks

Open worked solutions and complete marking criteria for Q5

Q5a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
An integral with equal limits is zero, so A(1)=0. On any closed interval with positive endpoints, ln t is continuous; the fundamental theorem therefore gives A′(x)=lnx throughout x>0. Since ln x<0 on (0,1), A is decreasing there. The derivative describes the change in A, not its sign.
  1. Q5a.1 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: A(1)=0
  2. Q5a.2 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: A′(x)=ln x
  3. Q5a.3 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Continuity on positive integration intervals supports FTC
  4. Q5a.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Decreasing because ln x<0 on (0,1)

Marking decisions and alternatives. Continuity must cover the positive interval between the endpoints, not include t=0. Criterion 4 needs the negative logarithm/derivative argument, not just “decreasing”. A claim that A is negative because it decreases is a material confusion even if the decreasing mark is earned; flag rather than invent a deduction.

Q5b · 6 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Set u=1+x2, so du=2xdx. The original endpoints x=0 and x=1 become u=1 and u=2. Thus
∫012x1+x2dx=∫121udu=[lnu]12=ln2.
The new interval is positive, so ln u is an antiderivative throughout it. If returning to x before evaluation, the original limits belong with the x expression.
  1. Q5b.1 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: u=1+x²
  2. Q5b.2 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: du=2x dx
  3. Q5b.3 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Transformed endpoints 1 and 2
  4. Q5b.4 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Transformed integral ∫₁²1/u du
  5. Q5b.5 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Antiderivative ln u on positive interval
  6. Q5b.6 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Evaluation ln 2

Marking decisions and alternatives. Each listed step is one mark. A valid substitution evaluated back in x can earn criteria 1,2,5,6, but loses 3–4 if transformed limits and the new-variable definite integral are absent. A direct reverse-chain solution without an explicit substitution earns 5–6 only; this is a specified-method task. An isolated endpoint arithmetic slip can carry through criterion 6 if bounds, transformed integrand and antiderivative are conceptually correct. Reusing x bounds with u is not that slip.

Q5c · 6 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Choose u=x and dv=cos x dx, giving du=dx and v=sin x. The product rule in reverse gives
∫xcosxdx=xsinx−∫sinxdx=xsinx+cosx+C.
For the check, differentiating the first term gives sin x+x cos x, while differentiating cos x gives −sin x. Their sum is the original integrand. The constant represents the family of antiderivatives on the real line.
  1. Q5c.1 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Chooses u=x and dv=cos x dx
  2. Q5c.2 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: du=dx and v=sin x
  3. Q5c.3 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Correct parts expression x sin x−∫sin x dx
  4. Q5c.4 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Answer x sin x+cos x+C
  5. Q5c.5 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Differentiates product as sin x+x cos x
  6. Q5c.6 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Cancels sine terms and verifies original integrand

Marking decisions and alternatives. Equivalent parts choices are accepted if carried through correctly. Criterion 4 requires the arbitrary constant; omitting it loses this mark only. Criterion 5 can be earned despite a wrong sign on cos x. Criterion 6 requires a successful check or an identified correction to the answer, not a claim of agreement contradicted by the derivative. Do not follow through a failed check as if it verified the integrand.

Q6. Signed energy transfer · 12 marks

Q6a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The requested change is
ΔE=∫03(3t2−12t+9)dt.
Power multiplied by time has units watt seconds, or joules. Keeping the sign subtracts energy leaving from energy entering. Taking an absolute value at this stage would answer a different question.
  1. Q6a.1 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Uses signed-power integral
  2. Q6a.2 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Both limits 0 and 3
  3. Q6a.3 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Units J or W s
  4. Q6a.4 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Explains inflow adds and outflow subtracts

Marking decisions and alternatives. The integrand, bounds, units and sign explanation are distinct. The same correct setup may appear in part (b); award once in part (a). Writing |P| earns no signed-integral criterion 1 but can still earn correct limits and units. No force/work law or other physics is needed.

Q6b · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
An antiderivative is t3−6t2+9t. At the upper endpoint it is 27−54+27=0, and at the lower endpoint it is 0. Therefore the net energy change is 0 J. The device ends with the same energy as it began, although energy has passed in both directions during the interval.
  1. Q6b.1 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Antiderivative t³−6t²+9t
  2. Q6b.2 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Correct endpoint subtraction
  3. Q6b.3 · 1 mark · O2 · fluency: Net change 0 J
  4. Q6b.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Interprets zero net without claiming no transfer

Marking decisions and alternatives. Criterion 2 rewards a correct upper-minus-lower evaluation using a valid antiderivative; follow through one arithmetic coefficient slip from criterion 1 if the integration method is otherwise sound. Criterion 3 is the accurate net change; criterion 4 may follow through a nonzero arithmetic result if its sign is correctly interpreted, but a claim of no energy transfer from zero net is never accepted. Units already rewarded in (a): accept a clearly attached J there without another penalty.

Q6c · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Factor P(t)=3(t−1)(t−3). It is positive on (0,1) and negative on (1,3). The antiderivative in part (b) has value 4 at t=1 and zero at both 0 and 3. Hence
∫03|P(t)|dt=∫01P(t)dt−∫13P(t)dt=4−(−4)=8J.
Four joules enter and four leave. They cancel in net change but add in the total transferred.
  1. Q6c.1 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Zero at 1 and sign positive before / negative after
  2. Q6c.2 · 1 mark · O2 · transfer: Correct split with negative sign on second integral
  3. Q6c.3 · 1 mark · O2 · connected: Total 8 J
  4. Q6c.4 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Explains cancellation in net versus addition of magnitudes

Criterion 3 follows through one arithmetic evaluation slip with a correct absolute-value split and positive total. Merely taking |net change| is an invalid method and earns neither 2 nor 3. Criterion 4 is independent conceptual evidence.

Q7. Numerical evidence and code interpretation · 12 marks

Q7a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The derivative at 1 is 2, so 2.1 is closer than 2.2. Exact algebra gives
(1+h)2−1h=2+h,h≠0,
so the forward-difference error is h in exact arithmetic. In the stated rounded calculation, the numerator is stored as zero; division then returns zero. Reducing h reduces the exact discretisation error, but may expose subtraction/rounding loss; it does not guarantee a better computed derivative.
  1. Q7a.1 · 1 mark · O5 · fluency: Derivative 2 and chooses 2.1
  2. Q7a.2 · 1 mark · O5 · connected: Exact forward difference 2+h explains error
  3. Q7a.3 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Identifies rounded zero numerator
  4. Q7a.4 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Explains trade-off; smaller step not guaranteed better

Marking decisions and alternatives. Criterion 2 accepts the derived quotient or a correct explicit statement that exact forward error is h with supporting algebra. The table alone does not prove this identity. Criterion 3 must locate loss in the rounded numerator. An asserted universal rule that smaller h is better cannot earn criterion 4 and flags O5a.

Q7c · 5 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The loop visits x=0,1,2 and adds 1,0,1, leaving totals 1,1,2; the printed value is 2.0. This weights both endpoints fully, whereas a composite trapezoidal sum gives each outer endpoint half weight. A correction is total = h * (0.5 * 1 + 0 + 0.5 * 1), giving 1.0. This corrects the quadrature implementation, not its approximation error for the curved function.
  1. Q7c.1 · 1 mark · O6 · fluency: First two totals 1,1 with visited nodes
  2. Q7c.2 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Third total 2
  3. Q7c.3 · 1 mark · O6 · fluency: Printed value 2 or 2.0
  4. Q7c.4 · 1 mark · O6 · connected: Explains double/full outer endpoint weighting
  5. Q7c.5 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Correct half-weight expression

Marking decisions and alternatives. No execution evidence is required. For criterion 1 include x=0,1 and totals 1,1 (a trace table suffices). Criterion 2 follows a consistent single arithmetic slip in earlier running total; criterion 3 may follow that trace if it identifies what print displays. Criterion 4 must identify full weights at both outer endpoints. Accept any equivalent correct expression or loop, including total = h*(0.5*f(0)+f(1)+0.5*f(2)) with f defined as (x−1)². A changed numerical answer alone earns no correction mark.

Q8. A detector calibration decision · 10 marks

Open worked solutions and complete marking criteria for Q8

Q8a · 4 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The spot is 3 mm along the positive first axis and 4 mm along the positive second axis. Its original distance is 5 mm. The calibrated coordinates are (3,8) mm, whose norm is 73 mm. A rotation preserves distances, so this scaling of the second coordinate is not a rotation.
  1. Q8a.1 · 1 mark · O3 · transfer: Interprets both signed coordinate components
  2. Q8a.2 · 1 mark · O3 · transfer: Original magnitude 5 mm
  3. Q8a.3 · 1 mark · O4 · transfer: Sr=(3,8) mm
  4. Q8a.4 · 1 mark · O4 · transfer: Rejects rotation using changed magnitude

Marking decisions and alternatives. Use column-vector convention; equivalent ordered pairs accepted. Criterion 4 requires evidence that a rotation would preserve magnitude, not just naming “stretch”. A nonzero computed vector with an isolated multiplication slip may support this reasoning if its norm differs from 5. It cannot support a false general conclusion that rotations change length.

Q8b · 2 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
The instantaneous change in the rate is q′(1), measured in counts per second squared. The accumulated expected count is ∫02q(t)dt, measured in counts. An expected count may be nonintegral. These quantities describe change of a rate and accumulation of a rate, respectively.
  1. Q8b.1 · 1 mark · O1 · transfer: q′(1) for rate of change
  2. Q8b.2 · 1 mark · O2 · transfer: ∫₀²q(t)dt for accumulation

Marking decisions and alternatives. No evaluation or units are required for these two marks, but supplied units must not contradict the selected quantities without review. A derivative for accumulated count is a substantive misconception, not a notation slip.

Q8c · 2 marks

Read the question

Worked solution
Changing gain changes the model input and predicted rate, while changing grid spacing changes the numerical representation of the same fixed model. An analytic integral checks the original count independently:
∫02(2+t)dt=[2t+t2/2]02=6.
This uses the supplied model; it does not verify the gain experimentally. Exact trapezoidal agreement is expected for a linear rate, but agreement by itself is not a general accuracy guarantee.
  1. Q8c.1 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Distinguishes model-input change from numerical resolution
  2. Q8c.2 · 1 mark · O5 · transfer: Independent analytic integral or justified linear-area calculation

Marking decisions and alternatives. A repeat with smaller panels is not independent evidence of the vulnerable quadrature step. Accept a trapezium area calculated from the linear graph or a justified average-rate-times-duration calculation. No new measurement need be collected during the examination.

Worked solution
The revised prediction 6.30 lies in the measured interval whereas 6.00 lies below it, so the revised model is more consistent with this observation. One interval comparison does not establish the model uniquely or prove its correctness. The matching grids only support calculation of the chosen model; additional independent measurements would test its wider adequacy.
  1. Q8d.1 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Uses interval to compare both predictions
  2. Q8d.2 · 1 mark · O6 · transfer: Qualified judgement: consistency, not proof from one observation/grid agreement

Marking decisions and alternatives. The first mark needs comparison of both predictions with the interval. The second requires a reason why one observation or grid agreement is insufficient for proof; “not proven” alone is too thin. No statistical test or probability calculation is expected.

Turn the marking into a study decision

Separate three kinds of correction: an isolated calculation slip, a method you could not select, and a conclusion whose meaning or conditions you misunderstood. For each substantial gap, return to the relevant unit and then retry the question without the solution visible. Use earlier TMA feedback to check whether the gap is new or recurring. The numerical score helps locate the performance; it does not replace this diagnosis.
