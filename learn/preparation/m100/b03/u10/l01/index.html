LEARNING MATERIALS

Vector components and direction

A vector has magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude without direction. Add and subtract vectors component by component. For (x,y), magnitude is √(x²+y²). A vector of magnitude r at angleθ has components (r cosθ,r sinθ). Unit vectors have magnitude1; divide a non-zero vector by its magnitude to obtain its direction unit vector. The zero vector has no unique direction. Use atan2(y,x), rather than arctan(y/x), to preserve quadrant information.

SECTION 1 · ABOUT 180 MINUTES

Understand the relationship

A vector has magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude without direction. Add and subtract vectors component by component. For (x,y), magnitude is √(x²+y²). A vector of magnitude r at angleθ has components (r cosθ,r sinθ). Unit vectors have magnitude1; divide a non-zero vector by its magnitude to obtain its direction unit vector. The zero vector has no unique direction. Use atan2(y,x), rather than arctan(y/x), to preserve quadrant information.

Worked example

For a=(3,4) and b=(−1,2), a+b=(2,6), a−b=(4,2), and |a|=5. The unit direction of a is(3/5,4/5). A displacement magnitude is not the sum of component magnitudes.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Find the magnitude of(−5,12).
  2. Resolve magnitude10 at30° into components.
  3. Explain why(−1,1) and(1,−1) cannot have the same direction despite equal y/x.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. 13.
  2. (5√3,5).
  3. They lie in different quadrants; the ratio alone loses that information.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.
