Two unknowns, two relations

Solve two linear equations by substitution and elimination, compare methods and interpret units.

4 hours of planned study, including explanation, handwritten practice, checks and any Python work below. Take breaks within your timetable. Attempt each practice set before revealing feedback; use the remaining practice time to explain corrections and retry without the answer visible.

SECTION 1 · ABOUT 84 MINUTES

Two constraints for two unknowns

One linear relation involving two unknowns usually leaves many possibilities. A second independent relation can determine a unique pair. Substitution uses one equation to express an unknown and inserts that expression into the other. The recovered pair must satisfy both original equations. Keep names and units consistent so that the second relation really refers to the same quantities.

Worked example

Solve x + y = 7 and 2x − y = 2. From the first, y = 7 − x. Substitute: 2x − (7 − x) = 2, so 3x = 9 and x = 3. Then y = 4. Checks: 3 + 4 = 7 and 6 − 4 = 2.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Solve x + y = 9 and x − y = 1 by substitution.
  2. Solve y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 10.
  3. List two different pairs satisfying x + y = 7 alone.
  4. Explain why satisfying only the first equation is insufficient.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. x = 5, y = 4.
  2. 3x + 1 = 10, so x = 3, y = 7.
  3. For example (0, 7) and (2, 5).
  4. The solution must lie in the set satisfying both constraints, not just one.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.

SECTION 2 · ABOUT 96 MINUTES

Choose a method and verify both relations

Elimination adds or subtracts suitable multiples of equations to remove one unknown. Multiplying an entire equation by a non-zero number preserves its solutions. Choose a multiple that makes one pair of coefficients opposite, then add. After finding one unknown, substitute into an original equation for the other. Substitution and elimination are alternative routes to the same pair; choose the route that keeps arithmetic manageable.

Worked example

For 2x + 3y = 13 and 3x − 2y = 0, multiply the first by 2 and the second by 3: 4x + 6y = 26 and 9x − 6y = 0. Add: 13x = 26, so x = 2. Then y = 3. Both originals check.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Solve 2x + y = 8 and x − y = 1 by elimination.
  2. Solve 3x + 2y = 16 and x − 2y = 0.
  3. Solve 2x + 3y = 1 and 4x − 3y = 11.
  4. For y = 3x − 2 and 2x + y = 8, explain which method is convenient and solve.
  5. Explain why multiplying only the left side of an equation is invalid.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. 3x = 9, so x = 3, y = 2.
  2. 4x = 16, so x = 4, y = 2.
  3. 6x = 12, so x = 2, y = −1.
  4. Substitution is convenient because y is isolated; 5x − 2 = 8 gives x = 2, y = 4.
  5. It changes the constraint rather than preserving equality.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.

SECTION 3 · ABOUT 60 MINUTES

Infer two quantities from measurements

Two supplied measurements can determine the two parameters of a simple model. Treat the values as exact for this exercise; real measurement uncertainty would need additional analysis. In s = s₀ + vt, an observation at each of two different times gives two equations for s₀ and v. Subtracting removes the offset and isolates the change over the elapsed time.

Worked example

At t = 1 s, s = 7 m; at t = 3 s, s = 11 m. Thus s₀ + v × 1 s = 7 m and s₀ + v × 3 s = 11 m. Subtract: v × 2 s = 4 m, so v = 2 m/s and s₀ = 5 m.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Use observations (2 s, 9 m) and (5 s, 18 m) to recover v and s₀.
  2. Check both measurements using your recovered parameters.
  3. Predict s at 4 s under the same model.
  4. Why would two measurements at the same time fail to determine a velocity uniquely?
Feedback and worked answers
  1. Subtracting gives v = 3 m/s; s₀ = 3 m.
  2. 3 + 3 × 2 = 9 and 3 + 3 × 5 = 18, in metres.
  3. 15 m.
  4. They provide no time separation: identical values repeat one constraint, while differing exact values contradict the model.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.
