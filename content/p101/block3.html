Oscillations, waves and light

A motion can return to where it began without its story being over. A vibrating object repeats a pattern; a wave carries a disturbance onwards; light brings evidence from a source to an observer. This block asks how those patterns can be described and what an observation can recover from them.

In Block 2 you used forces to explain changes of motion and energy balances to connect states. Here those ideas meet repeated motion. A restoring interaction can pull an object towards equilibrium while its inertia carries it through. The resulting oscillation has a timescale and a size. Describing these separately will let us compare a slow swing with a rapidly vibrating object using the same mathematical language.

Unit 8 develops that language: amplitude, period, frequency and phase. You will interpret a supplied sinusoidal model and explore how damping and driving change a response. Then you will sample the model at discrete times. A sequence of numbers is an observation of selected instants; learning to recognise what happened between them is part of the physics, not just a plotting choice.

Unit 9 extends the description from one oscillating quantity to a disturbance spread through space. A snapshot supplies wavelength; a record at one location supplies period. Their connection gives wave speed. Superposition then explains how two contributions can reinforce or cancel, and why some patterns appear to stand still. Unit 10 uses wave and ray pictures to discuss light, image formation and the limits of an observation.

The block contains 54 study hours, including eight hours of Python. Its calculations use algebra, graphs and explicitly supplied trigonometric models. The derivation of oscillator solutions from differential equations and a full theory of waves belong to later study. Required work here remains self-contained. You will use the functions, arrays and labelled plots established in Block 1, with hand checks and reproducible runs throughout.

Keep asking three questions: what physical quantity is varying, which features does the model predict, and what does the available record actually establish? These questions guide the block’s investigations and the separate six-hour TMA 03 that follows Unit 10.
