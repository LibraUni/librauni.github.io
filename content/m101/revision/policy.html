1. Completion rule

M101 completion should establish both a usable foundation for later mathematics and an independently demonstrated ability to reason. A weighted mark describes the overall performance; an explicit outcome review checks that this average has not concealed a substantial gap. This policy combines those two forms of evidence within the existing three TMAs and final examination.

Adopted rule

A student completes M101 when all three TMAs and the final examination have valid reviewed attempts, the TMA average is at least 40%, the examination score is at least 40%, and all six outcomes—including independent Python—are demonstrated through the evidence requirements below. Required moderation and any assessment-circumstances review must be resolved.

Weight the examination at 50% and the three TMAs together at 50%, with equal TMA weights. Report a module score, a completion decision and an outcome evidence profile. A numerical threshold by itself does not confer completion. A student who meets the numerical requirements but has an unresolved outcome is recorded as not yet complete: evidence required, with the specific gap and route to resolve it.

Adopted prospectively on 29 October 2026 from the calibrated v0.3 proposal. No mark, learner record or sitting is created by this policy.

2. Weights, thresholds and numerical decisions

Let the three current valid TMA scores be T1, T2 and T3, each out of 100, and the current valid examination score be E. The coursework score C is their arithmetic mean. The module score M gives equal weight to C and E:

C=T1+T2+T33

M=C+E2.

Require C ≥ 40 and E ≥ 40. These two requirements already imply M ≥ 40; the overall threshold is a summary, not a third independent hurdle. Use exact weights, not rounded 16.7% weights. Compare unrounded values with the thresholds and display the module score to one decimal place, rounding half up. A displayed 40.0 does not change an underlying value below 40 into a pass. Marking corrections and moderation occur before the comparison and retain an audit trail.

There is no separate numerical minimum for an individual TMA. Limited compensation within coursework is allowed only when all three assessments have been attempted validly and the required outcome evidence is present. A high exam score cannot compensate for a coursework average below 40; strong coursework cannot compensate for an exam score below 40. No assignment is dropped or treated as an optional “best two”.

A missing assessment has no score and prevents a completion decision. Do not insert zero merely because a file has not arrived. A valid marked attempt can legitimately earn zero, including a script that establishes no assessable answer; that score is retained and the outcome gaps remain visible. Work affected by unresolved technical circumstances or assistance concerns is pending review, not automatically failed or treated as valid independent evidence.

Why these choices? The three equal six-hour TMAs provide sustained work, checks and executable code; the examination provides a common cross-module test under restricted conditions. A 50:50 split gives each form substantial influence. Equal TMA weights follow their equal assessment budgets, while their differing subject scopes are protected by the outcome requirements. The adopted 40% component floor allows partial-credit marking to reflect sound but incomplete performance. It is an original policy choice, not a rule adopted from another university or proof of an academic standard. Complete marking guides and constructed borderline responses have been checked for internal consistency. Adjust tasks and criteria transparently before assessment, never after seeing a particular learner’s mark.

No distinction, honours band, degree classification or comparative ranking is created here. Report the actual score and whether the M101 completion requirements have been met. Later programme classification requires its own approved policy.

These twelve indicators unpack the existing six outcomes; they add no new topic or separate mandatory test. Each must be demonstrated: the student chooses and explains an appropriate method, shows sufficient intermediate reasoning, carries it through with enough accuracy to support the interpretation, and satisfies the indicator under the permitted assistance conditions. Minor isolated slips are compatible with this judgement when the method and meaning remain sound. A consequential unresolved misconception is not.

Use a short evidence record for each indicator: assessment and version, question/part, relevant reasoning or code, conditions, judgement and reason. “Partial” identifies a specific incomplete demonstration; “not evidenced” identifies missing or unsuitable evidence. Neither is a judgement of permanent inability. Judge competence from the actual reasoning across the record, not a requirement for full marks in a particular part. The calibration distinguishes a local arithmetic slip from an invalid method, and a valid alternative solution from an unsupported answer. Apply the assessment guide’s specified-method deductions where appropriate without automatically declaring the underlying capability absent. Every listed strand in an indicator must be represented across the evidence—for example, a dot product alone cannot establish O3b’s vector-product strand. The strands may be distributed across tasks; no student must reproduce every strand in every assessment.

Use the body of independent assessed work across the TMAs and examination. A sound earlier demonstration remains valid when a later paper does not sample the same detail. A blank examination part is absent evidence, not an automatic erasure of an earlier demonstration. A later response that explicitly contradicts a foundational idea triggers a review of that indicator. Resolve the contradiction from other current evidence where possible; require a fresh targeted demonstration only if it remains material. Record the particular conflict rather than imposing an unpublished discretionary hurdle.

Do not turn small examination outcome-mark totals into additional percentage pass thresholds: one or two marks are too coarse to support that decision. The proposed examination’s primary allocations remain O1 21, O2 25, O3 12, O4 12, O5 11 and O6 19 marks. Outcome judgements use the meaning of the work, while the component thresholds use its score.

Independent Python is essential. O6b needs learner-written, runnable and explained checks in each of the three existing TMA contexts: linear algebra, derivatives and quadrature. Each context needs a meaningful mathematical check and an account of limitations. It does not require flawless production software or every administrative mark. For each context, identify the learner-written implementation, the core method it actually performs, a successful reproducible run on the stated inputs, a meaningful mathematical check, and an explanation of the result and limits. An unresolved core error or a knowingly false interpretation prevents that context being demonstrated, even with a high coding-question score. A minor omission such as a version label instead receives its allocated deduction and proportionate clarification; it is not automatically a failure of independent programming. A declared limitation outside the intended input domain differs from silently using an invalid result. Preserve the existing allowed documentation and syntax-completion rules; generated solutions or answer-producing debugging cannot establish independence. The examination’s supplied-code interpretation corroborates explanation but cannot replace code production and execution.

Formative activities and iCMAs guide preparation, diagnosis and follow-up. Their completion is encouraged, but no additional graded hurdle or percentage minimum is introduced. Assisted practice and corrected copies with the solution already available do not close an independent-evidence gap by themselves. The normal completion route requires no extra viva, portfolio or fourth TMA.

The complete version 1.0 private marking guides for all three TMAs have now been read and the indicator homes checked against their criterion allocations. Nine constructed complete TMA scripts and six diagnostic response fragments were marked for calibration. The actual final-examination paper and guide do not yet exist: their criterion mapping and threshold-level responses remain a release gate. This local author review is not independent human moderation. Missing sampling is a design defect to repair before release, not a failure to attribute to a learner. The proposed indicators must be shared before the assessments to which they apply.

Reassessment is available to resolve non-completion, not solely to raise an already complete result. The student receives the criterion-level reason, a bounded preparation plan, the task’s scope and conditions, and the expected time before attempting it. Use a new equivalent problem, model or code task; changing only the numbers in a fully explained answer is insufficient. A correction to the original response remains useful learning but is not fresh independent evidence. State the follow-up’s success criteria before the attempt. A mathematical follow-up resolving an examination contradiction uses the examination reference/calculator conditions; a coding follow-up uses the existing independent TMA conditions. Other gaps use the conditions of their named assessment home. Do not choose easier conditions after seeing the response.

No mark cap applies. A full replacement assessment earns the mark its work warrants. Use the latest valid replacement score, even if lower; tell the student this before the attempt. Preserve all previous scores and attempts, label which score is current, and record completion after reassessment where applicable. Do not combine the best subparts from different papers or average old and replacement attempts. A focused outcome task cannot manufacture a new score for an entire TMA or examination.

There is no automatic permanent exclusion after a fixed number of attempts. After two unsuccessful reassessments of the same component or evidence requirement, following the initial attempt, pause further testing for a documented review of prerequisites, teaching, workload and access. Agree a revised preparation plan and readiness evidence before another attempt. Only valid unsuccessful reassessments count toward this trigger; approved deferrals and invalidated technical attempts do not. This is a support trigger; further attempts remain possible. Passing scores need not be repeated merely because another component is unfinished, although genuinely contradictory new evidence still requires review.

A numerical failure is addressed by the full replacement component because a narrow follow-up cannot establish an equivalent mark across that component’s scope. A narrow evidence gap is addressed narrowly because repeating already demonstrated work creates unnecessary workload. Reassessment preserves the original scope, marking standard and assistance conditions; accessible adjustments may change presentation or time without lowering the outcome.

When a replacement TMA is needed to improve the coursework mean, select among current TMA scores below 40. Normally choose the one with the most consequential evidence gap; where several are suitable, agree the choice with the student. Recompute after each replacement, stopping numerical reassessment when the mean reaches 40. For example, replacing 20 by 45 in scores 20, 45, 45 changes the mean from about 36.7 to 45. It does not require repeating the two satisfactory TMAs.

5. Fair decisions, circumstances and review

Use the marking rules in the examination specification: valid alternatives, method and interpretation credit, explicit rounding tolerances and proportionate follow-through. Assess mathematical meaning rather than handwriting style or unnecessary prose. Before finalising a result, check arithmetic, criterion totals, evidence locations and consistent handling of alternative solutions. Review borderline component scores and all “not demonstrated” decisions in a separate pass; do not round across a threshold or add discretionary “near-pass” marks.

The student may request an explanation or review of a mark, outcome judgement or assessment-circumstances decision. Provide the criterion, the relevant work and the reason; reconsider calculation errors, overlooked methods, inaccessible conditions or inconsistent treatment. Where another qualified reviewer is available, use them for a disputed judgement. Otherwise identify the review as reconsideration by the same tutor and offer a fresh equivalent task if the evidence remains genuinely ambiguous. An administrative appeal must not itself count as an unsuccessful reassessment. Preserve the original decision and record the reason for any change; clerical corrections remain possible after completion.

Illness, inaccessible materials, interruption, failed submission or unresolved assistance concerns require a documented decision before applying a score. Agree extensions or a deferred fresh assessment where warranted. No automatic late penalty, fabricated zero or deadline change is introduced by this policy. Actual submission arrangements and technical contacts must be issued before use. Retain the examination specification’s distinction between working time and packaging time, and its honest description of self-administered conditions.

Clarify the scope of assistance without assuming misconduct. Work influenced by answer-producing help cannot establish independent capability; unaffected evidence remains available. Resolve the affected assessment’s validity before selecting current marks. A missing or unsuccessful code part does not invalidate otherwise legitimate mathematics or turn the whole TMA into zero. Mark the supplied criteria and keep the independent-evidence requirement visible. If an entire attempt is invalidated after a documented review, its numeric score is excluded from the calculation with the reason preserved. Any separate misconduct sanction requires an approved process; this policy does not create an automatic punitive tariff.

Apply these rules prospectively to governed assessment attempts. Published question availability is not evidence that these grading rules were previously agreed. If any actual attempt predates adoption under different stated conditions, review those conditions explicitly rather than retrospectively imposing this policy. No such learner history has been assumed or changed here.

The standard route remains 300 hours and 30 internal credits. The final-assessment envelope retains the proposed specimen, review, format practice, final sitting and submission; it is not an 18-hour sitting. The separate 12 revision hours remain available for coherent retrieval and mixed practice. Outcome mapping is a tutor review of existing evidence, not a new compulsory student portfolio.

The six feedback hours are provisionally divided into 1.5 hours after each TMA and 1.5 after the final exam. Use them for reading feedback, corrections and an agreed next step. Do not silently treat all six as an unused resit reserve. Likewise, the six flexible exam-preparation hours already sit inside the 18-hour examination envelope. They may support a suitable pre-exam gap when an identified task replaces planned practice; they cannot be counted a second time after consumption.

Plan additional work explicitly. The following are starting estimates for one bounded reassessment cycle, including the student’s preparation and feedback. They are not guaranteed completion times or hard limits on access needs.

Before scheduling a cycle, identify genuinely unused suitable time in the existing revision, feedback or flexible preparation allocation. State the original activity being replaced, the time transferred and the remaining balance. Preserve the essential preparation and feedback for other assessments. If no suitable balance remains, record the cycle as additional time: a completed baseline plus one full exam reassessment is approximately 308 hours; a full TMA replacement plus that exam cycle is approximately 317 hours. Credits remain 30. Do not claim reassessment fits into 300 hours by deleting taught content, rushing feedback or reusing already spent hours.

Three absent coding contexts are three evidence gaps, not a single brief clarification. At the indicative two hours per focused coding cycle, they could require six additional hours if no suitable allocation remains; extensive missing preparation needs a fuller plan. A short follow-up is appropriate only for a bounded gap. Several weak strands may need more practice or a fuller replacement task; estimate that work explicitly and agree a revised schedule. No automatic calendar shift or new study-speed requirement is introduced. The student’s recorded attempts and preparation time inform subsequent estimates; tutor marking time is separate from the student-hour budget.

These are synthetic policy tests, not learner results. Numerical examples alone are not marked response calibration; section 9 records the separate criterion-level response work. The earlier 39.99 example was only a numerical robustness test, not a score justified by the current whole-mark examination blueprint. Use the actual marking resolution of each assessment: TMA 01 permits half-marks only in identified allocations; TMA 02 permits partial half-marks; TMA 03 has explicit fractional allocations and proportionate table credit. Do not invent further fractional marks to create a near-pass. “Complete” in an example always assumes all four valid reviewed attempts, every indicator demonstrated, independent Python in all three contexts, and resolved moderation/circumstances. A score never automatically supplies those judgements.

An invalidated exam followed by a valid deferred sitting uses the latter as the first valid score, with the original preserved as excluded. A well-supported alternative method receives its earned marks; a successful marking appeal corrects the original score rather than creating a new attempt. A successful targeted follow-up changes an evidence status but leaves an otherwise valid numerical mark intact.

8. Adoption and assessment operation

This policy v1.0 is adopted prospectively on 29 October 2026. It retains the calibrated v0.3 thresholds, exact weights, outcome requirements and reassessment rules. Existing attempts, if any, remain governed by their originally stated conditions until an explicit review; no learner history is assumed.

The teaching, revision, record and public specimen are published. The actual examination and replacement examination, with their marking guides, remain private. A sitting requires a dated candidate package and confirmed delivery instructions. The examiner records adjustments, start and finish, submission receipt and any interruption. No automatic mark or completion follows from opening material.

The user explicitly accepted documented timing and accessibility checks instead of an unseen learner trial on 29 October 2026. Timing estimates are not measured learner completion times. Reassessment preparation and feedback retain the additional-time rules in section 6.

Use the private local assessment workflow to issue the permitted paper and reference, retain the original submitted bytes, verify the receipt and recover from a separate backup. The learner receives the applicable policy and the submission route before starting. Marking and whole-record outcome review are explicit examiner actions. Current versus superseded decisions and corrections remain in an append-only history.

9. Calibration evidence and limits

The three complete TMA guides and public specimen were calibrated against constructed borderline responses. The actual Form A received independent AI sub-agent moderation. The replacement Form B received separate author calculations, criterion and equivalence checks. No human external moderation or learner trial is claimed.

These checks support internal consistency, not a claim of empirically identical difficulty. Boundary scripts at 38–42, outcome-blocking judgements and disputes require a fresh marking pass. Preserve the original criteria and evidence when correcting a result.
