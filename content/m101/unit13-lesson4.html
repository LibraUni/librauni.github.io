13.13 Transfer to a supplied transport model

Two vessels, A and B, contain water. A pipe transfers water from A to B at a supplied rate q(t)=2−t/2 L/min; B loses water to an external drain at rate r(t)=1/2 L/min. Time is in minutes and the model applies for 0≤t≤4. Initially A contains 12 L and B contains 3 L. Each vessel has capacity 20 L. Assume no other flows and that each stated rate is maintained throughout this interval.

Let VA and VB be stored volumes. Each rate of change is inflow minus outflow. A loses the transfer flow; B receives that flow and loses the drain flow. Therefore

(VA′VB′)=(−qq−r)=C(qr),C=(−101−1).

The matrix now encodes a flow network. Its first column says that one litre transferred removes one litre from A and adds one to B. Its second says that one litre drained affects only B. The components share units but are amounts in different vessels, rather than perpendicular spatial coordinates. Taking their Euclidean magnitude would not calculate the total stored water. The relevant total is their sum.

Example 13.9 · Accumulate and check conservation

Find both stored volumes after four minutes and check the total change independently.

The transfer amount is ∫04(2−t/2)dt=4 L. The drain removes ∫04(1/2)dt=2 L. Applying the flow matrix to these amounts gives changes (−4,2) L. Add the initial volumes to obtain (8,5) L.

For an independent total check, add the two rate equations. The internal transfer cancels: VA′+VB′=−r. Initial total 15 L minus drained amount 2 L gives 13 L, agreeing with 8+5. This cancellation follows from the network, so it holds for any admissible transfer history with the same drain history.

To inspect conditions throughout the interval, retain the variable upper limit:

VA(t)=12−2t+t24,VB(t)=3+32t−t24.

A decreases from 12 L to 8 L. B increases until its derivative 3/2−t/2 vanishes at 3 min, when it holds 5.25 L, and then decreases to 5 L. Neither vessel empties or exceeds capacity. Checking endpoints alone would have missed B's interior maximum, even though in this case it remains comfortably within capacity.

Activity 13.13 · A changed flow, an unchanged total

Increase the transfer rate by 0.1 L/min at every time while keeping the drain fixed. Predict changes in the final volumes and their sum. Is this a model-input change or a numerical-resolution change? Give an independent check.

Discussion of Activity 13.13

An extra 0.4 L moves from A to B: final volumes become 7.6 L and 5.4 L. Their sum remains 13 L because the drain has not changed. The model input changed; refining a numerical integration grid cannot produce this physical redistribution by itself. The summed rate equation provides an independent check. Volumes remain positive and below capacity; B's revised maximum is 5.56 L at 3.2 min.

The transfer lesson is about meaning. We used the same matrix and integration operations as for the journey, but selected a different operation on the resulting components because a different physical quantity was requested. The interpretation must travel with the algebra.

13.14 Formative synthesis checkpoint

Work without the discussions for about 50 minutes, then use 25 minutes to compare and correct. Supply reasoning, sketches where useful and units. You may consult the reference sheet after first choosing a method. This checkpoint samples all six M101 outcomes through a supplied model; it creates no grade or completion record.

A second tracked object has x(t)=t+t2 and y(t)=3t, with numerical time in seconds, coordinates in metres and 0≤t≤2. A fixed recorder gives p=2x+y, q=y. The starting position is the origin.

Activity 13.14 · Connect the whole argument

  1. Write the recorder matrix and recover (x,y) from (p,q). Decode the reading (7,3) m and test whether it is consistent with the journey.
  2. Find velocity and speed at 1 s. Give a geometric interpretation of the velocity direction.
  3. Predict position at 1.1 s using the tangent at 1 s, calculate the exact model position and explain the signed error.
  4. Find the displacement on 0–2 s by integration and by endpoint subtraction. Find endpoint separation.
  5. Write a speed integral for distance. Use two equal trapezoidal panels to estimate it. Give one exact benchmark obtainable by changing the model.
  6. A program computes hypot(trapezoid(vx,0,2,n), trapezoid(vy,0,2,n)). Explain the quantity it returns and whether increasing n makes it a distance calculation. Write a brief, justified conclusion about the two-panel distance estimate.
Separate hint for the synthesis checkpoint

Recover the second coordinate first. Keep physical coordinate rates separate from channel rates. For the tangent error, expand (1+h)2. For distance, put the magnitude inside the integral. A linear position model gives a constant-velocity benchmark.

Worked discussion and feedback criteria

1. The matrix is (2101). From q=y, obtain y=q and x=(p−q)/2. The reading gives (2,3) m. Its north coordinate gives t=1 s, and the east function also gives 2 m: it is consistent.

2. Velocity is (1+2t,3) m/s. At 1 s it is (3,3) m/s, pointing northeast with speed 32 m/s. The equal components give a path tangent of slope 1.

3. Position at 1 s is (2,3) m. Adding 0.1(3,3) gives (2.3,3.3) m. Exact position is (2.31,3.3) m. Exact minus predicted is (0.01,0) m. The east quadratic contributes h2; the tangent underestimates the increasing east coordinate.

4. Component integrals are [t+t2]02=6 m and [3t]02=6 m. Endpoint subtraction agrees, giving (6,6) m and separation 62 m.

5. The distance integral is ∫02(1+2t)2+9dt. With one-second panels, its trapezoidal estimate is 1210+18+1234≈8.73926 m. Removing the quadratic term from x gives constant velocity (1,3) m/s and exact distance 210 m for every panel count.

6. The program computes the magnitude of integrated velocity, so it gives endpoint separation, 62≈8.48528 m. Component integrands are linear and already integrate exactly; increasing resolution cannot change what quantity is calculated. The two-panel speed estimate is plausible and exceeds the endpoint separation. Refinement and independent checks are needed to justify more precise distance reporting.

Use the evidence. Items 1–2 check matrix and vector interpretation (O4/O3); items 2–3 check local rates and approximation (O1/O5); item 4 checks accumulation (O2); items 5–6 check method choice, code interpretation and coherent reporting (O5/O6). Success here is formative evidence for your next study decision, not a substitute for assessment.

13.15 Unit exercises

These exercises cover the full unit. Attempt all 14 over the reserved 155 minutes, then spend 45 minutes checking, diagnosing and correcting. Questions 1–5 retrieve individual connections; 6–10 require combinations and explanation; 11–14 test transfer and judgement. Hints and full solutions are separate. Unless another model is stated, use the baseline journey r(t)=(2t,4t−t2) m on 0–4 s and the fixed recorder (p,q)=(x+y,x−y).

Exercise 13.1 · Positions and changes

Find the displacement from 0.5 s to 2.5 s, its magnitude and the mean velocity over that interval. Explain why the mean speed cannot be obtained from these numbers alone.

Hint for Exercise 13.1

Evaluate both positions before subtracting. Mean velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.1

The positions are (1,1.75) m and (5,3.75) m. Their difference is (4,2) m, with magnitude 25 m. Dividing by 2 s gives mean velocity (2,1) m/s. Mean speed requires the actual distance along the curved route divided by 2 s; endpoint separation gives only a lower bound on that distance.

Return to Exercise 13.1

Exercise 13.2 · Decode a reading

The recorder gives (p,q)=(8,4) m. Recover the coordinates and decide whether the point is consistent with the baseline journey. Show a forward-calibration check.

Hint for Exercise 13.2

Use x=(p+q)/2, then use the east coordinate to determine the only possible model time.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.2

The decoded point is (6,2) m. Forward substitution gives 6+2=8 and 6−2=4. However, x=6 m requires t=3 s, when the model has y=3 m. Decoding is correct, but the reading and model disagree. A useful investigation would examine calibration, reading errors and model fit, rather than alter the inverse merely to force agreement.

Return to Exercise 13.2

Exercise 13.3 · Information in a path

A second schedule follows the same route in eight seconds: X(τ)=x(τ/2), Y(τ)=y(τ/2), with 0≤τ≤8. Write the functions, find the position and velocity at τ=2 s, and compare them with the original journey at 1 s.

Hint for Exercise 13.3

The substitution halves the rate at which the original time argument advances.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.3

The numerical coordinate functions are X=τ, Y=2τ−τ2/4. At 2 s the position is (2,3) m, the same point reached originally at 1 s. Differentiating gives velocity (1,2−τ/2) m/s, or (1,1) m/s at 2 s: half the original velocity. The path is unchanged, while the timing and speeds change. Total route distance remains the same.

Return to Exercise 13.3

Exercise 13.4 · Local motion

Find velocity and speed at 0.5 s. Predict position at 0.6 s using that velocity and calculate the exact signed position error.

Hint for Exercise 13.4

Differentiate first, then add hv to position with h=0.1 s.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.4

At 0.5 s, position is (1,1.75) m and velocity (2,3) m/s, with speed 13 m/s. The tangent predicts (1.2,2.05) m. Exact position is (1.2,2.04) m, so exact minus predicted is (0,−0.01) m. The magnitude is 0.01 m and agrees with kh2.

Return to Exercise 13.4

Exercise 13.5 · Choose an admissible step

At 3.9 s a tangent prediction is required with position error at most 0.04 m. Find the largest forward step permitted by both the tolerance and the supplied model domain.

Hint for Exercise 13.5

The error condition and the finishing-time condition must both hold.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.5

The tolerance allows h2≤0.04, so h≤0.2 s for forward time. The domain requires 3.9+h≤4, giving h≤0.1 s. The largest admissible step is therefore 0.1 s, with model error 0.01 m. Choosing 0.2 s would satisfy the algebraic error condition but leave the supplied domain.

Return to Exercise 13.5

Exercise 13.6 · Signed and unsigned northward travel

Compute ∫04vy(t)dt and ∫04|vy(t)|dt. Interpret them and explain why the second is still not the full planar distance.

Hint for Exercise 13.6

Split the absolute-value integral where vy changes sign.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.6

The signed integral is zero. On [0,2], vy=4−2t≥0, giving 4 m; on [2,4] its absolute value is 2t−4, also giving 4 m. Total north-coordinate travel is 8 m. Full planar distance integrates vx2+vy2, including simultaneous east motion. Its value is about 11.832 m, rather than 8 m.

Return to Exercise 13.6

Exercise 13.7 · Channel rates and geometry

At 3 s, calculate the channel-rate vector. Recover physical velocity, compare both magnitudes and explain the factor between them.

Hint for Exercise 13.7

The matrix acts on velocity because it is fixed in time. Expand the sum of squared channel rates.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.7

Physical velocity is (2,−2) m/s. Channel rates are (0,4) m/s; decoding gives (2,−2) m/s again. Channel magnitude is 4 m/s and physical speed is 22 m/s. Their ratio is 2. This comes from the calibration, since

(vx+vy)2+(vx−vy)2=2(vx2+vy2).

Return to Exercise 13.7

Exercise 13.8 · Reconstruct a quadrature result

Calculate the two-panel trapezoidal speed integral on 0–4 s. Compare it with the four-panel value and with endpoint separation. State what the comparison establishes.

Hint for Exercise 13.8

The two-panel width is 2 s, and the middle sample is at 2 s.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.8

The sampled speeds are 20,2,20 m/s. Thus D2=2(20+2), or 4+220≈12.94427 m. The four-panel value is about 12.12899 m and endpoint separation is 8 m. Refinement lowers the estimate, consistent with convexity; both exceed the lower geometric bound. Two resolutions alone do not establish a requested numerical accuracy.

Return to Exercise 13.8

Exercise 13.9 · Sensitivity without rerunning everything

Keep u=2 and w=4, but change k from 1 to 1.05 m/s². Predict the change in final displacement exactly and the tangent-error magnitude for a 0.2 s step. Identify a separate calculation needed to compare total distances.

Hint for Exercise 13.9

Use y(4)=4w−16k and the exact tangent remainder.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.9

The final north displacement changes by −16(0.05)=−0.8 m; east displacement remains 8 m. Tangent-error magnitude is 1.05(0.2)2=0.042 m. Comparing total distances requires integrating the changed speed 4+(4−2.1t)2 over the same interval. Endpoint displacement alone does not determine that change.

Return to Exercise 13.9

Exercise 13.10 · A misleading check

A program checks its computed distance with distance >= 0 and by rerunning the same function. Explain two defects those checks could miss, and propose one independent test for each.

Hint for Exercise 13.10

Consider a missing integration width and integration of channel-rate magnitude.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.10

Both checks can pass if the panel width is omitted: the sum is positive and repeatable. Integrating a constant, such as 3 over [1,4], with several panel counts must give 9 and exposes the width error. Both checks can also pass when channel-rate magnitude replaces physical speed. A hand-computed constant physical velocity, decoded before taking its magnitude, reveals the extra 2 factor. The tests have different purposes; repeatability alone does not establish meaning or scaling.

Return to Exercise 13.10

Exercise 13.11 · Conservation in the transport model

For Section 13.13, calculate the volumes at 2 min and their instantaneous rates of change. Check both total volume and total rate independently.

Hint for Exercise 13.11

Use the accumulated-volume functions and add the rate equations.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.11

At 2 min, VA=12−4+1=9 L and VB=3+3−1=5 L. The transfer is 1 L/min, so rates are (−1,0.5) L/min. The total volume 14 L equals initial 15 L minus 1 L drained. The summed rate is −0.5 L/min, equal to the negative drain rate. Internal transfer cancels in each check.

Return to Exercise 13.11

Exercise 13.12 · Recover flows from rates

In the same network, observed instantaneous storage rates are VA′=−1.2 L/min and VB′=0.7 L/min. Recover transfer and drain rates by solving the two equations. Explain what a negative recovered transfer would mean under the stated direction convention.

Hint for Exercise 13.12

The first equation determines transfer; use the second for the drain.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.12

From −q=−1.2, transfer is 1.2 L/min. From q−r=0.7, drain is 0.5 L/min. Substitution verifies both rates. A negative q would mean transfer from B to A under the chosen sign convention. If the apparatus permits flow only from A to B, such a result would instead signal that the assumed network or observations need examination.

Return to Exercise 13.12

Exercise 13.13 · A different calibration

A new recorder reports p=3x+y, q=x+y. Derive its inverse relation. Decode (10,6) m, and find coordinate errors caused by δp=0.3 m, δq=0.1 m. Check your result forward.

Hint for Exercise 13.13

Subtract the equations to eliminate y.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.13

Subtraction gives x=(p−q)/2, then y=(3q−p)/2. The point is (2,4) m. Error propagation is linear: δx=(0.3−0.1)/2=0.1 m and δy=(3(0.1)−0.3)/2=0. Forward errors are 3(0.1)+0=0.3 m and 0.1+0=0.1 m. This inverse is different from B; the same symbol “channel” does not imply the same calibration.

Return to Exercise 13.13

Exercise 13.14 · Choose and defend a route

A supplied model has x(t)=3t, y(t)=2t−t2 in metres for numerical seconds on [0,2]. Produce a short solution finding final displacement, velocity at 1 s, a distance integral and a two-panel trapezoidal estimate. Give one exact benchmark, one domain check and a limitation of the distance claim.

Hint for Exercise 13.14

Organise the answer by requested quantities, then choose checks with different vulnerabilities.

Worked solution to Exercise 13.14

Endpoint displacement is (6,0) m. Velocity is (3,2−2t) m/s, giving (3,0) m/s at 1 s. Distance is ∫029+(2−2t)2dt. With width 1 s and speeds 13,3,13, the estimate is 3+13≈6.60555 m. Component integration independently gives (6,0) m; replacing y by 2t gives a constant-speed benchmark 213 m. All samples are within [0,2]. The coarse distance is plausible because it exceeds endpoint separation 6 m, but further refinement or an error bound is needed for a precise accuracy claim. A good response connects each check to what it can reveal, rather than merely listing tests.

Return to Exercise 13.14

13.16 Conclusion and reference

The unit began with several records of one journey. Connecting them required more than translating symbols: we had to preserve the meaning of positions, components, rates and totals. A matrix decoded the recorder, differentiation identified local motion, and integration accumulated either directed change or travelled distance according to the integrand chosen.

Those connections also created checks. Endpoint differences tested accumulated velocity; a constant-velocity case tested distance; a tangent remainder explained a step-size error; and symmetry tested sensitivity calculations. The transport model then showed why mathematical operations must be interpreted afresh. Adding stored volumes gives a useful total, whereas their Euclidean magnitude does not have the same role as the length of a displacement vector.

This closes M101's taught sequence. Its progression has been from representing quantities, through describing change and accumulation, to explaining why a chosen calculation answers a particular question. The separately allocated revision period is the place to revisit weak connections across the module and prepare for the written examination. Use your checkpoint and exercise corrections to choose that work rather than simply rereading everything.

M102 and A101 will build on the ability to select methods, state assumptions and explain a short calculation with checks. Keep one complete investigation and one corrected misconception as evidence of what you can now explain. If a prerequisite still feels uncertain, name the specific operation or interpretation and return to its teaching before relying on it in a later model.

Use the final 40 minutes for active reference work: reconstruct the Recall relationships, explain one Understand item from a diagram or derivation, and locate the Consult information needed to reproduce a check. The printable reference PDF uses the same compact entries.
