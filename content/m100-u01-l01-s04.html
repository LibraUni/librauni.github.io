SECTION 4 · AVAILABLE · ABOUT 45 MINUTES

Which operation happens first?

An expression is an instruction with a structure. Changing that structure can change the answer even when every number stays the same.

You can now calculate with signed numbers. This section combines operations: allow 20 minutes for explanations and worked examples, then 25 minutes for practice and feedback. Work by hand before using Python in Section 5.

Grouping gives the calculation its meaning

Compare 2 + 3 × 4 with (2 + 3) × 4. In the first expression, multiplication has priority: three groups of four, then add two, giving 14. In the second, the parentheses group the sum: five groups of four, giving 20. Parentheses—also called round brackets—are instructions, not decoration.

For the operations in this lesson:

  1. Evaluate grouped expressions first, starting with the innermost parentheses. Apply the same rules inside each group.
  2. Multiply and divide at equal priority, working from left to right when they occur in the same ungrouped chain.
  3. Add and subtract at equal priority, again working from left to right.

Keep a unary sign attached to its number: in 5 × (−2), −2 is the negative factor. We are not introducing powers here; they require an additional rule in a later unit. The symbol × always means multiplication in this lesson. Later you may see a centred dot or multiplication implied by adjacent symbols; for now we write the operation explicitly.

A mnemonic is only a reminder

You may remember BODMAS or BIDMAS. Neither means “division always before multiplication”, nor “addition always before subtraction”. Each pair shares a priority. Left-to-right order within a pair matters: subtraction and division cannot generally be rearranged.

Worked example 1 · Follow a grouped calculation

Evaluate 3 × (2 + 4) − 5.

  1. Group first: 2 + 4 = 6. The expression becomes 3 × 6 − 5.
  2. Multiplication before subtraction: 3 × 6 = 18. It becomes 18 − 5.
  3. Subtract: the result is 13.

Each replacement preserves the value of the whole expression. Write the chain as 3 × (2 + 4) − 5 = 3 × 6 − 5 = 18 − 5 = 13. Do not write “2 + 4 = 6 × 3”: that equality would claim 6 equals 18.

The diagram below is a small expression tree. Each operation joins the two things it acts on. Read from the bottom upwards: first add 2 and 4, then multiply the result by 3, then subtract 5. The top operation happens last because it needs the results beneath it.

Worked example 2 · Multiplication does not jump the queue

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8. Division and multiplication share priority, so the leftmost operation comes first. Multiplying 6 × 2 first would instead calculate 24 ÷ (6 × 2) = 2, a different expression with an extra grouping.

A check in words: divide 24 into six equal shares, giving four in each share, then double four. That gives eight.

Worked example 3 · Preserve negative numbers

Evaluate 8 − (−2) × 3 + 1.

Multiplication first: (−2) × 3 = −6. Then work left to right through subtraction and addition:

8 − (−6) + 1 = 14 + 1 = 15.

Do not subtract −2 from 8 before multiplying: the product is the quantity being subtracted. The parentheses around −2 identify a negative factor; there is no binary calculation inside them to do first.

Your turn: retain the whole expression

Use about 18 minutes for these five calculations and feedback. Show intermediate steps and name the rule you used. In the last item, compare the grouped and ungrouped expressions.

Five calculation tasks

  1. 7 + 2 × 5
  2. (7 + 2) × 5
  3. 18 ÷ 3 × 2
  4. 10 − 4 + 2
  5. Compare 6 − (2 + 1) with 6 − 2 + 1.
Check the structure as well as the answers
  1. 7 + 10 = 17. Multiply first.
  2. 9 × 5 = 45. The grouped addition now comes first.
  3. 6 × 2 = 12. Divide then multiply, left to right.
  4. 6 + 2 = 8. Subtract then add, left to right. Adding 4 + 2 first would introduce a grouping that is not there.
  5. 6 − 3 = 3 for the grouped expression; 4 + 1 = 5 for the ungrouped one. Subtracting a sum differs from subtracting one number and then adding the other.

Find the first invalid step

Use the remaining seven minutes to review this solution: 20 ÷ 5 × 2 = 20 ÷ 10 = 2. Its author says “multiplication comes first”. Explain the error and write a valid chain. Then state which expression their working would correctly evaluate.

Compare your correction

The first equality changes the structure by combining 5 and 2 before the division. Multiplication has no priority over division. Correctly, 20 ÷ 5 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8. Their chain would evaluate 20 ÷ (5 × 2). The arithmetic within their chosen steps is correct; the choice of steps is not.

Optional replacement practice · if equal priority is unclear

Evaluate 12 ÷ 3 × 2 and 9 − 3 + 1. Then insert parentheses to express what would happen if you did the rightmost operation first.

Read the comparison

Left to right gives 8 and 7. The altered groupings are 12 ÷ (3 × 2) = 2 and 9 − (3 + 1) = 5. These are different instructions, so different results are expected.

You are ready to translate expressions when you can explain which operation acts on which values. Section 5 uses exactly these operations in Python; the computer will follow the expression you type, even if it is not the one you intended.
