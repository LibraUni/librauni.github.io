01 / THE ARCHITECTURE

A shared foundation. A considered choice of direction.

The OU R51 Physics degree informs our substantial-module architecture. LibraUni uses original modules and a fixed Stage 1 of two balanced semesters, followed by core physics and later specialisation. No OU equivalence is claimed.

STAGE 1 · 120FoundationsLearn the language and build physical intuition.STAGE 2 · 120Core physicsConnect theory, calculation and evidence.STAGE 3 · 120Depth & independenceSpecialise and complete a substantial investigation.

360 internal credits in total. Credits organise workload and completion within LibraUni; they are not transferable university credits. Stage describes progression, not a calendar year. Each substantial module will contain blocks, units and lessons: Degree → Stage → Module → Block → Unit → Lesson.

By completion, the learner should be able to construct and test physical models, solve unfamiliar quantitative problems, analyse measurements with uncertainty, write reproducible Python investigations, explain the assumptions and limits of a result, and complete a defensible independent project.

Semester timing: two sequential semesters, with two concurrent 30-credit modules in each. Each semester therefore contains 600 notional hours. Calendar weeks, holidays, start dates and assessment deadlines are not yet set; both modules in a pair will use a coordinated semester timetable. The superseded 30/31-week M100–A101 pairing is withdrawn. “Semester” does not silently reduce the required hours.

Optional preparation · outside the degree

LU-M100 · Mathematics & Python bridge is available only if preparation is needed. Its 30 internal credits represent 300 hours and do not count towards the 120-credit stage or 360-credit degree. It combines mathematics with relevant physics applications and substantial Python work, progressing towards controlled AI/agent assistance. Abstract exercises remain where they build fluency.

Readiness evidence will determine whether the full bridge or selected refreshers are appropriate. Selected refreshers do not automatically earn the full module credit. Completing the full bridge plus Stage 1 would mean 1,500 hours in total; the degree itself remains 360 credits.

PROGRESSION AUDIT / 26 September 2026

Protect the core on every route.

Provisional progression and capacity proposal — awaiting review · version 0.1. This tests the existing 360-credit architecture; it does not approve new blocks, units or timetables. Each estimate includes examples, practice and Python within study time. Module assessment envelopes are original hypotheses, not verified OU presentation patterns.

Finding: the architecture has plausible subject homes, but P201 and especially P302 are crowded. We propose compulsory analytical mechanics in P201, statistical foundations in P201, solid-state and nuclear/particle foundations in P302, and inference in X201. Choosing astronomy must not remove these outcomes.

Sequence and intentional revisits

M201 methods must lead their P201 applications. X201 can begin with measurement/data work, but a theory-dependent investigation waits for P201. M102 probability supports P201 statistics and X201 inference; X201 is not a prerequisite for the first thermal block. Stage 3 follows the relevant Stage 2 outcomes; an option or project requiring P302 material must wait for that material. Module enrolment alone never demonstrates readiness. No dates are assigned here.

P101 introduces physical models; P201 derives and extends them; P301/P302 build advanced treatments. A101 introduces authentic astronomical analysis; X201 generalises inference and experimental design; R300 demands independent choices. These revisits require harder outcomes and different evidence, not duplicated introductory lessons.

External checks and limits

The MIT focused degree explicitly separates quantum, statistical physics, mechanics, laboratory work and thesis requirements. Its three-course quantum sequence highlights the depth pressure on our smaller combined module. MIT units are not UK-style credits. The MIT subject descriptions also identify prerequisites between waves, quantum, statistics and solid-state work. Both were read on 26 September 2026.

Imperial’s Physics BSc page remains accessible only through indexed official content (rechecked 26 September; direct retrieval returned 403). Its solid-state listing supports retaining that subject, but this is not a complete second-university assessment audit. Earlier dated programme evidence and its limitations remain in the coverage register.

What this audit does not close

P201’s 108-hour mechanics envelope and P302’s 228 subject-study hours are capacity hypotheses. Detailed worked problems, practice and assessment mapping must test them. If depth does not fit, propose a visible credit/core-option reallocation for review; do not shorten essential explanations or add hidden hours. Advanced scattering, many-body theory and extensive general relativity are not promised as compulsory depth. Apparatus and collaboration remain unfulfilled practical outcomes.

Policy 1.0 disposition: module-level exploratory audit complete; allocations proposed for review. Parent: programme stages; peers: all modules and six alternative options; receiving foundation: approved Stage 1 units. Relevant missing subject homes now have proposed owners, but coverage gaps G01–G04 remain open until outcomes, units, evidence and feasibility are checked. No later-stage teaching release. Next: review this map, then blueprint all M100 B01 lessons; bridge construction does not require distant units to be mass-authored. OU calendars and detailed source/assessment checks remain release work for the affected modules.

05 / POSSIBLE PATHS

Choose an emphasis, keep the physics.

Paths are coordinated choices of investigations, specialist option and final project. They are not separate degrees or extra credits hidden within a module.

  • Astrophysics & cosmology: A101 in Stage 1, further astronomical investigations in Stage 2, A301 or A302 in Stage 3, and a related project. The OU distinguishes stellar/exoplanetary study from cosmology; keeping both as separate options preserves their depth.
  • Computational physics: emphasis on numerical investigation, C301, and a project whose central contribution is a validated physical model or analysis.
  • Mathematical & theoretical physics: greater emphasis on derivations and modelling, T301 or T302, and an analytical or computational project. This does not promise comprehensive coverage of all theoretical physics.
  • Matter & emergence: thermal and quantum investigations, the proposed P303 option, and a project connecting microscopic and macroscopic behaviour.
  • Broad physics: choose any eligible option and use the project to connect subjects across the programme.

Room to continue: completing both astrophysics options, or another additional option, would add 30 credits beyond the core programme, producing 390 in the extended record. It would earn a separate specialist certificate rather than displace electromagnetism, quantum physics or the project. Every further option adds its own workload and evidence.

07 / KEEPING THE CONTEXT

A living map, not a moving target.

Every module will state what it contributes to the programme, what it builds on and what it enables next. Every unit and lesson will locate itself within that module, connect back to earlier ideas and show the next destination. Periodic reviews will revisit the whole map and the evidence of progress.

The programme will carry a versioned decision record. Later changes must identify what changed, why, and whether prerequisites, workload or awards are affected. Completed work will retain its recorded outcomes rather than being silently reclassified.

Workload convention: use 10 notional study hours per internal credit as a planning convention: 300 hours per 30-credit module and 3,600 across the programme. Actual time can vary with prior knowledge and needed practice.

Our adaptations: original, self-contained teaching; Python throughout; free required resources; guided tutoring; flexible pacing; and carefully identified practical limitations. We borrow the OU’s organising logic, not its paid teaching materials. Improvements will be judged by clarity, coherence, evidence and fit—not presumed superiority.

From blueprint to a module you can preview

Before designing units, review reference teaching materials for their organisation and activity structure. Then cross-check the programme against two research-university undergraduate physics curricula, mapping traditional subjects and learning outcomes to modules and units. Record gaps, prerequisites and intentional revisits, distinguishing common-core coverage from specialist choices.

Develop the complete outline through Unit level, stage by stage and one module at a time. Each module preview will include its purpose, outcomes, prerequisites, every unit and its scope, expected hours and assessment workload. Review each module before proceeding to the next.

Complete the design and readiness review before teaching-material production. Then, for a module selected for study, develop one lesson or a smaller portion at a time: careful explanations, graduated examples and exercises, verified graphics and captions, curated free references, and relevant history and scientific anecdotes. Outlines and available lessons will be clearly distinguished.
