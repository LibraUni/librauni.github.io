01 / THE ARCHITECTURE

A shared foundation. A considered choice of direction.

The reference is the Open University’s R51 Physics degree. Its structure comprises three 120-credit stages, alternative starts, a common second stage, and a final stage combining electromagnetism, quantum physics, one option and a project. We retain that organising logic. LibraUni’s modules below are original proposals, not OU modules or claims of equivalence.

STAGE 1 · 120FoundationsLearn the language and build physical intuition.STAGE 2 · 120Core physicsConnect theory, calculation and evidence.STAGE 3 · 120Depth & independenceSpecialise and complete a substantial investigation.

360 internal credits in total. Credits organise workload and completion within LibraUni; they are not transferable university credits. Stage describes progression, not a calendar year. Each substantial module will contain units and lessons: Degree → Stage → Module → Unit → Lesson.

By completion, the learner should be able to construct and test physical models, solve unfamiliar quantitative problems, analyse measurements with uncertainty, write reproducible Python investigations, explain the assumptions and limits of a result, and complete a defensible independent project.

02 / STAGE 1

Foundations · 120 credits

Our starting proposal follows the shape of the OU basic-maths route: four substantial 30-credit modules. This provides space to rebuild fluency without assuming that a previous degree establishes current mathematical readiness.

Proposed Stage 1 modules
ModuleCreditsPurpose and scope
LU-M101 · Mathematical foundations30Rebuild number sense, algebra, functions, graphs, geometry and trigonometry through scientific contexts. Introduce Python as a tool for checking and exploring ideas.
LU-M102 · Mathematics for physics I30Develop calculus, vectors and introductory matrix methods, connecting symbolic reasoning with motion, rates of change and accumulated quantities.
LU-M103 · Mathematics for physics II30Extend calculus and linear algebra; introduce complex numbers, differential equations and mathematical reasoning needed for later physical models.
LU-P101 · Physics, space & scientific thinking30Build a quantitative introduction to motion, energy, matter, waves and the cosmos, with units, estimation, measurement and small computational investigations throughout.

Sequence: M101 prepares for M102; M102 prepares for M103. Introductory physics can accompany mathematical study, with its units sequenced to use tools already learned. These are proposed LibraUni prerequisites, to be checked when module outcomes are designed.

A flexible entry, without a missing foundation: if the later diagnostic and a fuller mastery check show that M101 is unnecessary, an alternative 30-credit module, LU-A101 · Exploring astronomy through computation, could replace it. Targeted refreshers would remain available. This is a proposed alternative, not an exemption already earned. Stage 1 would still total 120 credits.

The three mathematics modules make this a deliberately maths-rich start. Physics examples and the parallel physics module keep the purpose visible. We can revise that balance before approval; rusty skills alone do not establish the need for a full foundations module.

03 / STAGE 2

Core physics · 120 credits

All paths share these three modules. Different interests shape investigations from this stage onward, while the common physics foundation stays intact.

Proposed Stage 2 modules
ModuleCreditsPurpose and scope
LU-P201 · Core physics: motion, matter & fields60A connected account of mechanics, special relativity, thermal and statistical physics, fields, waves, optics and introductory quantum physics. Microscopic models connect to bulk matter and radiation.
LU-M201 · Mathematical methods for physics30Differential equations, multivariable and vector calculus, linear algebra and introductory boundary-value methods. Analytical work is paired with numerical checks in Python.
LU-X201 · Experimental & computational investigations30Experimental design, calibration, uncertainty, model testing, reproducible analysis and scientific reporting using simulations and freely accessible measured datasets.

The reference modules are S227, MST224 and SXPS288. One substantive adaptation is practical access: the OU uses real remotely controlled equipment and group work. Our initial plan provides simulations and analysis of measured data; those do not establish hands-on apparatus or teamwork competence. Any free remote-equipment opportunities would be verified before inclusion.

Sequence: Stage 1 outcomes prepare for this stage. M201 should precede or accompany the parts of P201 that require its tools; X201 investigations follow the relevant physical theory. We will design the unit-level prerequisite map before scheduling these modules.

Proposed Stage 3 structure
ModuleCreditsPurpose and scope

LU-P302 · Quantum physics & matter30Develop quantum states, operators, dynamics and measurement, with applications to atoms, matter and interactions with radiation.

Sequence: P201 and M201 prepare for the advanced physics modules. The project additionally requires the investigative skills of X201 and the relevant advanced subject knowledge. Project work follows or accompanies the chosen option only after its needed foundations have been covered.

The specialist menu

Each option is a proposed 30-credit module. The first five directions follow the option families in R51; the last is an additional LibraUni candidate. These are options to develop, not six completed courses.

Candidate specialist options — choose one within the 360-credit programme
OptionCentral questions
LU-A301 · Stars & exoplanetsHow do stars work and evolve? What can light reveal about planets around other stars?
LU-A302 · Cosmology & the evolving universeHow do expansion, gravity and observations constrain the history and contents of the universe? Includes the necessary introduction to general relativity, not a full relativity specialisation.
LU-C301 · Computational physicsHow can numerical models solve physical problems, and how do we establish that their results are reliable?

LU-P303 · Statistical physics & condensed matterHow do microscopic interactions produce phases, collective properties and emergent behaviour? This proposed addition needs a separate scope and prerequisite review.

05 / POSSIBLE PATHS

Choose an emphasis, keep the physics.

Paths are coordinated choices of investigations, specialist option and final project. They are not separate degrees or extra credits hidden within a module.

  • Astrophysics & cosmology: astronomical investigations in Stage 2, A301 or A302 in Stage 3, and a related project. The OU distinguishes stellar/exoplanetary study from cosmology; keeping both as separate options preserves their depth.
  • Computational physics: emphasis on numerical investigation, C301, and a project whose central contribution is a validated physical model or analysis.
  • Mathematical & theoretical physics: greater emphasis on derivations and modelling, T301 or T302, and an analytical or computational project. This does not promise comprehensive coverage of all theoretical physics.
  • Matter & emergence: thermal and quantum investigations, the proposed P303 option, and a project connecting microscopic and macroscopic behaviour.
  • Broad physics: choose any eligible option and use the project to connect subjects across the programme.

Room to continue: completing both astrophysics options, or another additional option, would add 30 credits beyond the core programme, producing 390 in the extended record. It would earn a separate specialist certificate rather than displace electromagnetism, quantum physics or the project. Every further option adds its own workload and evidence.

06 / AWARDS & EVIDENCE

Milestones worth marking.

Proposed award names are deliberately our own. LibraUni awards are independent, non-accredited acknowledgements of demonstrated learning, with no OU affiliation or recognised degree status. Exact wording and visual design will be agreed before any award is issued.

Proposed LibraUni awards
MilestoneAwardEvidence required
Stage 1 · 120 cumulative creditsLibraUni Certificate in the Foundations of PhysicsDemonstrated mathematical and introductory physical outcomes across the approved Stage 1 route.
Stage 2 · 240 cumulative creditsLibraUni Diploma in PhysicsStage 1 plus the common core, mathematical methods and assessed investigation reports.
Stage 3 · 360 cumulative creditsLibraUni Diploma in Advanced PhysicsAll three stages, the selected option and a successfully defended independent project. The transcript records the pathway emphasis.
Additional specialist studyLibraUni Specialist Certificate in the named subjectA separately completed and assessed additional option; no double-counting of the same work.

Each award will have a designed diploma, the LibraUni mark and seal, an issue date and identifier, and a private transcript listing modules, internal credits and the evidence behind completion. A portfolio can be shared only at the learner’s choice. No awards or credits have yet been earned.

Assessment will combine practice with feedback, independent problem solving, computational and experimental reports, and discussion of the learner’s reasoning. Reading completion and demonstrated understanding remain separate. Pass criteria, reassessment and any distinction system must be established before assessed teaching begins; no automatic awards for page visits.

07 / KEEPING THE CONTEXT

A living map, not a moving target.

Every module will state what it contributes to the programme, what it builds on and what it enables next. Every unit and lesson will locate itself within that module, connect back to earlier ideas and show the next destination. Periodic reviews will revisit the whole map and the evidence of progress.

The programme will carry a versioned decision record. Later changes must identify what changed, why, and whether prerequisites, workload or awards are affected. Completed work will retain its recorded outcomes rather than being silently reclassified.

Workload convention: use 10 notional study hours per internal credit as a planning convention: 300 hours per 30-credit module and 3,600 across the programme. Actual time can vary with prior knowledge and needed practice. At an illustrative 10.5 hours a week over 46 study weeks each year, the full allocation is about 7.5 years. That is a planning illustration, not a deadline or an equivalence guarantee.

Our adaptations: original, self-contained teaching; Python throughout; free required resources; guided tutoring; flexible pacing; and carefully identified practical limitations. We borrow the OU’s organising logic, not its paid teaching materials. Improvements will be judged by clarity, coherence, evidence and fit—not presumed superiority.

From blueprint to a module you can preview

Before designing units, review reference teaching materials for their organisation and activity structure. Then cross-check the programme against two research-university undergraduate physics curricula, mapping traditional subjects and learning outcomes to modules and units. Record gaps, prerequisites and intentional revisits, distinguishing common-core coverage from specialist choices.

Develop the complete outline through Unit level, stage by stage and one module at a time. Each module preview will include its purpose, outcomes, prerequisites, every unit and its scope, expected hours and assessment workload. Review each module before proceeding to the next.

Enrolment starts teaching-material production. Once a module is selected for study, develop one lesson or a smaller portion at a time: careful explanations, graduated examples and exercises, verified graphics and captions, curated free references, and relevant history and scientific anecdotes. Outlines and available lessons will be clearly distinguished.
