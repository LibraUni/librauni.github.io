01 / METHOD

Compare content, not prestige or titles.

The OU provides the organisational model. MIT Course 8 supplies a demanding physics-depth reference; Imperial’s Physics BSc supplies a second, UK-style breadth reference. These programmes have different entry preparation, credit conventions and elective structures. We do not equate their first year with our foundation stage or convert their credits into ours.

This is an initial subject-level comparison, not a declaration that the degree is complete or equivalent. The outcome descriptions and destinations below are our design judgments derived from the published scope. A full match requires detailed unit outcomes, expected demand, prerequisites, hours and assessed evidence.

Status key: “Outlined” means a draft unit-level home exists, not that teaching exists or mastery has been demonstrated. “Pending” means only a module-level home is proposed. “Gap to resolve” means the blueprint lacks an explicit required allocation. “Depth to resolve” means a subject is named but its depth and workload are unproven. Practical limitations and deliberately optional subjects are separate.

We compare the shared undergraduate physics foundation plus selected specialisations, not every elective or every university-wide requirement. Source dates and retrieval limits are recorded below.

02 / FIRST FINDINGS

The broad blueprint needs sharper boundaries.

MIT’s focused route includes analytical mechanics and an extended quantum sequence. Imperial’s published core includes solid-state and nuclear/particle physics. These checks expose vague or missing homes in our blueprint; they do not justify simply adding more topics to an unchanged timetable.

MIT degree reference · Imperial course reference

G01 · OPEN

Make the missing subject homes explicit

When designing the relevant modules, specify compulsory outcomes, units, assessment and hours for analytical mechanics, solid-state basics, nuclear/particle physics and probability/data methods. Until then these gaps remain open.

Coverage rows: C06, C11, C12, C13.

G02 · OPEN

Protect depth within the fixed credit budget

Audit P201’s 600 hours and P302’s 300 hours before adding topic commitments. If breadth and depth do not fit, propose a visible reallocation or an extension; do not quietly increase required work or claim every benchmark outcome is covered.

Coverage rows: C09, C10.

G03 · OPEN

State the practical boundary honestly

Separate outcomes demonstrable through simulations/data from outcomes requiring real apparatus or collaborators. Investigate free opportunities later; do not certify unavailable experiences.

Coverage rows: C15.

G04 · OPEN

Finish the evidence audit progressively

Retrieve detailed authoritative syllabi and assessments for the subject being designed. Imperial’s accessible indexed material supports a first comparison only; fuller module-level verification remains required.

Coverage rows: C02, C03, C04, C05, C07, C08, C14, C16, C17.

These are tracked decisions for later module design, not silent changes to the agreed 360-credit architecture. Resolving a gap requires an explicit curriculum and workload decision.

04 / FIRST MODULE AUDIT

A defined precalculus foundation.

LU-M101 Mathematical foundations now has twelve proposed units and seven assessable outcomes. Its role is preparatory. It does not claim to deliver MIT calculus or Imperial first-year physics.

Intentional overlap is documented: M101 teaches algebra and graphs; later modules retrieve them to introduce new mathematics or derive physical laws. Repeating the same introductory course would need a specific remediation reason, not a second allocation by default.

Outline checks: 240 unit hours plus 60 module-wide hours; 300 total. Every outcome has a unit home and independent assessment home. Dependencies refer to earlier units. These structural checks do not establish lesson quality, learner attainment or a complete degree audit.
