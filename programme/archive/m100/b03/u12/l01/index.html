PROTOTYPE ARCHIVE

From a question to a model

Compare model and observations using residuals: observed minus predicted. Inspect their size and pattern without treating a small residual as proof of a mechanism. Worked example

For a supplied constant-speed model s=2+3t and synthetic observations(0,2),(1,5.1),(2,7.9), predictions are2,5,8 m and residuals0,0.1,−0.1 m. These small discrepancies describe fit to three supplied values; they do not prove constant velocity outside the interval or reveal measurement uncertainty.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Calculate the predicted position at3 s and state the extrapolation assumption.
  2. What changes if every observed position has a systematic+1 m offset?
  3. List one dimensional, one boundary and one numerical check for this model.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. 11 m, assuming the same relationship continues.
  2. Residuals shift by+1 m; an offset can be confused with a changed intercept.
  3. For example vt has metres; t=0 gives2 m; at t=2 the formula gives8 m.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.

Carry forward: Continue with the practice and check your reasoning before moving on.

SECTION 2 · ABOUT 160 MINUTES

Work, explain and check

Choose a representation before calculating. Draw or tabulate the quantities, show intermediate steps, and keep domains and units alongside the result. Numerical agreement is useful, but distinguish a checked example from a general argument.

Worked example

For a supplied constant-speed model s=2+3t and synthetic observations(0,2),(1,5.1),(2,7.9), predictions are2,5,8 m and residuals0,0.1,−0.1 m. These small discrepancies describe fit to three supplied values; they do not prove constant velocity outside the interval or reveal measurement uncertainty.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Redo the first problem with a labelled diagram or table.
  2. Explain one likely mistake and how your check would detect it.
  3. Change one input in the worked example; predict the direction of change before calculating.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. The representation must preserve the given quantities and units. Compare it with your original result.
  2. Use the worked reasoning to identify a specific sign, domain, scale or interpretation error.
  3. State the changed input explicitly and check by substitution; ask your tutor if the alternative result is unclear.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.
