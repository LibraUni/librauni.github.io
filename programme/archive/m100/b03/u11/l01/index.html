PROTOTYPE ARCHIVE

Summaries, variation and probability

The mean is sum divided by count; the median is the middle ordered value, averaging the two middle values for an even count. Range is maximum minus minimum. Population standard deviation is the square root of the average squared departure from the mean; the sample convention divides by n−1 instead. State which convention you use. Random variation creates scatter; a systematic offset can shift all readings together. Low scatter does not establish accuracy. A sample is not automatically representative of its population.

Worked example

For values1,2,3 the mean and median are2. Squared departures1,0,1 sum to2. Population SD is√(2/3); sample SD is1. For a fair six-sided die, P(even)=3/6=1/2. Complements satisfy P(not A)=1−P(A). Independent event probabilities multiply; independence must be justified, not assumed from different names.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Find mean, median and range of2,3,3,8.
  2. For two independent fair coin tosses, find P(two heads) and P(at least one head).
  3. Can repeated readings all0.2 m too large still have small scatter?
Feedback and worked answers
  1. Mean4, median3, range6.
  2. 1/4 and3/4, using complement of no heads.
  3. Yes; a systematic offset can coexist with good repeatability.
