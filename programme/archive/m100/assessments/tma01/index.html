M100 · ASSESSMENT VERSION 1

TMA 01

Numbers and algebra: explain, check and interpret

Archived prototype assessment · retained for historical reference. Submissions are closed.

Working independently

Notes, these lessons, a calculator and documentation for taught Python syntax are permitted. Write your own answers and code. Do not use generated solutions, agents or another person’s answers. Ask for clarification if wording is unclear; record any assistance that affects your work. Reading a page does not begin or submit an attempt.

Allow 6 hours: the six question groups below total 330 minutes, followed by 30 minutes to scan, check and submit. This is separate from the 80 unit hours. Total: 100 marks. Show mathematical reasoning by hand and submit a legible PDF with pages in order. Submit Q6 as an .ipynb notebook with outputs. Typed accessibility alternatives are welcome. Include your name, M100, TMA 01 and question numbers.

Marking awards credit for valid method, correct calculations, units/restrictions, independent checks and clear interpretation. A carried arithmetic error can still earn subsequent method credit. Unexplained answers may lose method marks. Feedback identifies specific corrections and essential skills needing follow-up; an aggregate score alone does not establish mastery.

Q1 · A clear calculation record

15 marks · about 45 minutes

  1. Evaluate 5/6 − 3/8 + 1/4 exactly. Show the common denominator and a plausibility check. (6 marks)
  2. Evaluate −4 + 3(2 − 5)², showing operation order. (4 marks)
  3. A recorded interval changes from 48 s to 54 s. Find its percentage increase and explain the reference base. Report a sensible precision. (5 marks)

Q2 · Units and scaling

15 marks · about 45 minutes

  1. Convert 6400 cm² to m² and 90 km/h to m/s, showing unit factors. (6 marks)
  2. Using the supplied model t = d/v, calculate travel time for d = 7.2 × 10⁶ m and v = 2.4 × 10³ m/s. Give scientific notation and units. (4 marks)
  3. For I = k/r² with k fixed, predict the factor in I when r is halved. Explain why dimensions alone cannot distinguish d = vt from d = 3vt. (5 marks)

Q3 · Algebra and the scope of a claim

20 marks · about 60 minutes

  1. Simplify 4(2x − 3) − 3(x + 1), with steps. (5 marks)
  2. Simplify (6x² − 12x)/(3x), stating the original restriction and explaining why it remains. (6 marks)
  3. A claim says (x + 3)² = x² + 9 for every real x. Give a permitted counterexample and an algebraic explanation of the missing term. (5 marks)
  4. Explain why two agreeing numerical tests cannot establish an identity, while one permitted counterexample can disprove it. (4 marks)

Q4 · Solve, rearrange and interpret

20 marks · about 75 minutes

  1. Solve (x + 1)/3 + x/2 = 7, checking the original equation. (6 marks)
  2. Rearrange p = ax + b for x. Describe all possibilities when a = 0. (6 marks)
  3. Exact observations in s = s₀ + vt give s = 11 m at t = 2 s and s = 23 m at t = 5 s. Determine both parameters, check both observations and state the model assumption. (8 marks)

Q5 · Bounds and boundaries

15 marks · about 45 minutes

  1. Solve 7 − 3x ≤ 16, giving interval notation and a boundary check. (7 marks)
  2. A supplied model s = 4 + 2t (metres, seconds) is valid for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10. Find all model times for which s < 16. Explain the inclusion or exclusion of each endpoint. (8 marks)

Q6 · An independently written notebook

15 marks · about 60 minutes

  1. From a blank notebook, use named quantities for the supplied model s = 3 + 4t, in metres and seconds. Explain the units in Markdown. Use an if/else guard for a single elapsed time, rejecting t < 0. (5 marks)
  2. Use a list and for loop to evaluate the model at t = 0, 2, 5. Predict the outputs in Markdown first. Add an assert checking one exact integer result. Explain the limits of this check. (6 marks)
  3. Restart and run in order; save with visible outputs. Include a short account of one check or correction you made. Do not use functions, libraries or generated code for this task. (4 marks)
