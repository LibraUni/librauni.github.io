SECTION 1 · ABOUT 84 MINUTES

Integer powers and their laws

For a positive integer n, aⁿ means n factors of a multiplied together. It is not a×n. Multiplying powers with the same base joins the factors: aᵐaⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Dividing cancels factors: aᵐ/aⁿ=aᵐ⁻ⁿ when a≠0. Consistency with a/a=1 gives a⁰=1 for a≠0; a negative exponent denotes a reciprocal, a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ. Zero to a negative power is undefined. We do not assign a value to 0⁰ here. A power of a product distributes across factors; a power of a sum generally does not.

Worked example

2³×2²=(2×2×2)(2×2)=2⁵=32. Also 2⁻³=1/8. But(2+3)²=25 whereas 2²+3²=13: the square of a sum contains cross terms. Parentheses identify the base: (−3)²=9 while −3²=−9 means the negative of 3².

SECTION 2 · ABOUT 96 MINUTES

Roots, rational powers and real domains

The principal square root √x is the non-negative number whose square is x; it is real only for x≥0. Thus√9=3, although the equation y²=9 has two solutions,3 and −3. Odd roots can be negative: the real cube root of −8 is −2. For positive a, define aᵖ⁄ᑫ as the qth root of aᵖ; positive-base exponent laws remain consistent. At zero, negative powers are excluded. Negative bases with fractional exponents require careful domain choices; do not apply all positive-base exponent rules to them without justification. In this lesson use positive bases for rational-power manipulation, and discuss negative numbers only through explicit odd roots.

Worked example

16³⁄⁴=(fourth root of 16)³=2³=8. To check,8⁴=4096 and 16³=4096. Also√(25/36)=5/6. The assertion√(x²)=x is false for negative x: for x=−4,√16=4. The correct real statement is√(x²)=|x|.

SECTION 3 · ABOUT 60 MINUTES

Python powers and safe roots

Python writes powers with **. Exponentiation binds more tightly than unary minus, so -3**2 gives −9; (-3)**2 gives 9. An import makes a library available: import math lets you call math.sqrt(...). The dot selects a function provided by that library; you are using a supplied function, not yet defining your own. math.sqrt expects a non-negative real input. A ValueError indicates an unacceptable value for that operation. Do not treat a fractional power of a negative base in Python as a general real odd-root calculator.

Worked example

The example prints −9,9 and 5.0. A deliberate math.sqrt(-1) raises ValueError. The failure is not missing punctuation: the input is outside the operation’s real domain. Remove or isolate that deliberate error before a clean run of the rest of your notebook.

Type, predict, run

import math
print(-3**2)
print((-3)**2)
print(math.sqrt(25))
