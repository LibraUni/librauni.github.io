SECTION 1 · ABOUT 84 MINUTES

Quantities, units and conversion factors

A physical quantity is represented by a numerical value and a unit. One metre and 100 centimetres describe the same length. A conversion factor is a ratio of equal quantities, so multiplying by it changes the representation without changing the quantity. Use the orientation that cancels the old unit. Prefixes denote powers of ten: kilo 10³, centi 10⁻², milli 10⁻³ and micro 10⁻⁶. A prefix belongs to the unit symbol, not to the physical kind of quantity.

Worked example

Convert 250 cm to m:250 cm×(1 m/100 cm)=2.5 m. The centimetres cancel. Using 100 cm/1 m instead would leave cm²/m, revealing a wrong orientation. Convert 3 ms to s:3×10⁻³ s=0.003 s.

SECTION 2 · ABOUT 96 MINUTES

Compound and squared units

When a unit is squared, square the entire conversion factor. Since 1 cm=0.01 m,1 cm²=(0.01 m)²=0.0001 m². A square with sides 1 cm has area one ten-thousandth of a square metre, not one hundredth. For compound units, convert numerator and denominator separately. Keep parentheses around factors so their powers are explicit.

Worked example

72 km/h×(1000 m/km)×(1 h/3600 s)=20 m/s. For area,2500 cm²×(1 m/100 cm)²=0.25 m². A separate geometric check: a50 cm square has side 0.5 m and area 0.25 m².

SECTION 3 · ABOUT 30 MINUTES

A conversion that does not hide its units

Ordinary Python arithmetic does not know that a number represents kilometres, hours or metres. You must perform conversions explicitly and document the convention. A correctly executed calculation with mismatched units can be physically wrong. Put unit conversion before combining quantities, and compare with a hand-calculated result.

Worked example

The example converts 72 km/h to 20 m/s. Multiplying only by 1000 would give 72000 with the old “per hour” still unresolved. A result labelled m/s after that incomplete conversion would be mislabelled.

Type, predict, run

speed_km_per_h = 72
speed_m_per_s = speed_km_per_h * 1000 / 3600
print(speed_m_per_s)

SECTION 4 · ABOUT 60 MINUTES

Compare journeys on one consistent scale

To compare journeys, express distance and time consistently before taking their ratio. Average speed is total distance divided by elapsed time in this supplied model. It does not reveal how the speed varied within the journey. Converting after calculating is possible, but mixing unmatched units partway through a calculation makes mistakes harder to spot.

Worked example

Journey A covers 600 m in 2 min:2 min=120 s, so its average speed is 5 m/s. Journey B covers 0.9 km in 150 s:900/150=6 m/s. B has the higher average speed. This comparison says nothing about either journey’s maximum speed.

SECTION 5 · ABOUT 30 MINUTES

Conversion checkpoint

A conversion checkpoint should test the factor as well as the arithmetic. Keep the unit symbols on paper even when the notebook stores only numeric values. If an answer seems too large, ask whether changing to a larger unit should increase or decrease its numerical value.

Worked example

A square area recorded as 400 cm² becomes 0.04 m², because 400×10⁻⁴=0.04. The square could have side 20 cm=0.2 m;0.2²=0.04 confirms the result by another route.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Correct 400 cm²=4 m².
  2. Convert 0.08 m² to cm².
  3. Convert 108 km/h to m/s.
  4. Explain why 1 m/s² is not converted like 1 m²/s.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. The length factor was not squared; correct value 0.04 m².
  2. 800 cm².
  3. 30 m/s.
  4. The first squares the time unit in the denominator; the second squares the length unit in the numerator. Their dimensions differ.
