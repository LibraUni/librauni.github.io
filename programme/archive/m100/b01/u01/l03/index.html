SECTION 1 · ABOUT 72 MINUTES

A percentage needs a reference base

Percent means “per hundred”: p% of a quantity B is (p/100)B. The reference B must be stated. Percentage change from an original value A to a new value N is 100(N−A)/A, provided A ≠ 0. The sign describes increase or decrease. A percentage point is a difference between two percentages, not a relative change. A rate rising from 20% to 25% rises by 5 percentage points; relative to its original 20%, that is a 25% increase.

Worked example

A duration rises from 80 s to 100 s. The increase is 20 s, and 20/80 = 1/4 = 25%. Reversing the comparison, the decrease from 100 s to 80 s is 20/100 = 20%. The same difference has different percentages because the bases differ.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Find 15% of 240 and 2.5% of 80.
  2. A value rises from 50 to 65. Find the percentage increase.
  3. It then falls from 65 to 50. Is this a 30% decrease?
  4. A fraction changes from 40% to 50%. Give the percentage-point change and relative percentage change.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. 36 and 2.
  2. 30%.
  3. No: the decrease is 15/65 ×100 ≈ 23.08%.
  4. 10 percentage points; 25% relative increase.

SECTION 2 · ABOUT 78 MINUTES

Estimate first, round last

Estimation is a deliberate simplification used to predict scale or catch mistakes. It is not a substitute for the final calculation. Round inputs to easy nearby values, calculate mentally and state whether your approximation tends to increase or decrease the result. Round the final answer only after intermediate calculations are complete. Decimal places count digits after the point; significant figures start at the first non-zero digit. For measured quantities, extra display digits do not establish better knowledge of the measurement. Without an uncertainty model, describe rounding as a reporting choice, not a formal accuracy guarantee.

Worked example

Estimate 19.8 × 4.9 using 20 × 5 = 100. Both positive factors were increased, so 100 is an upper estimate. The exact decimal multiplication is 97.02. To three significant figures it is 97.0; the trailing zero records the chosen precision. By contrast 0.004706 to two significant figures is 0.0047, not 0.00.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  2. Round 0.007846 to two and three significant figures.
  3. Round 12.346 to two decimal places and three significant figures.
  4. Compare rounding 1/3 to 0.3 before multiplying by 30 with retaining 1/3 until the end.
  5. A measurement is recorded as about 2 m. Explain why printing 2.000000 m does not improve it.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. About 100; calculated value 102.9.
  2. 0.0078 and 0.00785.
  3. 12.35 and 12.3.
  4. Premature rounding gives 9 rather than 10. The lost information propagates.
  5. Formatting adds digits, not observations or knowledge about error.

SECTION 3 · ABOUT 60 MINUTES

Change the input, explain the output

When Python raises NameError, it could not resolve a name at the point of execution. Python distinguishes upper- and lower-case names: Time and time are different. Check spelling and whether the cell assigning the name has run. Do not simply rerun random cells until an error disappears: restart and run in order to establish a reproducible sequence. Reassignment changes future calculations, but does not retroactively change values already computed.

Worked example

Set original = 80 and new = 100, then change_percent = 100 * (new - original) / original. This gives 25.0. If you change original to 100 without recomputing change_percent, the old stored result remains. A notebook’s visible output can also be stale after code has been edited but not rerun.

Type, predict, run

original = 80
new = 100
change_percent = 100 * (new - original) / original
print(change_percent)

SECTION 4 · ABOUT 30 MINUTES

Reporting a measurement comparison

A comparison of measurements needs both a named reference and a statement about what is known. A difference between two recorded values is not automatically evidence of a physical change: measurement variation could contribute. At this stage we calculate the difference and relative difference; later physics will treat uncertainty systematically. Avoid the word “error” unless you have an independently justified reference value.

Worked example

Two recorded lengths are 2.40 m and 2.46 m. Their difference is 0.06 m. Relative to 2.40 m this is 2.5%. We can report that numerical comparison, but cannot conclude from these two numbers alone that an object truly expanded by 2.5%.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Compare 12.0 s with 12.6 s using 12.0 s as reference.
  2. Repeat using 12.6 s as reference.
  3. Write a three-sentence report giving the reference, difference and limitation.
  4. Choose sensible reporting precision for your percentage and explain the choice.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. 0.6 s; 5%.
  2. 0.6/12.6 ×100 ≈ 4.76%.
  3. For example: relative to the 12.0 s record, the later value is 0.6 s or 5% higher. No uncertainty information is supplied. The calculation alone does not establish a physical change.
  4. A small number of significant figures is appropriate; explain the choice rather than treating every calculator digit as measured information.
