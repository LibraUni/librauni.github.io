PROTOTYPE ARCHIVE

Quadratics and excluded inputs

Factorisation can reveal quadratic roots: x²−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3). A product is zero when at least one factor is zero. Completing the square gives x²−4x+1=(x−2)²−3, identifying vertex (2,−3). For ax²+bx+c=0 with a≠0, roots are (−b±√(b²−4ac))/(2a). The discriminant D=b²−4ac distinguishes two, one repeated, or no real roots. A rational expression remains undefined where its original denominator is zero; plotted lines must not bridge that gap.

SECTION 1 · ABOUT 200 MINUTES

Understand the relationship

Factorisation can reveal quadratic roots: x²−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3). A product is zero when at least one factor is zero. Completing the square gives x²−4x+1=(x−2)²−3, identifying vertex (2,−3). For ax²+bx+c=0 with a≠0, roots are (−b±√(b²−4ac))/(2a). The discriminant D=b²−4ac distinguishes two, one repeated, or no real roots. A rational expression remains undefined where its original denominator is zero; plotted lines must not bridge that gap.

Worked example

x²−4x+1=0 gives x=2±√3. For 1/(x−1), x=1 is excluded and the magnitude grows without bound as x approaches 1. The curve approaches zero for large |x|. This is a qualitative asymptote description, not a formal limit proof.

Work it through

Show intermediate steps and give units or restrictions where relevant. For code, record a prediction before running.

  1. Solve x²−7x+12=0 and check both roots.
  2. Classify the real roots of x²+2x+1 and x²+1.
  3. A supplied trajectory h=6+5t−t² is valid for t≥0 until h=0. Find that ending time.
Feedback and worked answers
  1. (x−3)(x−4)=0; roots3,4.
  2. D=0 gives repeated root−1; D=−4 gives no real roots.
  3. t²−5t−6=(t−6)(t+1); choose6 s, rejecting−1 s.

If an answer differs, locate the first step where your reasoning changes. Correct that step, close this feedback, and solve it again independently.
