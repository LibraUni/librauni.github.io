01 / PURPOSE & READINESS

A foundation with a clear destination.

Entry: No calculus or programming is assumed. Recognising basic arithmetic operations and reading a simple table is sufficient to begin; fractions, signed numbers and algebra are rebuilt. A readiness conversation will identify any additional support. Existing qualifications do not automatically establish readiness or earn exemption.

Exit: Prepare for LU-M102 Mathematics for physics I and support LU-P101 and the computational readiness needed by LU-A101. On the astronomy route this is optional preparation outside the degree allocation; on the foundation route it counts within Stage 1. Completion establishes a precalculus foundation, not completion of university calculus or mechanics.

Everyday quantitative reasoning→M101 · mathematical fluency→M102 · calculus & P101 · physical models

Our reference comparison distinguishes entry knowledge from degree content. MIT’s 18.01SC syllabus assumes algebra and trigonometry before calculus. M101 deliberately teaches those foundations rather than presenting them as a substitute for calculus. OU MU123 is a useful foundations reference; this original outline is not a copy or a certified equivalent.

If later readiness evidence makes the full module unnecessary, the programme allows an alternative Stage 1 route, subject to review. No exemption has been awarded and no diagnostic is being set here.

U01 · 16 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Numbers, notation & reliable calculation

Before this unit: Module entry readiness; no previous unit.

Scope
Signed numbers; operation order; fractions, decimals and percentages; absolute value; number lines; exact versus approximate values; rounding and estimation. Introduce mathematical statements and the difference between an example and a general claim.
By the end
Calculate with fractions and signs, justify an estimate and explain the precision retained in a result.
Computing strand
Open and save a notebook; run arithmetic cells; distinguish mathematical notation from Python operators and a displayed approximation from an exact value.
Physical context
Compare everyday and astronomical numerical scales without requiring astronomy knowledge.
Evidence to look for
Short hand calculations with estimates and an explained computer cross-check.
Boundary with later study
Powers, roots and scientific notation are developed next; floating-point error analysis belongs later.

Contributes to O1, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U02 · 18 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Powers, units & proportional reasoning

Before this unit: U01

Scope
Integer and rational powers, roots and real-number restrictions; scientific notation; ratios and direct/inverse proportionality; SI prefixes, compound-unit conversions, dimensions and scale factors.
By the end
Compare scales, convert a derived quantity consistently and test a proposed relation for dimensional compatibility.
Computing strand
Use named variables and math functions; attach units in names, labels and explanations rather than treating plain numbers as automatically unit-aware.
Physical context
Light-travel times and inverse-square scaling with all physical relations supplied.
Evidence to look for
A unit conversion and a scaling argument with an order-of-magnitude check.
Boundary with later study
Dimensional consistency is a necessary check, not proof of a physical law. Dimensional-analysis theorems are deferred.

Contributes to O1, O5, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U03 · 22 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Algebra as a language

Before this unit: U01, U02

Scope
Variables, constants and parameters; substitution; collecting terms; distributivity, brackets and simple factorisation; algebraic fractions; equivalent expressions and excluded values; identities versus equations.
By the end
Translate a verbal relationship into symbols and simplify it without changing its domain unnoticed.
Computing strand
Evaluate two proposed expressions at selected inputs and recognise that numerical agreement does not prove an identity.
Physical context
Rearrangements used in measurement formulas, with no new physics assumed.
Evidence to look for
A justified simplification and a counterexample to an invalid cancellation.
Boundary with later study
Full quadratic factorisation is reserved for U07; symbolic-algebra software is not required.

Contributes to O2, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U04 · 24 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Equations, inequalities & changing the subject

Before this unit: U03

Scope
Linear equations; formulas with several variables; two simultaneous linear equations; inequalities and intervals; sign changes; extraneous solutions; checking answers by substitution.
By the end
Choose a solution method, retain restrictions and distinguish no solution, one solution and an underdetermined relationship.
Computing strand
Use Boolean comparisons to check candidates, alongside a written algebraic argument.
Physical context
Infer two unknown quantities from two supplied measurement relations.
Evidence to look for
An independently explained formula rearrangement, system of equations and inequality.
Boundary with later study
General matrix solution methods belong to M102/M103. Nonlinear root-finding algorithms are deferred.

Contributes to O2, O5, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U05 · 20 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Coordinates, graphs & linear models

Before this unit: U02, U04

Scope
Axes, scales and units; ordered pairs; slope and intercept; line equations and intersections; piecewise linear graphs; secant slopes and average rates; interpolation versus extrapolation.
By the end
Recover and interpret a linear relationship from a graph or table and identify when a visual presentation is misleading.
Computing strand
Create a labelled plot with supplied plotting scaffolding; introduce lists or arrays only as needed to represent coordinate pairs.
Physical context
Position–time and calibration plots: distinguish a graph of a quantity from a picture of motion.
Evidence to look for
A graph, its mathematical model and a plain-language interpretation with units.
Boundary with later study
A secant slope is not a derivative. Least-squares fitting and inferential claims belong to X201.

Contributes to O3, O5, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U06 · 24 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Functions, transformations & inverse relationships

Before this unit: U03, U05

Scope
Function notation, inputs and outputs, domain and range; multiple representations; composition; translations and rescaling; symmetry; piecewise and absolute-value functions; one-to-one restrictions and inverses.
By the end
Trace a composition, specify a meaningful domain and distinguish an inverse function from a reciprocal.
Computing strand
Define a small Python function, generate values and check behaviour against a sketch and known cases.
Physical context
Convert between a sensor reading and a modelled physical quantity.
Evidence to look for
A function interpreted in three representations and an inverse with a justified domain.
Boundary with later study
Limits and continuity become explicit concepts in M102; no hidden calculus requirement here.

Contributes to O2, O3, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U07 · 22 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Quadratic & rational relationships

Before this unit: U04, U06

Scope
Polynomial arithmetic at elementary level; quadratic factorisation, completing the square and quadratic formula; discriminants and real roots; parabolas; rational expressions, excluded inputs and qualitative asymptotes.
By the end
Select a suitable quadratic method and connect its solutions to a graph, while checking the original equation.
Computing strand
Compare a plot with analytical roots and investigate a plotting artefact near an excluded input.
Physical context
Geometric constraints and supplied trajectory formulas; no mechanics derivation is assumed.
Evidence to look for
A quadratic problem solved and checked in two representations; a rational-domain explanation.
Boundary with later study
Complex roots, general polynomial theory and partial fractions are later work. Asymptotes are qualitative here.

Contributes to O2, O3, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U08 · 24 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Geometry, angles & trigonometric relationships

Before this unit: U02, U06, U07

Scope
Similarity and Pythagoras; circle, area and volume formulas; degrees and radians; unit-circle sine, cosine and tangent; triangle methods including sine/cosine rules; basic identities; periodic graphs; inverse trig and elementary trig equations on stated intervals.
By the end
Choose a geometric construction, resolve a triangle and interpret a periodic graph with a correct angle convention.
Computing strand
Plot periodic functions using radians and check special angles and period against hand reasoning.
Physical context
Sightlines, triangulation and a supplied oscillation model.
Evidence to look for
A labelled geometric diagram and a justified trigonometric solution, including ambiguous cases where relevant.
Boundary with later study
Advanced identities, complex-exponential representations and calculus of trig functions are deferred. This is the densest unit; its hours need pilot review.

Contributes to O3, O4, O5, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U09 · 22 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Exponential change, logarithms & scale

Before this unit: U02, U04, U06

Scope
Exponential functions and repeated factors; logarithms as inverses, bases and laws; simple exponential equations; logarithmic axes; linearisation of supplied exponential and power-law models; positive, dimensionless log arguments.
By the end
Distinguish additive, multiplicative and power-law change, and interpret the scale and restrictions of a logarithmic plot.
Computing strand
Compare ordinary, semilog and log–log plots; check a recovered parameter by substitution.
Physical context
Half-life and brightness-ratio models, with their physical assumptions stated rather than derived.
Evidence to look for
Interpret a model parameter and explain why a particular axis scale is useful.
Boundary with later study
Differential-equation derivations of exponential behaviour and derivatives of logarithms belong later.

Contributes to O2, O3, O5, O6. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U10 · 18 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Vectors & the geometry of direction

Before this unit: U05, U08

Scope
Scalars versus vectors; two-dimensional components; addition, subtraction and scaling; magnitude and direction; unit-vector notation; Cartesian/polar conversion; projections and the geometric dot product.
By the end
Move between a diagram and components, combine vectors and check a projection using geometry.
Computing strand
Represent vectors as small arrays and compare component calculations with a labelled diagram.
Physical context
Displacement and vector quantities in a supplied model; physical laws are taught in P101.
Evidence to look for
A diagram, component solution and independent magnitude/direction check.
Boundary with later study
Three-dimensional cross products, vector spaces, eigenvectors and vector calculus belong to M102/M103/M201.

Contributes to O1, O4, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U11 · 14 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

Data, variation & elementary probability

Before this unit: U02, U05, U06

Scope
Data types; mean, median and descriptive spread including standard deviation; simple distributions and plots; samples versus populations; systematic versus random effects; finite sample spaces, complements and simple independent events.
By the end
Describe a small dataset honestly, distinguish precision from accuracy and interpret a simple probability without claiming certainty.
Computing strand
Summarise and plot supplied repeated observations, explain every reported quantity and compare selected calculations by hand.
Physical context
Repeated measurements and their variation. Observed spread is not automatically the uncertainty of a mean.
Evidence to look for
A descriptive data note and a finite-probability calculation with stated assumptions.
Boundary with later study
Conditional probability, continuous distributions, inference, uncertainty propagation and weighted fitting must be allocated later; this is orientation, not a statistics course.

Contributes to O1, O5, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

U12 · 16 HOURS · OUTLINE ONLY

From a question to a defensible model

Before this unit: U07, U08, U09, U10, U11

Scope
Combine existing tools to define a question, select representations, state assumptions, compare model and supplied observations, test limiting cases and explain limitations. No new mathematical topic is introduced.
By the end
Explain why a model is suitable, what its results mean and what the available evidence cannot establish.
Computing strand
Organise a small notebook with headings, readable calculations, labelled figures and a clean rerun; record source and units of supplied data.
Physical context
A guided geometric or scaling investigation linking a concrete observation with an abstract relationship.
Evidence to look for
A formative model review and oral or written explanation of method choices.
Boundary with later study
This rehearses integration; the independently assessed notebook has its own separate workload allocation.

Contributes to O1, O2, O3, O4, O5, O6, O7. Activities and assessment questions will be authored after the Stage 1 design and readiness review.

Assessment vocabulary: TMA means tutor-marked assignment; iCMA means interactive computer-marked assignment; EMA means end-of-module assessment. Three one-hour iCMAs are scheduled within existing unit checkpoint time, so they do not add hours. M101 ends with one EMA combining a Python modelling notebook, handwritten mathematics submitted as a scanned PDF, and explanation/discussion. There is one EMA deadline and no separate final examination. Papers, questions, submission links, allowed aids and grading rules will be published before use; no live assessment or upload is available yet.

Proposed completion rule: every module outcome must be demonstrated through independent work and the combined EMA, including explained Python results. Reading completion or a strong average must not conceal an unresolved foundational outcome. The review vocabulary is “not yet demonstrated”, “demonstrated in familiar work” and “demonstrated in transfer”; a final rubric and any numerical grade policy must be agreed before assessment begins.

Routine practice may use tutor support. Independent submissions must state the tools and assistance used, and must be followed by explanation or a fresh task where authorship or understanding is unclear. Feedback leads to targeted repair and reassessment, not an invented pass. All submissions and attainment records remain private.

06 / SCIENTIFIC PROGRAMMING

Write it. Understand it. Then delegate carefully.

The foundations begin with learner-written code and deliberate practice. Later work introduces AI assistance and bounded coding agents, with responsibility for the model, code and results staying with the learner. Abstract mathematical exercises remain; physics supplies context when it helps.

Budget: 48 of the existing 240 unit hours are allocated to integrated mathematical computing; 192 remain for other unit study. These are not 48 extra hours, and computing still practises the mathematics. TMA 02 and the EMA retain their separate existing budgets. This is a revised design allocation to check during authoring, not a measured learning-time claim.
