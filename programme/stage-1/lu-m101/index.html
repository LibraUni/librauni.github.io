B01 · Vectors and linear relationships

54 study hours, including 4 Python hours · Prior blocks: Stage 1 entry readiness · Outcomes: M101-O3, M101-O4, M101-O6

Purpose
Express direction and simultaneous relationships precisely, providing mathematical tools for early P101 mechanics.
Scope
Coordinate vectors in two and three dimensions; magnitude, components, addition and scalar multiplication; scalar products, projections and elementary vector products with geometric meaning. Matrices as transformations; matrix-vector multiplication, composition and small linear systems by elimination. Recognise inconsistent and underdetermined systems; interpret elementary determinants and invertibility in small examples.
Boundaries
Retrieve algebra and trigonometry within the block, not a second full bridge. No abstract vector-space axioms, general eigenproblem or multivariable calculus. M102 extends bases, orthogonality and eigenvectors. P101 teaches the physics of forces; mathematical applications here supply required physical assumptions.
Python
After P101 scalar/list/function induction, check a small vector or matrix calculation against hand work using readable code. No unexplained numerical solver. If the computing handover is late, written teaching can begin but independent code assessment waits; no extra M101 installation induction.
Expected evidence
Resolve a vector, explain a product geometrically, apply a transformation and solve/classify a small linear system. Explain a code check and its limits.
Forward handover
Vector components and projections must reach P101 before force-resolution tasks. Matrix methods prepare M102 linear algebra. Full block completion is not required before the first vector handover.
Assessment role
TMA 01 follows this block: vectors, transformations, small systems, mathematical explanation and an independently written short check. Six hours remain in the separate TMA envelope.

B02 · Change and local behaviour

72 study hours, including 8 Python hours · Prior blocks: B01 · Outcomes: M101-O1, M101-O5, M101-O6

Purpose
Develop derivatives from rates and limits, then use them to reason about the behaviour of functions.
Scope
Functions and domains as targeted retrieval; limits and continuity through examples and counterexamples; derivative from difference quotients. Elementary power, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric derivatives; product, quotient and chain rules; implicit differentiation and second derivatives. Curve behaviour, extrema, related rates and tangent-line approximation. State hypotheses when invoking a theorem; introduce the mean-value idea without a full real-analysis course.
Boundaries
No general epsilon-delta analysis course, infinite Taylor-series theory or ODE solution catalogue. Linear approximation is taught here; systematic series belong to M102. Keep substantial abstract practice alongside supplied physical rate models.
Python
Compare analytical derivatives with forward/central finite differences at ordinary and problematic points; vary step size and notice truncation versus round-off. Plot only after P101 array/plot handover. Python functions represent mathematics, not proof.
Expected evidence
Derive a simple derivative from its definition, select rules correctly, justify an extremum and explain when a derivative/linear approximation is unsuitable. Check a numerical result against analytical reasoning.
Forward handover
M101-O1 reaches P101 before calculus-based velocity/acceleration problems. Tangent approximation and derivative checks support M102 and later data/model investigations.
Assessment role
TMA 02 follows this block: differentiation, interpretation, local approximation and an explained independent numerical check. Six hours outside block study.

B03 · Accumulation and integral models

78 study hours, including 10 Python hours · Prior blocks: B02 · Outcomes: M101-O2, M101-O5, M101-O6

Purpose
Connect local rates with accumulated quantities and develop reliable analytical and numerical integration.
Scope
Definite integrals as limits of sums and signed accumulation; indefinite integrals and constants; fundamental theorem of calculus. Elementary antiderivatives, substitution and introductory integration by parts. Area, average values and simple volumes; supplied work and accumulated-flow models with dimensional interpretation. Rectangle/midpoint/trapezoidal estimates and refinement checks; explain the difference between an approximation and a justified error bound.
Boundaries
Further rational/trigonometric integration techniques, improper integrals and systematic series move to M102 for explicit allocation. Multiple and line/surface integrals remain M201. A signed integral need not equal geometrical area or distance; split the domain when needed. This is not a duplicate numerical-methods course.
Python
Construct a transparent quadrature calculation, compare with an analytic integral and a hand-computable subset, vary resolution and diagnose a misleading apparently converged value. Validate units and interval orientation.
Expected evidence
Explain an integral as accumulation, use the fundamental theorem with appropriate conditions, select a taught technique and connect signs/units to the result. Produce and critique an independent numerical integration check.
Forward handover
Integral accumulation and scalar-product geometry precede the corresponding P101 work examples. Integration foundations prepare M102 probability/ODEs and A101 interpretation of sampled signals.
Assessment role
TMA 03 follows this block: analytical integration, interpretation and independent quadrature work; cumulative earlier mathematics may be sampled. Six hours outside block study.

B04 · Approximation, validation and mathematical judgement

36 study hours, including 8 Python hours · Prior blocks: B01, B02, B03 · Outcomes: M101-O1, M101-O2, M101-O3, M101-O4, M101-O5, M101-O6

Purpose
Connect the module’s tools and decide when an answer or approximation deserves confidence.
Scope
Synthesis with a bounded supplied physical model: choose representations, identify domains/scales, compare local linear and accumulated predictions, vary a numerical resolution, and investigate sensitivity to inputs. Separate model assumptions, approximation error and arithmetic/computing mistakes. Explain agreement or disagreement between hand, graphical and computational results. Local Newton iteration may illustrate a derivative application after its assumptions and failure cases are introduced; no general numerical-analysis theory is implied.
Boundaries
This is taught mathematical validation and transfer, not the separately budgeted revision period or an extra EMA. Reuse methods from B01–B03 and deepen error reasoning; do not smuggle in complex numbers, eigenmethods, ODE solvers or Taylor-series theory. No required AI/agent operation.
Python
Combine established calculations into a short clean-runnable notebook, identify an edge case and repair a supplied flawed check. Optional assistance must be declared and does not substitute for independent TMA evidence.
Expected evidence
Formative synthesis and feedback: explain method choice, provide independent checks, challenge a plausible wrong result and communicate limitations. Final written exam samples transferable reasoning; executed-code evidence comes from the TMAs.
Forward handover
Prepare M102 and A101 to expect explained code, analytical checks, explicit assumptions and justified approximation. Do not declare those capabilities attained from participation alone.
Assessment role
Embedded formative checkpoint plus later final examination. No fourth TMA or additional assessment hours. Core coding capabilities have already been sampled independently in TMAs; practical extensions here remain formative.
