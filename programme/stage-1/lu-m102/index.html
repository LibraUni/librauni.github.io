B01 · Complex numbers and oscillatory representations

36 study hours, including 4 Python hours. Prior blocks: M101 and P101 readiness. Outcomes: M102-O1, M102-O7.

Purpose
Extend real-number methods to phase and oscillatory quantities.
Scope
Complex arithmetic, conjugation, modulus and argument; Cartesian, polar and exponential forms; Euler representation, elementary identities, integer powers and roots. Interpret phase geometrically and connect real/imaginary parts with supplied sinusoidal models.
Boundaries
No complex analysis, contour integration or general AC-circuit theory. Euler representation is introduced with a clear statement of what is established here; later series provide a connection, not a hidden prerequisite.
Python
Use built-in complex arithmetic and established arrays to check hand calculations and phase conventions. Explain branch/angle choices; numerical agreement is not proof.
Assessment evidence
Convert representations, calculate roots and justify a phase relation; independent hand reasoning and a small explained code check in TMA 01 after B02.
Forward handover
Supports B03 complex eigenvalue examples and B04 oscillatory solutions. A101 may use phase enrichment after this block, but required activities retain Semester 1 prerequisites.

B02 · Further integration, series and approximation

48 study hours, including 6 Python hours. Prior blocks: B01. Outcomes: M102-O2, M102-O7.

Purpose
Extend the calculus deliberately deferred from M101 and control approximations.
Scope
Selected rational integration by elementary partial fractions, trigonometric identities/substitution and further integration by parts. Improper integrals through limits and simple convergence tests. Sequences, geometric series, basic convergence/divergence reasoning and elementary Taylor polynomials/power series; domain of validity, truncation and examples of error control.
Boundaries
A bounded repertoire, not a complete integration catalogue or real-analysis course. Distinguish finite Taylor approximation from equality to an infinite series. No Fourier series, general convergence theorem proofs or special functions. Reserve explicit unit hours for each strand; revise openly if capacity fails.
Python
Compare approximations with known functions, vary order and domain, test an improper-integral cutoff against analytic reasoning. Software agreement cannot establish convergence.
Assessment evidence
Select and verify an integration method, classify simple improper integrals and justify a local approximation with its limitations. TMA 01 follows B02, sampling O1/O2/O7; six separately budgeted hours.
Forward handover
Supplies integration for B04 first-order equations and B05 densities; approximation/error habits prepare M201. A101 need not wait for Taylor-series teaching.

B03 · Bases, orthogonality and eigenproblems

48 study hours, including 6 Python hours. Prior blocks: B01, B02. Outcomes: M102-O3, M102-O7.

Purpose
Understand how a change of representation reveals the structure of a linear model.
Scope
Finite-dimensional span, independence, basis, dimension and rank in small examples; coordinate changes, orthogonality and projections; elementary orthonormalisation. Small eigenvalue/eigenvector problems, diagonalisation when possible, real symmetric examples and the significance of repeated or complex eigenvalues. Check results by substitution.
Boundaries
No full abstract linear-algebra course, general Jordan form, SVD or advanced spectral theory. Retrieve elimination and determinants from M101 rather than teach them twice. Least-squares/inference depth requires later allocation.
Python
Compare a hand-solved small eigenproblem with a numerical library result, check residuals, normalisation and ordering/sign ambiguity; perturb a simple input and explain what changes.
Assessment evidence
Explain a basis change, test independence/orthogonality and solve a small eigenproblem with justified checks. TMA 02 follows B03, sampling O3/O7 and stated earlier prerequisites; six separate hours.
Forward handover
Prepares M201/P201 normal-mode and later quantum representations. A101 core investigations do not assume concurrent eigenmethods.

B04 · Differential equations and numerical evolution

66 study hours, including 12 Python hours. Prior blocks: B01, B02, B03. Outcomes: M102-O4, M102-O5, M102-O7.

Purpose
Connect a rate law and initial conditions with an evolving model, analytically and numerically.
Scope
Formulate simple rate laws and distinguish equation, solution and initial condition. Separable and linear first-order equations with integrating factors; constant-coefficient homogeneous second-order equations and a bounded set of elementary forcing examples. Real/complex characteristic roots and oscillator interpretation. Euler stepping as the required introductory numerical method, step-size error, stability limitations and comparison with a known solution.
Boundaries
No general ODE existence theory, nonlinear dynamics survey, general coupled-system solver course, Laplace/Fourier transforms or PDEs. Improved Euler may replace an enrichment example within hours, not become an extra requirement. Physical assumptions are supplied or retrieved from P101.
Python
Implement transparent Euler steps, compare with an exact solution, halve step size and investigate a failure case such as an oscillator. Explain initial-condition handling, units and reproducibility rather than trusting a solver.
Assessment evidence
Solve and check equations by substitution and initial conditions; critique a numerical trajectory. TMA 03 follows B04, sampling O4/O5/O7 with independent executed code; six separate hours.
Forward handover
Supplies Stage 2 rate-model readiness. A101 may offer clearly timed optional model enrichment; late ODE teaching cannot become a hidden entry requirement.

B05 · Probability models and mathematical expectation

42 study hours, including 6 Python hours. Prior blocks: B02, B04. Outcomes: M102-O6, M102-O7.

Purpose
Provide an explicit mathematical probability foundation for later inference and statistical physics.
Scope
Sample spaces/events, addition and multiplication rules, conditional probability, independence and elementary Bayes reasoning in finite examples. Random variables, probability tables, expectation and variance; bounded Bernoulli/binomial examples. Continuous density versus point probability, normalisation and interval probability using single-variable integration; expectation/variance in simple integrable models.
Boundaries
Not a complete statistics course. No catalogue of distributions, inferential testing, confidence intervals, general fitting or central-limit-theorem proof. Normal models can be identified for later study without claiming their derivation. Keep the finite and continuous foundation substantial within the reserved hours.
Python
Enumerate a finite model exactly, compare with a reproducible seeded simulation and show why finite-sample frequencies differ from probabilities. Check a simple density normalisation analytically; simulation is not proof.
Assessment evidence
Embedded iCMA/formative written checks and simulation critique; final written examination explicitly assesses O6. No fourth TMA. Probability code is formative; independent executed-code evidence for O7 already comes from the three TMAs.
Forward handover
M201/X201/P201 must allocate deeper probability, distributions, uncertainty propagation and inference before use. A101 core work uses P101 descriptive uncertainty; any conditional/probabilistic extension must follow this block or be explicitly deferred.
