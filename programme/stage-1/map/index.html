02 / ENTRY

Readiness without a hidden bridge requirement.

Before Semester 1: manipulate fractions and signed quantities, rearrange elementary equations, use powers/logarithms and basic trigonometry, interpret graphs and units. Diagnose these skills without assuming them from qualifications. M100 or scoped refreshers can supply preparation. No calculus or prior Python proficiency is required for entry; P101 explicitly introduces the shared computing workflow.

Evidence-based retrieval can shorten repeated practice for a prepared learner; it does not automatically award a module’s credits. Module orientation is budgeted below. Additional remedial work is recorded separately when it exceeds those hours.

SEMESTER 1 / LU-M101

Mathematics for physics I

Mathematical language for quantitative physics

Entry: Stage 1 mathematical readiness; no calculus or Python mastery assumed. P101 is concurrent, not a prerequisite already completed.

Approved module-level exit outcomes
OutcomeExpected capability
M101-O1Explain limits and derivatives as local behaviour and rates; differentiate elementary functions and justify the method used.
M101-O2Interpret definite integration as accumulation and apply the fundamental theorem; calculate elementary integrals with units and checks.
M101-O3Use coordinate vectors, magnitudes, scalar products and elementary vector products to represent and resolve physical quantities.
M101-O4Use matrices as linear transformations and solve small linear systems, recognising when a solution is not unique.
M101-O5Use elementary approximations and numerical derivative/integral checks; distinguish a numerical check from a proof.
M101-O6Present a coherent mathematical argument and explain short Python calculations using the shared foundation introduced in P101.
First teaching and responsibility
First degree teaching of single-variable calculus, coordinate vector algebra and introductory matrix methods. Retrieve precalculus where needed without repeating the full bridge. P101 owns the physical interpretation of force, work and measurement.
Boundaries and later depth
M102 owns systematic complex-number methods, series, further linear algebra and introductory ODE solution methods. M201 owns full multivariable/vector calculus, transforms and PDE methods. Numerical checks do not replace analytical working.
Python and practical work
Apply scalar calculations, functions and plots after P101 foundation handover. Introduce finite-difference and quadrature checks as mathematical methods, including changes of step size.
Assessment role
TMAs combine handwritten/scanned reasoning, physical interpretation and short explained computational checks. iCMAs provide feedback within study hours. Proposed final written exam samples independent mathematical reasoning; format and marking remain to be designed.
Forward handover
M101-O1/O2 support later P101 rate/work applications; O3 supports force resolution; O1–O5 prepare M102; O2/O3/O6 support astronomical calculations and plots in A101.

30 internal credits / 300 hours. Proposed final assessment: Final written examination; detailed format and timing await module review. Existing module preview

SEMESTER 1 / LU-P101

Physics, space & scientific thinking

Physical reasoning, measurement and scientific computing

Entry: Stage 1 mathematical readiness and concurrent M101. Begin with algebraic/graphical problems; do not require calculus before its M101 teaching.

Approved module-level exit outcomes
OutcomeExpected capability
P101-O1Construct and test elementary motion/force models using diagrams, Newtonian reasoning, units and stated assumptions.
P101-O2Use momentum and energy balances in simple systems, distinguishing work, heat and conserved quantities.
P101-O3Relate simple oscillation, wave and light descriptions to observations; use frequency, wavelength and propagation relations.
P101-O4Explain introductory field/potential and simple DC-circuit models and use microscopic/atomic pictures with explicit limits; distinguish qualitative introduction from formal theory.
P101-O5Plan a small investigation, interpret repeated measurements and instrument resolution, distinguish random/systematic effects and justify reported precision and limitations.
P101-O6Write and explain small Python programs with variables, conditions, loops and functions; load small tables, use basic arrays, plot labelled data and save a clean reproducible notebook.
P101-O7Communicate a physical conclusion supported by calculations/data and traceable sources; explain simple numerical/model checks and disclose assistance.
First teaching and responsibility
First shared degree teaching of scientific Python and notebook practice, measurement conventions, elementary descriptive statistics and uncertainty reasoning. Mechanics and conservation provide the main quantitative spine; waves/light, simple circuits and microscopic pictures broaden physical context.
Boundaries and later depth
Not a compressed complete undergraduate physics survey. Relativistic mechanics, formal quantum/statistical mechanics, full electromagnetism and quantitative solid-state/nuclear/particle foundations require later core allocations. Introductory mentions do not close coverage gaps. P101 does not take over M101 calculus derivations.
Python and practical work
Explicitly teach the Python foundation to entrants who skipped M100; offer a demonstrated-skill retrieval route for prepared learners. Separate independent code from later assisted experiments. Use free local tools and small data; no paid AI or telescope required.
Assessment role
TMAs sample explained problem solving, measurement/data critique and independent code. Proposed EMA combines a bounded investigation with written conceptual/quantitative checks so a project cannot omit whole outcome areas. Embedded iCMAs support retrieval.
Forward handover
P101-O5/O6/O7 form the practical entry contract for A101 and the starting evidence strand for X201. O1–O4 prepare later core physics; they do not replace P201.

30 internal credits / 300 hours. Proposed final assessment: EMA; detailed format and timing await module review. Existing module preview

SEMESTER 2 / LU-M102

Mathematics for physics II

Mathematical structures, approximation and change

Entry: M101-O1–O6 and P101-O6 computing foundation. A101 is concurrent; neither module may require an unfinished result from the other to begin.

Approved module-level exit outcomes
OutcomeExpected capability
M102-O1Use complex arithmetic, polar/exponential representations and elementary identities to represent oscillatory quantities.
M102-O2Apply selected further integration techniques and series/Taylor approximations with explicit validity and truncation considerations.
M102-O3Solve small eigenvalue/eigenvector problems and use basis changes and orthogonality in finite-dimensional examples.
M102-O4Solve introductory separable and linear first-order ODEs and constant-coefficient second-order ODEs with initial conditions; check solutions by substitution.
M102-O5Use an elementary numerical ODE method, compare it with a known solution and investigate step-size error and limitations.
M102-O6Use probability rules, conditional probability, expectation and variance in elementary finite models and interpret a simple continuous density with single-variable integration.
M102-O7Present analytical and computational reasoning with explicit assumptions, error checks and appropriate notation.
First teaching and responsibility
Further mathematical development after M101; first systematic complex-number, series, eigenproblem and ODE treatment. Provide an explicit mathematical probability foundation supporting later statistical physics and inference.
Boundaries and later depth
Full multivariable/vector calculus, Fourier/PDE methods and general ODE theory remain M201. Inference, parameter estimation and extensive distribution theory require X201/M201/P201 coordination. Probability is a deliberate capacity reservation, not a claim that a complete statistics course fits here.
Python and practical work
Extend established functions/arrays into small numerical experiments; compare analytic and numerical results, vary a resolution parameter and explain failure cases. Do not repeat P101 Python induction.
Assessment role
TMAs sample analytical derivations, approximation, probability reasoning and independent numerical checks. Embedded iCMAs test prerequisites and feedback. Proposed final written exam requires coverage across core outcomes, with practical code assessed separately in TMAs.
Forward handover
O1–O5/O7 prepare mathematical work in M201/P201; O6 supports later statistical reasoning. A101 core activities must remain viable with Semester 1 prerequisites; any M102-linked enrichment is explicitly labelled and timed.

30 internal credits / 300 hours. Proposed final assessment: Final written examination; detailed format and timing await module review. Existing module preview

SEMESTER 2 / LU-A101

Exploring astronomy through computation

Astronomical evidence and reproducible investigation

Entry: M101 calculus/vector/numerical foundations and P101 light, measurement, Python and reporting outcomes. Exact outcome-to-unit links await its block/unit review; no compulsory M100 prerequisite.

Approved module-level exit outcomes
OutcomeExpected capability
A1Place observations in astronomical context while distinguishing apparent position, physical scale and inference.
A2Explain how detector response, sampling, background and selection affect images, spectra and light curves.
A3Use introductory flux, spectral-shift, transit, parallax and magnitude models with units, assumptions and plausibility checks.
A4Form a bounded astronomical question, analyse a small documented dataset and compare evidence with model predictions.
A5Own and validate the computation: explain code, preserve provenance, rerun cleanly and audit any assistance.
A6Communicate results with labelled figures, uncertainty/limitations and a defensible account of what the evidence establishes.
First teaching and responsibility
Application and extension of Semester 1 skills to observational astronomy: calibrated data, spectra, time series and distance/brightness inference. Retain existing A1–A6 identifiers; these are concise module-level restatements, not approval of revised units.
Boundaries and later depth
No repeat of full Python or arithmetic foundations. No full stellar-structure, galactic-dynamics or cosmology derivation course; later specialist options retain those roles. Revised ten-unit version0.3 remains proposed within approved blocks, with exact Semester 1 prerequisites and reduced introductory duplication.
Python and practical work
Deeper notebook investigations with small arrays/tables, calibrated plots, units, checks against subsets/limiting cases and a documented environment. Free datasets and tools need verification before teaching release. Optional observing equipment may enrich a later activity; required outcomes retain a free route.
Assessment role
Retain the existing provisional three-TMA and one-EMA model for review: evidence/provenance, calibration, signals/distance, then a reproducible investigation and defence with cross-outcome checks. iCMAs remain embedded.
Forward handover
Prepare X201 investigation design/validation and later astronomy/project choices. An introductory astronomy result is not evidence that advanced stellar/cosmological prerequisites have been met.

03 / WORKLOAD

A budget, not a promise of unmeasured capacity.

Proposed envelope for each 30-credit module
WorkHours
Unit study and embedded practice/iCMAs240
Orientation and module-specific workflow6
TMAs (provisional three × six hours)18
Final assessment, including required preparation/submission tasks18
Revision12
Feedback and follow-up6

240 + 6 + 18 + 18 + 12 + 6 = 300 hours per module; 600 per semester pair; 1,200 for Stage 1. This is an original provisional envelope, retaining A101’s existing draft total, not a verified OU week-by-week schedule. Explanation, worked examples, required exercises, Python and embedded iCMAs belong within the 240 study hours, not on top. Extra backup practice is optional unless explicitly budgeted as required.

The final-assessment allowance includes the task, instructions and required preparation/submission steps, including scan checks for handwritten work. The separate revision allowance covers broader consolidation, not duplicate task preparation. Module reviews must test whether each assessment fits. The common envelope may be redistributed within 300 hours with an explicit decision; it does not assert equal practical intensity.

P101 owns shared computing induction; M101’s orientation covers its own mathematical workflow. A101 orientation covers astronomy-specific data conventions rather than another generic Python installation course. Exact computing hours and topic budgets will be set in module reviews. Dates, semester lengths, final exam arrangements and assessment identifiers are not created here.

06 / REVIEW DECISIONS

What this proposal settles—and what it does not.

Approved at module-map level: the outcome split, P101’s shared computing/measurement responsibility, M102’s explicit probability foundation, the provisional workload envelope, and written finals for the two mathematics modules versus EMAs for P101/A101.

Capacity risks remain: P101 combines foundational physics and a first computing strand; M102 combines substantial mathematical methods and probability. Their detailed blocks/units must demonstrate that this fits. If it does not, reallocate scope visibly within the degree before approval; do not silently increase hours or remove required foundations. A101’s existing unit scopes need review for depth after Semester 1; this map does not approve them unchanged.

Open degree obligations: analytical mechanics, solid-state and nuclear/particle foundations, thermal/statistical and quantum depth, advanced inference and physical apparatus/collaboration remain in the coverage register. Introductory references in P101 or optional specialist study do not close these gaps.

Construction disposition: parent/peer map approved; M101 block proposal is now available for review. This does not approve its blocks or units. Blueprint the complete M101 block structure before its units. Keep each subsequent module review separate. Full Stage 1 units and the stronger M100 lesson-production gate remain outstanding. Free tools/data and representative workloads require validation during detailed design.
