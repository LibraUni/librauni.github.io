STAGE 1 / SEMESTER 2 / LU-A101

Exploring astronomy through computation

Learn how astronomical observations become evidence: investigate images, spectra, changing brightness and stellar catalogues through reproducible, critically checked computation.

30 internal credits · 300 notional hours · Alongside M102 · Blocks and revised units approved

A101 belongs in Semester 2 after M101 and P101. The earlier first-module pairing, beginner readiness gates and 31-week timetable are withdrawn. The approved revised unit blueprint below replaces the earlier individual hour allocations; unit IDs are preserved.

01 / POSITION

Apply the first semester’s foundations.

Semester 2 alongside M102. Required work builds on M101 and P101: unit-specific links below identify mathematical, measurement, light and computing readiness. No required activity depends on unfinished M102 material. M100 remains optional preparation outside the degree.

M102 · Mathematics for physics II is the concurrent mathematical partner. A101 should apply established mathematics and physics, teach new astronomical methods explicitly, and avoid repeating M100 preparation. Its assessment dates will be coordinated with M102.

Semester timing: two sequential semesters, with two concurrent 30-credit modules in each. Each semester therefore contains 600 notional hours. Calendar weeks, holidays, start dates and assessment deadlines are not yet set; both modules in a pair will use a coordinated semester timetable. The superseded 30/31-week M100–A101 pairing is withdrawn. “Semester” does not silently reduce the required hours.

B01 · Astronomical questions, scales and records

30 hours, including 8 Python hours. Prior blocks: Semester 1 readiness. Outcomes: A1, A2, A5, A6.

Purpose
Translate an astronomical question into a documented, checkable data task.
Scope
Sky views versus spatial structure, angular versus physical scale and look-back interpretation; catalogue identifiers, units, missing values, metadata and provenance. Audit records and distinguish observations from inferences. Historical changes of sky models support evidence reasoning.
Boundaries
Apply Semester 1 arithmetic, graphs and Python; do not repeat their foundations or claim full spherical astronomy. No telescope required. Detailed cosmological distance relations remain later.
Python
Use existing functions/arrays to audit a small table, check a conversion independently and preserve a reproducible source record. Introduce astronomy-specific conventions explicitly.
Investigation and deliverable
A compact catalogue/evidence audit with a clean notebook and short claims-versus-evidence explanation. Use a small documented subset, with synthetic examples clearly labelled.
Evidence
TMA 01 after B01: provenance critique, scale reasoning and independently written numerical checks; six separate hours.
Prerequisites and handover
P101 U01–U03 computing/measurement and M101 vector/graph readiness are prerequisites. B01 establishes the records and conventions used by later investigations.

B02 · From light and images to calibrated measurements

78 hours, including 24 Python hours. Prior blocks: B01. Outcomes: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A6.

Purpose
Understand what a detector records and turn counts into a defensible measurement.
Scope
Retrieve P101 light/photon ideas, then develop passbands, false colour, spectral information and observational limits. Read sky coordinates/epoch and image metadata; pixel scale, background, exposure, saturation and resolution. Elementary aperture sums and supplied calibration/exposure factors, with quality flags and sensitivity to analysis choices.
Boundaries
No repeat wavelength/graph foundations, full coordinate transformations, detector engineering, advanced error propagation or automated survey-scale pipeline. Preserve physical meaning behind library calls.
Python
Inspect small image arrays and FITS metadata with explained Astropy helpers; calculate a source/background estimate, compare a hand subset and test how an aperture or background choice changes results.
Investigation and deliverable
A documented image-calibration notebook, observation/measurement plan and limitations argument. A CSV/pixel-table fallback supports access without changing the measurement outcomes.
Evidence
TMA 02 after B02 retains calibration notebook plus written interpretation and independent checks; six separate hours.
Prerequisites and handover
P101 U08–U13 light/measurement and M101 U08–U11 accumulation/numerical checks provide entry. Supplies calibrated-signal reasoning for B03 and B04.

B03 · Spectra and changing light

56 hours, including 22 Python hours. Prior blocks: B02. Outcomes: A2, A3, A4, A5, A6.

Purpose
Compare astronomical models with spectral and time-dependent evidence.
Scope
Wavelength calibration, competing line identifications and small-shift Doppler relation with explicit limits. Time stamps, cadence, gaps and flags; repeated-event period estimates, normalised brightness and a simple transit-depth/radius-ratio model. Consider alternative explanations and sensitivity to selection.
Boundaries
No relativistic-redshift derivation, automatic planet confirmation, advanced periodogram theory, Bayesian fitting or late-M102 ODE requirement. A redshift is not automatically a local velocity.
Python
Use functions, arrays, units and plots to measure shifts and inspect a curated light curve; test interval/flag/background choices and compare with a hand case or known injected signal. Inspect and verify any optional assistance after independent work.
Investigation and deliverable
Two bounded investigations: spectral-shift interpretation and variability/transit evidence. Deliver reproducible figures, model checks, provenance and a plausible confounder.
Evidence
Formative spectral/light-curve checks. Retain TMA 03 after U08 in B04 so it samples signals plus parallax; six separate hours, no additional TMA here.
Prerequisites and handover
P101 waves/light and M101 established maths suffice. M102 phase/approximation can enrich only after readiness; required work does not depend on it.

B04 · Distances, stellar populations and investigation

76 hours, including 24 Python hours. Prior blocks: B03. Outcomes: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A6.

Purpose
Use catalogue evidence to compare populations and defend a bounded astronomical conclusion.
Scope
Parallax geometry and its limitations; distinguish proper motion and parallax, reject unjustified inverse-distance estimates. Flux/luminosity, magnitudes, distance modulus and colour–magnitude diagrams; selection, extinction, binaries and completeness. Qualitative stellar evolution provides context without claiming a structure derivation. Formative synthesis with one familiar data family.
Boundaries
No Bayesian distance inference, stellar-structure/age derivation or full cosmology course. A colour–magnitude diagram is not automatically a theoretical HR diagram. M102 late probability/ODE/eigenmethods are not compulsory prerequisites.
Python
Use a release-pinned small catalogue subset, document quality cuts and compare population plots; hand-check a magnitude/distance calculation and test selection sensitivity. Clean rerun and assistance audit, including critique of a bounded suggested refactor.
Investigation and deliverable
A distance/population notebook and a formative investigation plan with tutor critique. Actual EMA work is separately budgeted. Local data copies preserve a free route if archive access changes.
Evidence
TMA 03 after U08 covers signals/distance, not U09 population material. EMA after U10 retains investigation10h + written/handwritten checks5h + preparation/tutor defence3h =18h; includes cross-outcome checks. No final exam.
Prerequisites and handover
Prepares X201 and later astronomy options with data provenance, reproducibility and qualified inference. Optional affordable observing equipment may enrich future work but never becomes a required purchase or apparatus-skill claim.

U01 · Reading the sky as evidence

14 hours, including 3 Python hours · Semester 1 readiness.

Required Semester 1 units: P101 U01, P101 U02, P101 U03.

Scope
Objects and apparent patterns; Solar System, stars and galaxies; observation versus explanation; apparent daily motion and a qualitative Earth–sky model; images as processed measurements. Introduce observation logs, provenance and claims supported by evidence.
Investigation
Compare a small supplied set of sky views and annotated observations; construct an evidence log distinguishing observation, interpretation and unanswered questions.
Computing
Apply established notebook skills to a small source/provenance comparison; save reproducible links and metadata. No Python installation or beginner tutorial.
Evidence
Annotated evidence log and explanation of why a photograph is not a map of physical distances.
Validation and deliverable
Classify each claim as observation or interpretation; check that a sky image does not determine physical depth. Deliver a brief annotated evidence log.
Boundary
No spherical trigonometry, telescope ownership or physical observing requirement. Historical changes in sky models support evidence reasoning, not a survey of all astronomy.
Forward handover
Provides observation/inference conventions for U02–U10; TMA01 samples A1/A2/A6.

U02 · Scales, signals and astronomical records

16 hours, including 5 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U01.

Required Semester 1 units: M101 U01, P101 U03.

Scope
Catalogue identifiers, quantities, units, missing values, epochs and provenance. Relate angular and physical scale with diagrams, explicit small-angle assumptions and checked conversions; distinguish source, instrument and interpretation.
Investigation
Audit a small table of observations and compare numerical scales using a provided unit legend; flag unsupported comparisons and trace each value to a source.
Computing
Audit identifiers, units and missing values using readable Python; independently check selected conversions and numerical scales.
Evidence
A reproducible arithmetic check and a short data-provenance audit.
Validation and deliverable
Check units and one conversion by hand, trace a record to its source and test missing/invalid entries. Deliver a catalogue audit and reproducible notebook.
Boundary
Introduce astronomical data conventions without repeating elementary arithmetic or Python foundations. Magnitudes are developed with their physical interpretation in U09.
Forward handover
Provides provenance conventions for all investigations; iCMA41 is included here (1h). TMA01 follows U02.

U03 · Light, colour and the information we receive

22 hours, including 6 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U02.

Required Semester 1 units: P101 U10, P101 U13.

Scope
Retrieve P101 wavelength/frequency and photon-energy relations, then develop astronomical passbands, false colour, continuum versus lines, absorption/emission interpretation and observational limits. Explain why colour alone does not uniquely determine temperature.
Investigation
Compare a few supplied multi-band images and spectra; calculate selected wavelengths/frequencies and separate measured colour from an inferred temperature.
Computing
Compare wavelength and frequency representations with scalar and array calculations; explain the relation to passbands and check units.
Evidence
A wavelength calculation, annotated spectrum and justified comparison across passbands.
Validation and deliverable
Check a wavelength/frequency/photon-energy case against P101 reasoning; compare passband labels before interpreting colours. Deliver annotated multi-band/spectral figures.
Boundary
No Planck-law derivation, quantum transitions calculation or black-body fitting. P101 and later thermal/quantum study own the deeper explanation.
Forward handover
Provides passband/spectral interpretation for imaging U04/U05 and spectra U06; TMA02 includes physical interpretation.

U04 · Positions, images and observing choices

24 hours, including 7 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U03.

Required Semester 1 units: M101 U01, M101 U02, P101 U02, P101 U10.

Scope
Reading right ascension and declination as labelled coordinates; epoch and field of view; pixels, image scale, background, saturation and resolution; exposure choices and selection bias. Distinguish angular separation from physical distance.
Investigation
Compare small image cutouts with provided scale bars and metadata; plan an observation to answer a question and explain which instrument limitations matter.
Computing
Inspect small arrays, FITS headers and labelled coordinate/epoch metadata with explained Astropy helpers. Compare display choices and physical pixel scale; use a supplied pixel-table fallback for access.
Evidence
A measurement plan, an image-scale calculation and a critique of a misleading display.
Validation and deliverable
Check pixel-to-angle scale manually; vary display stretch and explain why the measured quantity must not change with cosmetic choices. Deliver image metadata audit and observation plan.
Boundary
No coordinate transformations, spherical geometry or hands-on instrument certification. Observatory location, access and environmental effects are discussed within the unit hours.
Forward handover
Provides measurement choices for aperture work U05; later practical modules deepen instrument handling.

U05 · From detector counts to a defensible graph

32 hours, including 11 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U04.

Required Semester 1 units: M101 U08, M101 U11, P101 U02, P101 U03.

Scope
Source/background counts, exposure normalisation and supplied calibration factors. Develop an elementary aperture-sum model with assumptions; compare quality flags, repeated values and sensitivity to chosen regions. Distinguish descriptive uncertainty, a supplied measurement range and a formally propagated error.
Investigation
Measure a source from a tiny image cutout or equivalent pixel table, change the background region, and explain the effect on the reported signal.
Computing
NumPy arrays, supplied CSV files and labelled Matplotlib plots; compare an array sum with a hand calculation on a small subset. Inspect a curated FITS header through provided support, without needing to write a FITS reader.
Evidence
A calibration notebook with independent arithmetic checks, figures and a limitations paragraph.
Validation and deliverable
Compare a tiny hand-summed aperture/background subset with code; vary the background region and report sensitivity separately from a claimed statistical error bar. Deliver calibration notebook and limitations.
Boundary
No error propagation, least-squares theory or automated source-extraction pipeline. The data are small enough for local study; raw full survey processing is unnecessary.
Forward handover
Provides calibrated-signal reasoning for U06/U07 and catalogue selection U09; iCMA42 included here (1h), then TMA02.

U06 · Spectra as measurements of change

28 hours, including 10 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U05.

Required Semester 1 units: P101 U10, P101 U13, M101 U07.

Scope
Line identification from supplied references; wavelength calibration; observed versus reference wavelength; the small-shift Doppler approximation with explicit assumptions. Compare line locations and test how sampling or a mistaken line identity changes the answer.
Investigation
Measure shifts in curated spectra, compare two plausible identifications and explain what extra evidence would distinguish them.
Computing
Use functions and selected Astropy units to check a spectral-shift measurement, after a hand-worked case. Compare plausible line identities and document wavelength frame/calibration assumptions; no unexplained automated fitting.
Evidence
An explained spectral-shift notebook with a limiting-case check and a defended interpretation.
Validation and deliverable
Check zero shift and a hand-worked line; compare two identifications and wavelength sampling effects. Deliver spectral-shift notebook with a qualified physical interpretation.
Boundary
No relativistic derivation, stellar-atmosphere modelling or claim that every redshift is a local Doppler velocity. Galactic dynamics and cosmology remain later work.
Forward handover
Prepares cross-evidence reasoning in U07 and TMA03 after U08. M102 phase enrichment is optional after M102 U02 only.

U07 · Changing light: variability and transits

28 hours, including 12 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U06.

Required Semester 1 units: P101 U08, M101 U07.

Scope
Time stamps, cadence, gaps, repeated features and normalised brightness. Infer a period from identifiable repeated events; use a supplied box-transit model and radius-ratio relation with stated idealisations. Compare a transit candidate with alternative explanations.
Investigation
Investigate a small curated TESS light curve, inspect the effect of a gap or flagged point and test whether the observations support the proposed interpretation.
Computing
Plot, mask and compare a small light curve with a transparent box model. Check cadence, period and depth by hand and show how flags/gaps change the claim. Critique optional AI suggestions only after independent work, recording changes and checks.
Evidence
A light-curve argument with a reproducible plot, model assumptions and at least one plausible confounder.
Validation and deliverable
Check a repeated-event interval and depth manually; change a quality mask and inject a known simple model into a labelled synthetic example. Deliver a candidate-versus-confounder argument, not a discovery claim.
Boundary
Periodogram theory, advanced orbital integration and a claim to have confirmed a new planet are outside this introductory investigation.
Forward handover
Provides time-series evidence for TMA03 and the later investigation. M102 numerical-orbit/ODE work is not required; no default enrichment adds hours.

U08 · Distance from geometry

24 hours, including 7 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U07.

Required Semester 1 units: M101 U01, M101 U07, P101 U02.

Scope
Parallax geometry, arcseconds, parsecs and inverse distance; proper motion versus parallax. Explain when inverse-parallax estimates are useful and why noisy, zero or negative measurements cannot be blindly inverted.
Investigation
Compare a geometry simulation with a small high-quality Gaia subset; test scale and limiting behaviour, then inspect deliberately unsuitable records.
Computing
Use radians and triangle reasoning before a supplied catalogue conversion; annotate rejection criteria. Check library conversions against a hand-worked case.
Evidence
A labelled parallax diagram, distance estimate and explanation of selection effects.
Validation and deliverable
Check geometric scale and an inverse-parallax example; inspect unsuitable negative/low-quality records and explain why naive inversion fails. Deliver a diagram, selected estimates and selection rationale.
Boundary
No Bayesian distance inference, full astrometric solution or three-dimensional orbital reconstruction. Logarithmic distance modulus waits until U09.
Forward handover
Provides distance estimates for U09; TMA03 follows U08 and does not assess U09 population material.

U09 · Stellar populations: patterns and explanations

32 hours, including 10 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U08.

Required Semester 1 units: P101 U10, M101 U04, M101 U07.

Scope
Flux versus luminosity, inverse-square scaling, apparent and absolute magnitude, distance modulus and colour–magnitude diagrams. Teach the supplied magnitude definitions. Compare selected nearby/cluster samples; introduce extinction, binaries and completeness as confounders. Relate observed populations to qualitative stellar evolution.
Investigation
Construct a colour–magnitude diagram from curated Gaia data, vary a documented quality cut and compare the result with a supplied reference population.
Computing
Use established logarithms and arrays to build a colour–magnitude diagram, record source-release identifiers and quality cuts, and inspect a proposed code change against scientific requirements. Supplied background ranges support sensitivity checks without hidden formal inference.
Evidence
A population-comparison notebook with reproducible selection, checks and qualified conclusions.
Validation and deliverable
Check a magnitude difference and distance modulus by hand; vary a documented quality cut and distinguish selection effects from population physics. Deliver reproducible colour–magnitude comparison.
Boundary
A colour–magnitude diagram is not automatically a theoretical HR diagram. No independent derivation of stellar structure, ages or detailed evolutionary tracks; those belong to A301.
Forward handover
Provides a population-data family for U10/EMA; iCMA43 included here (1h). Statistical inference beyond descriptive comparisons belongs later.

U10 · A small investigation with a defensible conclusion

20 hours, including 7 Python hours · Earlier A101 units: U09.

Required Semester 1 units: M101 U12, M101 U13, P101 U13.

Scope
Synthesis of existing tools: formulate a bounded question, compare observations with a supplied model, describe variation and limitations, and justify a reproducible workflow. Place the result in the wider astronomical picture, distinguishing established evidence from open questions.
Investigation
Rehearse a small investigation using one previously studied data family and peer-style tutor critique. Discuss how observational constraints relate to questions about stars, planetary systems and galaxies without adding new technical cosmology.
Computing
Reproduce a bounded investigation from clean input to labelled figures; record source release, environment, assumptions and assistance. A tutor-supplied refactor suggestion can be audited without access to an AI service; any actual agent use stays optional and controlled.
Evidence
Formative investigation plan and method defence, distinct from the separately budgeted EMA.
Validation and deliverable
Rerun from a clean kernel; trace all data and dependencies; defend a model assumption and an alternative explanation. Deliver formative plan, notebook rehearsal and tutor critique, not the submitted EMA.
Boundary
Use the earlier A101 investigations and Semester 1 preparation. This formative synthesis is distinct from the separately budgeted EMA.
Forward handover
Prepares X201 and later astronomy/project work. EMA follows, with its own 18-hour budget. A bounded refactor critique is possible; no paid agent or unverified autonomous analysis is required.
