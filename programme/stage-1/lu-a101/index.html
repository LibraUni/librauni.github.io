STAGE 1 / LU-A101 / PREVIEW 0.1

Exploring astronomy through computation

Learn how astronomical observations become evidence: investigate images, spectra, changing brightness and stellar catalogues through reproducible, critically checked computation.

24 September 2026 · 30 internal credits · 300 notional hours · Unit outline proposed for review

Designed as a possible first-module pairing with M101. This is a complete unit-level proposal, not available lessons or an enrolment. The 31-study-week timetable is provisional; exact OU calendar fidelity remains open.

01 / ROLE & READINESS

Astronomy alongside your foundations.

No prior astronomy, calculus or completed P101 is required for this paired route. Begin with ordinary arithmetic and reading simple tables. Study M101 concurrently, or demonstrate the listed mathematics and Python readiness before each dependent unit. Running supplied code does not establish programming mastery.

The module teaches the minimum astronomical context needed for its observations: how light is measured, what a spectrum records, and what a supplied model assumes. It does not assume P101 or M102 as completed modules. P101 later develops the physical laws systematically; M102/M103 supply calculus and further mathematics. Their content is not counted as covered merely because an astronomical example uses a supplied relation.

The endpoint is a small, independently explained astronomical investigation. It prepares questions and data habits for later astrophysics, not advanced stellar structure, orbital dynamics or cosmology.

U01 · WEEKS 1–2 · 18 HOURS

Reading the sky as evidence

Before this unit: Module entry readiness.

Scope
Objects and apparent patterns; Solar System, stars and galaxies; observation versus explanation; apparent daily motion and a qualitative Earth–sky model; images as processed measurements. Introduce observation logs, provenance and claims supported by evidence.
Practical investigation
Compare a small supplied set of sky views and annotated observations; construct an evidence log distinguishing observation, interpretation and unanswered questions.
Computing progression
Orientation to the supplied notebook and file structure only. No independent Python task before M101 U01 is complete; image interpretation and written reasoning carry the early investigation.
Evidence
Annotated evidence log and explanation of why a photograph is not a map of physical distances.
Boundary
No spherical trigonometry, telescope ownership or physical observing requirement. Historical changes in sky models support evidence reasoning, not a survey of all astronomy.

Outcomes A1, A2, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U02 · WEEKS 3–4 · 18 HOURS

Scales, signals and astronomical records

Before this unit: A101 U01. M101 preparation: U01, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
How a catalogue record describes an observation: identifiers, quantities, units, missing information and provenance. Contrast an angular view with physical size using diagrams and supplied conversion tables. Separate signal, instrument and interpretation.
Practical investigation
Audit a small table of observations and compare numerical scales using a provided unit legend; flag unsupported comparisons and trace each value to a source.
Computing progression
Learner-written arithmetic cells and named values; predict outputs first. Scientific notation and compound-unit calculations begin in U03 after M101 U02, not here.
Evidence
A reproducible arithmetic check and a short data-provenance audit.
Boundary
No early logarithmic magnitude calculation or array/CSV programming. This is scientific data literacy, not a repeat of arithmetic instruction.

Outcomes A1, A2, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U03 · WEEKS 5–7 · 26 HOURS

Light, colour and the information we receive

Before this unit: A101 U02. M101 preparation: U02, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Wavelength, frequency and the electromagnetic spectrum; photons as a qualitative model; passbands and false colour; continuum versus lines; what absorption and emission can indicate. Explain the supplied c = wavelength × frequency relation and its scope without deriving electromagnetism.
Practical investigation
Compare a few supplied multi-band images and spectra; calculate selected wavelengths/frequencies and separate measured colour from an inferred temperature.
Computing progression
Scalar calculations with variables and math functions; supplied plots remain figures for interpretation. No array programming required.
Evidence
A wavelength calculation, annotated spectrum and justified comparison across passbands.
Boundary
No Planck-law derivation, quantum transitions calculation or black-body fitting. P101 and later thermal/quantum study own the deeper explanation.

Outcomes A1, A2, A3, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U04 · WEEKS 8–11 · 28 HOURS

Positions, images and observing choices

Before this unit: A101 U03. M101 preparation: U03, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Reading right ascension and declination as labelled coordinates; epoch and field of view; pixels, image scale, background, saturation and resolution; exposure choices and selection bias. Distinguish angular separation from physical distance.
Practical investigation
Compare small image cutouts with provided scale bars and metadata; plan an observation to answer a question and explain which instrument limitations matter.
Computing progression
Short scalar formulas and simple condition checks on image metadata. Inspect pre-rendered pixel grids; full image-array manipulation is not required before M101 U05.
Evidence
A measurement plan, an image-scale calculation and a critique of a misleading display.
Boundary
No coordinate transformations, spherical geometry or hands-on instrument certification. Observatory location, access and environmental effects are discussed within the unit hours.

Outcomes A1, A2, A4, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U05 · WEEKS 12–14 · 28 HOURS

From detector counts to a defensible graph

Before this unit: A101 U04. M101 preparation: U04, U05, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Small tables and image arrays; source and background counts; supplied calibration factors; exposure normalisation; uncertainty as a stated range and quality flags. Teach an elementary aperture-sum model and its assumptions.
Practical investigation
Measure a source from a tiny image cutout or equivalent pixel table, change the background region, and explain the effect on the reported signal.
Computing progression
NumPy arrays, supplied CSV files and labelled Matplotlib plots; compare an array sum with a hand calculation on a small subset. Inspect a curated FITS header through provided support, without needing to write a FITS reader.
Evidence
A calibration notebook with independent arithmetic checks, figures and a limitations paragraph.
Boundary
No error propagation, least-squares theory or automated source-extraction pipeline. The data are small enough for local study; raw full survey processing is unnecessary.

Outcomes A2, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U06 · WEEKS 15–18 · 30 HOURS

Spectra as measurements of change

Before this unit: A101 U05. M101 preparation: U06, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Line identification from supplied references; wavelength calibration; observed versus reference wavelength; the small-shift Doppler approximation with explicit assumptions. Compare line locations and test how sampling or a mistaken line identity changes the answer.
Practical investigation
Measure shifts in curated spectra, compare two plausible identifications and explain what extra evidence would distinguish them.
Computing progression
Functions for scalar or array calculations, simple tests, plots and supplied measurement ranges. Introduce selected Astropy units only as an explicit checking aid after hand calculations.
Evidence
An explained spectral-shift notebook with a limiting-case check and a defended interpretation.
Boundary
No relativistic derivation, stellar-atmosphere modelling or claim that every redshift is a local Doppler velocity. Galactic dynamics and cosmology remain later work.

Outcomes A2, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U07 · WEEKS 19–20 · 20 HOURS

Changing light: variability and transits

Before this unit: A101 U06. M101 preparation: U06, U07, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Time stamps, cadence, gaps, repeated features and normalised brightness. Infer a period from identifiable repeated events; use a supplied box-transit model and radius-ratio relation with stated idealisations. Compare a transit candidate with alternative explanations.
Practical investigation
Investigate a small curated TESS light curve, inspect the effect of a gap or flagged point and test whether the observations support the proposed interpretation.
Computing progression
Plot and modify a simple box model using functions and conditionals; check depth and period against selected data by hand. AI suggestions may be reviewed after an independent attempt, with changes and checks recorded.
Evidence
A light-curve argument with a reproducible plot, model assumptions and at least one plausible confounder.
Boundary
No trigonometric fitting before M101 U08, periodogram theory, orbital integration or claim to have confirmed a new planet.

Outcomes A2, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U08 · WEEKS 21–22 · 22 HOURS

Distance from geometry

Before this unit: A101 U07. M101 preparation: U08, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Parallax geometry, arcseconds, parsecs and inverse distance; proper motion versus parallax. Explain when inverse-parallax estimates are useful and why noisy, zero or negative measurements cannot be blindly inverted.
Practical investigation
Compare a geometry simulation with a small high-quality Gaia subset; test scale and limiting behaviour, then inspect deliberately unsuitable records.
Computing progression
Use radians and triangle reasoning before a supplied catalogue conversion; annotate rejection criteria. Check library conversions against a hand-worked case.
Evidence
A labelled parallax diagram, distance estimate and explanation of selection effects.
Boundary
No Bayesian distance inference, full astrometric solution or three-dimensional orbital reconstruction. Logarithmic distance modulus waits until U09.

Outcomes A1, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U09 · WEEKS 23–26 · 30 HOURS

Stellar populations: patterns and explanations

Before this unit: A101 U08. M101 preparation: U09, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Flux versus luminosity, inverse-square scaling, apparent and absolute magnitude, distance modulus and colour–magnitude diagrams. Teach the supplied magnitude definitions. Compare selected nearby/cluster samples; introduce extinction, binaries and completeness as confounders. Relate observed populations to qualitative stellar evolution.
Practical investigation
Construct a colour–magnitude diagram from curated Gaia data, vary a documented quality cut and compare the result with a supplied reference population.
Computing progression
Logarithms, clean catalogue tables and labelled plots; test selected magnitude differences by hand. Review a proposed code change against scientific and data-selection requirements.
Evidence
A population-comparison notebook with reproducible selection, checks and qualified conclusions.
Boundary
A colour–magnitude diagram is not automatically a theoretical HR diagram. No independent derivation of stellar structure, ages or detailed evolutionary tracks; those belong to A301.

Outcomes A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

U10 · WEEKS 27–28 · 20 HOURS

A small investigation with a defensible conclusion

Before this unit: A101 U09. M101 preparation: U11, or demonstrated equivalent readiness.

Scope
Synthesis of existing tools: formulate a bounded question, compare observations with a supplied model, describe variation and limitations, and justify a reproducible workflow. Place the result in the wider astronomical picture, distinguishing established evidence from open questions.
Practical investigation
Rehearse a small investigation using one previously studied data family and peer-style tutor critique. Discuss how observational constraints relate to questions about stars, planetary systems and galaxies without adding new technical cosmology.
Computing progression
Clean rerun, source and dependency record, labelled figures and an assistance log. A bounded agent may suggest a refactor; the learner must inspect and verify it. No paid agent is required.
Evidence
Formative investigation plan and method defence, distinct from the separately budgeted EMA.
Boundary
Use M101 U11 before this unit. M101 U12 is concurrent supporting practice, not an entry prerequisite; the assessed EMA follows both modules’ integration work.

Outcomes A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A6. Original lessons, diagrams, questions and selected data will be prepared after design review.

06 / PRACTICAL EXPERIENCE

Small data, substantial reasoning.

Planned tools are local Python notebooks, NumPy and Matplotlib, followed by selected Astropy unit/table/FITS helpers. No paid software, AI account, GPU or telescope is required. Use small local datasets and provide a documented offline path; live archive access is an extension of the reproducible workflow, not a fragile prerequisite for every session.

Gaia catalogue subsets support positions, parallax and population diagrams; MAST/TESS light curves support variability. Image and spectral cutouts will be selected and rights-checked before lesson release. Each released dataset needs its source, product/release, fields, units, filters, acknowledgements, retrieval/query recipe and limitations. Derived teaching files must retain provenance and third-party terms; public access alone is not permission to relicense them.

Simulations will expose assumptions: change one quantity, predict an effect, measure it, compare with the model and explain discrepancies. Planned practical evidence includes a source/background comparison, spectral-shift check, transit-candidate critique, parallax selection audit and population diagram. No discovery claim, instrumental competence or advanced uncertainty analysis is implied.

Before launch, test a pinned compatible environment and representative datasets on an ordinary laptop. Exact dataset selections, download sizes and setup instructions are not yet released. AI or agents may help inspect or refactor a bounded piece of work after foundations; their changes must be reviewed and independently tested.

07 / DESIGN REVIEW

A first-module design, not an advanced-course shortcut.

Proposed for review: ten units, 300 hours, three TMAs, three iCMAs and one EMA. The mathematics/Python pairing is checked against M101’s present baseline; both remain closed to enrolment until materials and the presentation are ready. No start date has been set.

Benchmark boundary: MIT’s astronomy syllabus expects mechanics and calculus. Imperial’s advanced astrophysics scope is a later-depth reference. A101 concentrates on introductory observational evidence; later modules still need orbital dynamics, stellar structure/evolution, compact objects, galactic dynamics and cosmology. P101 must teach general physics rather than repeat these archive investigations. See the coverage register for the wider unresolved allocations.

Timing limitation: the OU S284 page identifies an assessment in study week 31. We use 31 study weeks as a provisional horizon, not as proof of its full calendar length or weekly workload. A101’s units and EMA differ from S284. Exact OU-reference reconciliation remains required before final timetable approval, rather than claiming that the paired design reproduces an OU presentation.

Release checks: review unit density and practical allocations, obtain a fuller reference calendar, verify data/tool access and permissions, pilot representative activities, finish marking/submission arrangements, and recheck cross-module prerequisites after any M101 change. These are design conditions, not tasks already completed.

  1. OU timing and scope

    Accessed 24 September 2026. S284 has a later-entry astronomy scope and identifies an assessment in study week 31. This anchors a provisional 31-study-week planning horizon only; its full calendar and weekly hours have not been reproduced. A101 uses original units and an EMA, not S284’s examination scheme.

  2. MIT astronomy benchmark

    Historical 2006 syllabus, checked 24 September 2026. It expects mechanics and calculus. Compare observational themes here; defer orbital dynamics, stellar structure and cosmology derivations to later modules. No equivalence claim.

  3. Imperial astrophysics benchmark

    Historical 2023–24 specification available through official indexed text, not a full direct-PDF review. Compact objects, interstellar matter and galactic dynamics are later-depth checks; A101 does not cover this entire module.

  4. Imperial current elective listing

    Official indexed 2026–27 listing checked 24 September 2026. Confirms advanced astrophysics and computational options; does not provide a complete outcome-level audit.

  5. Gaia archive

    Public astrometry and photometry support U08–U09. Select a documented, small release-pinned subset before teaching; record query, fields, filters, acknowledgements and known limitations.

  6. TESS public data downloads

    MAST provides light-curve products. Use small curated subsets for U07; inspect quality flags and document product/version. Do not require bulk downloads.

  7. Astropy licence

    Open-source BSD-3-Clause astronomy tools; selected units, table and FITS helpers are candidates, introduced with explanation.

  8. Matplotlib licence

    Freely usable plotting software under its published licence. Required work uses a local Python/notebook workflow; pinned compatible versions and a tested setup will be supplied before release.
