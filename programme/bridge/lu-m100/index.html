01 / PREPARATION

Catch up where needed.

No calculus or programming is assumed. Recognising basic arithmetic operations and reading a simple table is sufficient to begin; fractions, signed numbers and algebra are rebuilt. A readiness conversation will identify any additional support. Existing qualifications do not automatically establish readiness or earn exemption.

Prepare for Stage 1: M101 Mathematics for physics I alongside P101, followed by M102 alongside A101. This optional 30-credit bridge is outside the 360-credit degree. Take the full module only if needed; targeted preparation requires its own scope and does not earn the full module credit. Completion is not assumed.

The full bridge must justify its 300 hours through mathematical fluency, meaningful physics applications and a thorough scientific Python refresher. Begin with learner-written code, then introduce AI suggestions and bounded coding agents while retaining specification, understanding, independent testing, review and control. No diagnostic or exemption has been awarded.

U01 · Numbers, notation & reliable calculation

Teaching sequence

  1. Signed quantities, number lines and order of operations.
  2. Fractions, decimals, percentages and exact versus approximate answers.
  3. Estimation, rounding and clear written reasoning.
  4. Notebook cells, arithmetic operators, numeric types and named values; predict before executing.
Practice progression
Move from single-step calculations to mixed expressions, then explain and repair a plausible wrong solution. Revisit signs and fractions in later units rather than assuming one successful exercise establishes fluency.
Physical investigation
Compare a short physical timescale with a much longer one using supplied values. Estimate first, calculate second, and explain whether the displayed precision is justified. Scientific notation is introduced fully in U02.
Independent evidence
Without a generated solution, calculate a mixed fraction/sign expression, defend an estimate and correct an order-of-operations error. Write and explain the corresponding short Python calculation.
Misconceptions to address
Confusing a negative value with subtraction; applying operations left to right indiscriminately; treating every displayed decimal as meaningful. Python assignment is not mathematical equality.
Planned visual explanations
Number line for signed quantities; annotated expression showing operation order; exact value and rounded display side by side.
Preparation for what follows
U02 uses reliable arithmetic for powers, compound units and scale. Preserve the distinction between a quantity, its numerical value and the chosen unit.

U02 · Powers, units & proportional reasoning

Teaching sequence

  1. Powers, roots and the restrictions on real-number operations.
  2. Scientific notation, orders of magnitude and SI prefixes.
  3. Ratios, direct/inverse proportionality and compound-unit conversion.
  4. Dimensions and scale factors; distinguish a consistency check from evidence that a physical law is correct.
Practice progression
Alternate abstract power manipulation with unit-bearing calculations. Include a conversion involving squared units and an error caused by mixing units. The two checkpoint hours include the existing one-hour iCMA 41.
Physical investigation
Use a supplied distance–speed–time relation to compare travel times, then a supplied inverse-square relation to predict a change in intensity. State what stays fixed and test whether the change has the expected direction.
Independent evidence
Convert a compound quantity, predict a scaling factor before calculation and explain why a dimensionally compatible relation can still be physically wrong. Write a short named-variable calculation and check it against an estimate.
Misconceptions to address
Scaling an area as though it were a length; confusing an exponent with a multiplier; accepting Python numbers as automatically unit-aware; assuming dimensional compatibility proves correctness.
Planned visual explanations
Conversion chains with cancelling units; comparative length/area scaling diagram; order-of-magnitude ladder.
Preparation for what follows
U03 turns quantitative relations into expressions. Functions and inverse relationships return in Block 2; logarithmic scales wait until U09.

U03 · Algebra as a language

Teaching sequence

  1. Symbols as quantities: variables, constants, parameters and substitution.
  2. Collecting terms, brackets, distributivity and simple factorisation.
  3. Algebraic fractions, cancellation and excluded values.
  4. Identities versus equations; counterexamples; Boolean comparisons and short if/else checks.
Practice progression
Translate between words and symbols, compare expressions, identify invalid transformations and explain a counterexample. Introduce conditions through simple numerical restrictions; general symbolic solvers are not required.
Physical investigation
Interpret a supplied measurement relation with an offset and a scale factor. Track the role and units of each symbol, and diagnose a calculation that applies the offset in the wrong place.
Independent evidence
Simplify an expression with a stated domain, reject invalid cancellation and explain why several matching numerical tests do not prove an identity. Write a small condition that prevents an invalid calculation.
Misconceptions to address
Treating unlike terms as combinable; cancelling across addition; losing excluded inputs; confusing = and ==; assuming agreement at sampled values constitutes proof.
Planned visual explanations
Expression structure annotated by role; an equivalence chain with restrictions kept visible; a small decision diagram for a domain check.
Preparation for what follows
U04 changes an expression into a problem with unknowns. U06 later formalises functions, inputs, outputs and domains; the current measurement context must not assume that teaching has occurred.

U04 · Equations, inequalities & changing the subject

Teaching sequence

  1. Solve linear equations while preserving equality.
  2. Change the subject of a formula; retain restrictions on division and reversible steps.
  3. Solve two simultaneous linear equations; recognise inconsistent and dependent cases.
  4. Solve linear inequalities, represent intervals and reverse the sign correctly when multiplying by a negative.
  5. Use lists, short loops and elementary assertions to check selected candidate values.
Practice progression
Choose a method, show its justification, substitute into the original relation and interpret the answer. Mix earlier fractions, units and algebra into later tasks. Keep code short enough to explain line by line.
Physical investigation
Infer two unknown quantities from two supplied measurement relations, then examine whether the result satisfies a stated bound. All required physical assumptions are supplied; operational procedures or safety decisions are outside scope.
Independent evidence
Rearrange a formula, solve a small system and an inequality, preserving all restrictions. Independently write a loop over candidate values and explain what its checks do—and do not—establish.
Misconceptions to address
Dividing by an expression that could be zero; reversing an inequality unnecessarily or failing to reverse it; treating an underdetermined system as a unique answer; accepting a numerical check instead of the algebraic argument.
Planned visual explanations
Balance interpretation of equivalent operations; interval/number-line diagrams; two independent relations represented as labelled information constraints. Graphical solution follows in U05.
Preparation for what follows
U05 represents the same linear relationships graphically. TMA 01 samples independent calculation, algebra, interpretation and short code before that transition.

U05 · Coordinates, graphs & linear models

Teaching sequence

  1. Coordinates, axis scales and units; read a graph without treating it as a picture of a path.
  2. Slope, intercept and line equations; connect two simultaneous equations to intersecting lines.
  3. Piecewise linear graphs, average rates and secant slopes; interpolation and extrapolation.
  4. Use small NumPy arrays, load a supplied clean CSV with explained columns, and create a labelled plot.
Practice progression
Sketch and predict before plotting. Translate a simple table into a graph and a line equation, then identify a misleading axis choice. Retrieve algebra from Block 1 rather than re-teaching the full solution method.
Physical investigation
Interpret a supplied position–time record and a simple calibration relation. Compare slopes with units and distinguish stationary intervals from changes in direction. No acceleration or calculus is assumed.
Independent evidence
Produce and explain a labelled graph, infer a line relation, calculate an average rate, and justify the limits of extrapolation. Write the short data/plotting code yourself with a supplied library reference.
Misconceptions to address
Confusing height with slope; mixing coordinate and physical units; confusing a motion graph with a spatial route; trusting a smooth plotted line between sparse observations.
Planned visual explanations
Same observations displayed with different axis scales; annotated secant triangle; linked table, equation and graph.
Preparation and boundaries
U06 formalises functions using these representations. Do not spend U06 re-teaching plotting mechanics. Statistical fitting, large datasets and sophisticated file cleaning remain later work.

U06 · Functions, transformations & inverse relationships

Teaching sequence

  1. Function notation, input/output, domain and range; distinguish the mathematical function from the program implementing it.
  2. Composition and the order of operations, with numerical and symbolic examples.
  3. Translations, rescaling, symmetry and simple piecewise/absolute-value functions.
  4. Inverse relationships, one-to-one restrictions and inverse versus reciprocal.
  5. Write short Python functions with arguments, return values, docstrings, local variables and tests.
Practice progression
Translate among words, graphs and formulas; predict a transformation and check it; trace a composition in each order; restrict a domain to make an inverse meaningful. Keep examples within familiar linear, square and absolute-value relationships.
Physical investigation
Convert a sensor reading into a supplied modelled quantity, then reverse the conversion and check the result. Explain any range restriction instead of treating the inverse as a mechanical symbol swap.
Independent evidence
Explain a domain and range, distinguish an inverse from a reciprocal, and write a tested scalar Python function. Separate numerical calculation from plotting and explain why selected tests cannot establish every mathematical property.
Misconceptions to address
Assuming every relation is a function or every function has a global inverse; confusing print with return; confusing mathematical parameters with mutable notebook state.
Planned visual explanations
Linked input/output and graph diagrams; overlays of transformed curves; an inverse shown by exchanging coordinate roles.
Preparation and boundaries
U07 uses function notation, domains and transformations for quadratics and rational relationships. Defer formal limits/continuity, proof-heavy function theory, decorators/classes and generic array-aware validation. The proposed two-hour reduction removes repeated graph introduction, not these core outcomes.

U07 · Quadratic & rational relationships

Teaching sequence

  1. Elementary polynomial operations and quadratic factorisation.
  2. Complete the square; connect vertex form, the quadratic formula and discriminant to a parabola.
  3. Select a root-solving method and check solutions in the original problem.
  4. Rational expressions, excluded inputs and qualitative asymptotes; avoid plotting across an excluded value.
  5. Debug a short calculation systematically; use a guided version-control snapshot, inspect a diff and restore a prior version.
Practice progression
Choose between factorisation, completing the square and the formula rather than applying one method indiscriminately. Include a repeated root and a no-real-root case. Rational examples stay simple; iCMA 42 occupies one of the two checkpoint hours.
Physical investigation
Use a supplied one-dimensional trajectory or geometric constraint to find candidate solutions and decide which satisfy the physical domain. State the model assumptions; do not derive mechanics that P101 has not taught.
Independent evidence
Solve and interpret a quadratic, check against a sketch and substitution, and explain an excluded input. Repair a faulty plot or calculation and show what changed. A software-generated root is not a substitute for the algebraic argument.
Misconceptions to address
Choosing both roots without checking the physical interval; interpreting a plotting line across a singularity as real behaviour; confusing a zero with an excluded input.
Planned visual explanations
Factored, expanded and vertex forms linked to one parabola; discriminant cases; a rational plot broken at its excluded input.
Preparation and boundaries
TMA 02 checks U05–U07 before U08. Keep rational functions qualitative: no partial fractions, general polynomial long division or formal limit analysis. The proposed two-hour reduction limits extension practice, not quadratic fluency. Version control is a small guided routine, not a full software-development course.

U08 · Geometry, angles & trigonometric relationships

Teaching sequence

  1. Retrieve similarity and Pythagoras; use area, circle and volume formulas with units.
  2. Understand radians through arc length and connect degrees, radians and dimensionless ratios.
  3. Build sine, cosine and tangent from the unit circle, with signs, special angles and right-triangle interpretation.
  4. Solve triangles using appropriate sine/cosine rules; recognise when supplied data can give two triangles.
  5. Read periodic graphs, amplitude and period; use inverse trig carefully and solve elementary equations on a stated interval.
  6. Plot periodic functions in radians, check special cases, then critique one supplied faulty AI suggestion.
Practice progression
Progress from a labelled diagram to method choice and interpretation. Distribute mixed angle/triangle/graph practice across the unit. Require the meaning of sin²θ+cos²θ=1 and tanθ=sinθ/cosθ where defined; defer extensive identity manipulation and general solution families.
Physical investigation
Estimate a distance from a simple triangulation arrangement and interpret a supplied oscillation relation. Distinguish geometric assumptions from measured information; use consistent angle units and explain ambiguity rather than selecting a convenient answer.
Independent evidence
Construct and label a diagram, solve a triangle with a justified method, interpret a periodic graph and check a radians-based program. Independently complete the core problem before reviewing an AI-produced degree/radian error; the review is assisted-code critique, not autonomous agent operation.
Misconceptions to address
Mixing degrees and radians; treating inverse trig output as the only possible angle; forgetting quadrants or domains; assuming a sketch is to scale; treating period and amplitude as the same parameter.
Planned visual explanations
Arc-length/radius explanation of radians; unit circle connected to a sine graph; possible ambiguous triangles; side-by-side period and amplitude changes.
Preparation and boundaries
U10 uses these ideas for vector components and direction. Trigonometric calculus and complex-exponential methods remain in degree mathematics. U08 evidence feeds TMA 03 after U09–U10, not TMA 02. AI critique stays one bounded exercise within the three Python hours; no new tool setup or extra agent workflow is introduced here.

It assesses graphs, functions and quadratics using learner-written code; library documentation is allowed, generated solutions are not. It does not assess U08 trigonometry. U08 is assessed later in TMA 03 and the EMA. Tutoring and AI-review practice remain separate from independent evidence.

U09 · Exponential change, logarithms & scale

Teaching sequence

  1. Repeated multiplicative change and exponential functions; distinguish exponential from power-law behaviour.
  2. Logarithms as inverses, laws and bases; solve simple exponential equations with valid arguments.
  3. Ordinary, semilog and log–log axes; interpret slope and parameters in the appropriate representation.
  4. Specify inputs, outputs, domains and tests before reviewing a small supplied code suggestion or refactor.
Practice progression
Alternate symbolic manipulation, scale reading and interpretation. Choose an axis representation for a stated purpose and recover a parameter by substitution. Every logarithm must have a positive, dimensionless argument, using a reference quantity where necessary.
Physical investigation
Use supplied half-life or intensity-ratio relations to compare predictions and a small dataset. Explain assumptions; do not derive a differential equation or claim a straight transformed plot alone validates a physical model.
Evidence
Solve an exponential equation, choose and label a suitable plot and check a recovered parameter. Independently write the central calculation, then assess a suggested change against an explicit specification.
Misconceptions to address
Confusing powers with exponentials; taking a log of a dimensional or non-positive quantity; reading logarithmic spacing as additive; trusting a visual straight line as proof.
Planned visual explanations
Linear/semilog/log–log views of the same quantities; repeated-factor diagram; inverse relationship connecting exponentiation and logarithms.
Preparation and boundaries
U12 can use scaling or exponential models. Formal calculus and differential-equation derivations remain in degree mathematics. This unit develops specifications and change review, not a new agent platform.

U10 · Vectors & the geometry of direction

Teaching sequence

  1. Scalars and vectors; coordinate conventions and two-dimensional components.
  2. Addition, subtraction, scaling, magnitude, direction and unit-vector notation.
  3. Cartesian/polar conversion; projections and the geometric dot product.
  4. Implement vectors as small arrays; independently check diagrams, components and limiting cases.
  5. Inspect one supplied agent trace: task, file changes, tests, failure and recovery.
Practice progression
Move repeatedly between diagrams and components. Check zero vectors, opposing/equal vectors and direction conventions. Use quadrant-aware direction handling; distinguish an undefined direction from a numerical zero.
Physical investigation
Combine supplied displacement vectors and analyse a projection. All physical interpretations are explained. This is mathematical modelling with small synthetic examples, not operational navigation or a procedure for real-world decisions.
Evidence
A labelled diagram, justified component calculation and magnitude/direction check. Explain a dot product geometrically. Identify an unrequested or incorrect change in an agent trace and describe an independent check and rollback.
Misconceptions to address
Adding magnitudes instead of components; losing the quadrant; confusing a vector with its norm; treating a program’s successful execution as physical validation.
Planned visual explanations
Head-to-tail and component constructions; projection onto a stated direction; labelled comparison of a vector and its magnitude.
Preparation and boundaries
TMA 03 samples U08–U10 mathematical reasoning. Propose moving actual agent operation to U12 so it belongs to a guided investigation. Trace critique here is observation, not evidence of hands-on competence. Three-dimensional products and vector-space theory remain later.

U11 · Data, variation & elementary probability

Teaching sequence

  1. Data types, units and provenance; repeated observations versus independent evidence.
  2. Mean, median and descriptive spread, including the meaning and stated convention of standard deviation.
  3. Precision/accuracy, random/systematic effects and limits of a small dataset.
  4. Finite sample spaces, complements and simple independent events.
  5. Load a small documented dataset, detect missing/invalid values and compare library summaries with selected hand calculations.
Practice progression
Use small inspectable examples, including an outlier and a missing observation. Explain whether a reported standard deviation describes the supplied values or estimates population spread, and state the convention. Keep probability finite and elementary. One of the two checkpoint hours is the existing iCMA 43.
Physical investigation
Describe repeated measurements and discuss what variation does and does not establish. Do not equate the spread of observations with uncertainty of the mean or attempt formal statistical inference.
Evidence
A short data note with units, provenance, summaries and limitations; a hand-checked statistic; a finite-probability calculation with assumptions. Explain why removing a value needs a reason.
Misconceptions to address
Silently discarding missing data; confusing precision and accuracy; claiming independence without justification; reporting a library default without understanding its convention.
Planned visual explanations
Raw observations paired with a distribution view; mean/median response to an outlier; simple finite sample-space diagram.
Preparation and boundaries
U12 uses cautious data interpretation and reproducible loading. Conditional probability, continuous distributions, inference, uncertainty propagation and weighted fitting need explicit later homes in the coverage register. Fourteen hours is introductory scope, not a complete statistics refresher.

U12 · From a question to a defensible model

Teaching sequence

  1. Frame a bounded question and choose among already taught relationships; identify assumptions and available evidence.
  2. Build a learner-written baseline calculation and compare it with supplied data using appropriate plots.
  3. Plan independent checks, explain limitations and organise a reproducible notebook.
  4. Operate one tightly bounded coding-agent task in a disposable copy of the prepared project, then inspect the diff, validate independently and practise rollback.
  5. Rerun from a clean state and explain every retained calculation, source and assistance used.
Practice progression
This is a guided formative rehearsal, with no new mathematical topic. Feedback supports the process. Preserve an unaided baseline before any assistance and keep the delegated change small enough to inspect fully.
Physical investigation
One coherent geometric, scaling or exponential investigation using a small documented dataset. Do not force every module topic into one artificial story. Choose a different problem/data instance for the later EMA so this is preparation rather than a rehearsed assessed answer.
Evidence
A reproducible formative notebook and explained model choices. For hands-on agent evidence retain the bounded request, relevant change record, independent checks and rollback demonstration. A supplied trace alone cannot establish that capability.
Misconceptions to address
Building a model around a desired answer; delegating the scientific assumptions; accepting a green test without an independent check; confusing a notebook that ran once with a reproducible result.
Planned visual explanations
Question–model–evidence diagram; model/data comparison with units and captions; compact annotated change review where it helps explain the computational decision.
Preparation and boundaries
The five computing hours propose two hours for reproducibility/clean-run work and three for the prepared agent exercise, diff review and rollback. Tool setup must fit existing orientation/retrieval allowances, with a verified genuinely free workflow on the learner’s machine before release. If this cannot be delivered within scope, revise the plan openly; do not silently replace hands-on operation with watching a trace. The EMA follows as independent evidence.

B01 · Calculate and express relationships

Build reliable calculation and algebra, alongside learner-written Python calculations, conditions, loops and checks.

U01 · 16 HOURS · DRAFT

Numbers, notation & reliable calculation

Preparation: Module entry readiness.

Scope
Signed numbers; operation order; fractions, decimals and percentages; absolute value; number lines; exact versus approximate values; rounding and estimation. Introduce mathematical statements and the difference between an example and a general claim.
By the end
Calculate with fractions and signs, justify an estimate and explain the precision retained in a result.
Computing
Open and save a notebook; run arithmetic cells; distinguish mathematical notation from Python operators and a displayed approximation from an exact value.
Physics context
Compare everyday and astronomical numerical scales without requiring astronomy knowledge.
Evidence
Short hand calculations with estimates and an explained computer cross-check.
Boundary
Powers, roots and scientific notation are developed next; floating-point error analysis belongs later.

U02 · 18 HOURS · DRAFT

Powers, units & proportional reasoning

Preparation: U01.

Scope
Integer and rational powers, roots and real-number restrictions; scientific notation; ratios and direct/inverse proportionality; SI prefixes, compound-unit conversions, dimensions and scale factors.
By the end
Compare scales, convert a derived quantity consistently and test a proposed relation for dimensional compatibility.
Computing
Use named variables and math functions; attach units in names, labels and explanations rather than treating plain numbers as automatically unit-aware.
Physics context
Light-travel times and inverse-square scaling with all physical relations supplied.
Evidence
A unit conversion and a scaling argument with an order-of-magnitude check.
Boundary
Dimensional consistency is a necessary check, not proof of a physical law. Dimensional-analysis theorems are deferred.

U03 · 22 HOURS · DRAFT

Algebra as a language

Preparation: U01, U02.

Scope
Variables, constants and parameters; substitution; collecting terms; distributivity, brackets and simple factorisation; algebraic fractions; equivalent expressions and excluded values; identities versus equations.
By the end
Translate a verbal relationship into symbols and simplify it without changing its domain unnoticed.
Computing
Evaluate two proposed expressions at selected inputs and recognise that numerical agreement does not prove an identity.
Physics context
Rearrangements used in measurement formulas, with no new physics assumed.
Evidence
A justified simplification and a counterexample to an invalid cancellation.
Boundary
Full quadratic factorisation is reserved for U07; symbolic-algebra software is not required.

U04 · 24 HOURS · DRAFT

Equations, inequalities & changing the subject

Preparation: U03.

Scope
Linear equations; formulas with several variables; two simultaneous linear equations; inequalities and intervals; sign changes; extraneous solutions; checking answers by substitution.
By the end
Choose a solution method, retain restrictions and distinguish no solution, one solution and an underdetermined relationship.
Computing
Use Boolean comparisons to check candidates, alongside a written algebraic argument.
Physics context
Infer two unknown quantities from two supplied measurement relations.
Evidence
An independently explained formula rearrangement, system of equations and inequality.
Boundary
General matrix solution methods belong to M101/M102. Nonlinear root-finding algorithms are deferred.

B02 · Understand functions and models

Connect graphs, functions and geometry; handle small datasets, write functions and tests, and begin critically reviewing assistance.

U05 · 20 HOURS · DRAFT

Coordinates, graphs & linear models

Preparation: U02, U04.

Scope
Axes, scales and units; ordered pairs; slope and intercept; line equations and intersections; piecewise linear graphs; secant slopes and average rates; interpolation versus extrapolation.
By the end
Recover and interpret a linear relationship from a graph or table and identify when a visual presentation is misleading.
Computing
Create a labelled plot with supplied plotting scaffolding; introduce lists or arrays only as needed to represent coordinate pairs.
Physics context
Position–time and calibration plots: distinguish a graph of a quantity from a picture of motion.
Evidence
A graph, its mathematical model and a plain-language interpretation with units.
Boundary
A secant slope is not a derivative. Least-squares fitting and inferential claims belong to X201.

U06 · 22 HOURS · DRAFT

Functions, transformations & inverse relationships

Preparation: U03, U05.

Scope
Function notation, inputs and outputs, domain and range; multiple representations; composition; translations and rescaling; symmetry; piecewise and absolute-value functions; one-to-one restrictions and inverses.
By the end
Trace a composition, specify a meaningful domain and distinguish an inverse function from a reciprocal.
Computing
Define a small Python function, generate values and check behaviour against a sketch and known cases.
Physics context
Convert between a sensor reading and a modelled physical quantity.
Evidence
A function interpreted in three representations and an inverse with a justified domain.
Boundary
Limits and continuity become explicit concepts in M101; no hidden calculus requirement here.

U07 · 20 HOURS · DRAFT

Quadratic & rational relationships

Preparation: U04, U06.

Scope
Polynomial arithmetic at elementary level; quadratic factorisation, completing the square and quadratic formula; discriminants and real roots; parabolas; rational expressions, excluded inputs and qualitative asymptotes.
By the end
Select a suitable quadratic method and connect its solutions to a graph, while checking the original equation.
Computing
Compare a plot with analytical roots and investigate a plotting artefact near an excluded input.
Physics context
Geometric constraints and supplied trajectory formulas; no mechanics derivation is assumed.
Evidence
A quadratic problem solved and checked in two representations; a rational-domain explanation.
Boundary
Complex roots, general polynomial theory and partial fractions are later work. Asymptotes are qualitative here.

U08 · 28 HOURS · DRAFT

Geometry, angles & trigonometric relationships

Preparation: U02, U06, U07.

Scope
Similarity and Pythagoras; circle, area and volume formulas; degrees and radians; unit-circle sine, cosine and tangent; triangle methods including sine/cosine rules; basic identities; periodic graphs; inverse trig and elementary trig equations on stated intervals.
By the end
Choose a geometric construction, resolve a triangle and interpret a periodic graph with a correct angle convention.
Computing
Plot periodic functions using radians and check special angles and period against hand reasoning.
Physics context
Sightlines, triangulation and a supplied oscillation model.
Evidence
A labelled geometric diagram and a justified trigonometric solution, including ambiguous cases where relevant.
Boundary
Advanced identities, complex-exponential representations and calculus of trig functions are deferred. The approved 28-hour allocation gives this dense unit more practice; observed timing still needs review.

B03 · Investigate and explain

Combine exponential, vector and data reasoning in reproducible investigations, with controlled assistance and independent explanations.

U09 · 22 HOURS · DRAFT

Exponential change, logarithms & scale

Preparation: U02, U04, U06.

Scope
Exponential functions and repeated factors; logarithms as inverses, bases and laws; simple exponential equations; logarithmic axes; linearisation of supplied exponential and power-law models; positive, dimensionless log arguments.
By the end
Distinguish additive, multiplicative and power-law change, and interpret the scale and restrictions of a logarithmic plot.
Computing
Compare ordinary, semilog and log–log plots; check a recovered parameter by substitution.
Physics context
Half-life and brightness-ratio models, with their physical assumptions stated rather than derived.
Evidence
Interpret a model parameter and explain why a particular axis scale is useful.
Boundary
Differential-equation derivations of exponential behaviour and derivatives of logarithms belong later.

U10 · 18 HOURS · DRAFT

Vectors & the geometry of direction

Preparation: U05, U08.

Scope
Scalars versus vectors; two-dimensional components; addition, subtraction and scaling; magnitude and direction; unit-vector notation; Cartesian/polar conversion; projections and the geometric dot product.
By the end
Move between a diagram and components, combine vectors and check a projection using geometry.
Computing
Represent vectors as small arrays and compare component calculations with a labelled diagram.
Physics context
Displacement and vector quantities in a supplied model; physical laws are taught in P101.
Evidence
A diagram, component solution and independent magnitude/direction check.
Boundary
Three-dimensional cross products, vector spaces, eigenvectors and vector calculus belong to M101/M102/M201.

U11 · 14 HOURS · DRAFT

Data, variation & elementary probability

Preparation: U02, U05, U06.

Scope
Data types; mean, median and descriptive spread including standard deviation; simple distributions and plots; samples versus populations; systematic versus random effects; finite sample spaces, complements and simple independent events.
By the end
Describe a small dataset honestly, distinguish precision from accuracy and interpret a simple probability without claiming certainty.
Computing
Summarise and plot supplied repeated observations, explain every reported quantity and compare selected calculations by hand.
Physics context
Repeated measurements and their variation. Observed spread is not automatically the uncertainty of a mean.
Evidence
A descriptive data note and a finite-probability calculation with stated assumptions.
Boundary
Conditional probability, continuous distributions, inference, uncertainty propagation and weighted fitting must be allocated later; this is orientation, not a statistics course.

U12 · 16 HOURS · DRAFT

From a question to a defensible model

Preparation: U07, U08, U09, U10, U11.

Scope
Combine existing tools to define a question, select representations, state assumptions, compare model and supplied observations, test limiting cases and explain limitations. No new mathematical topic is introduced.
By the end
Explain why a model is suitable, what its results mean and what the available evidence cannot establish.
Computing
Organise a small notebook with headings, readable calculations, labelled figures and a clean rerun; record source and units of supplied data.
Physics context
A guided geometric or scaling investigation linking a concrete observation with an abstract relationship.
Evidence
A formative model review and oral or written explanation of method choices.
Boundary
This rehearses integration; the independently assessed notebook has its own separate workload allocation.
