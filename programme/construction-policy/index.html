PHYSICS / PUBLIC COMMITMENT

Coherence by construction.

Every teaching section belongs to a larger purpose. We commit to planning the surrounding structure before developing its details, and to reviewing the whole when those details reveal a problem.

Adopted 25 September 2026 · Policy 1.0

01 / PRINCIPLE

Plan before elaborating.

Before developing a component in detail, establish its parent’s complete structure, blueprint its peers, and verify its dependencies and contribution to the wider programme.

Our hierarchy is Degree → Stage → Module → Block → Unit → Lesson → Section. Blueprinting specifies a component’s purpose, structure and interfaces; authoring produces its explanations, examples, activities and feedback. These are separate activities: planning all lessons is not writing all lessons.

This commitment applies whenever we construct, revise or release curriculum and teaching materials. It is a working review policy, not a claim that software tests can certify educational quality.

02 / CONSTRUCTION CONDITIONS

A clear threshold at every level.

Recursive construction conditions
To begin…Required surrounding blueprint
Author a sectionEvery section in its lesson is blueprinted; every lesson across its block is blueprinted.
Blueprint a lesson’s sectionsEvery lesson across its block is blueprinted, and the unit’s outcomes, workload and assessment role are settled.
Blueprint a unit’s lessonsEvery unit across its module is blueprinted; every block across its stage is mapped.
Blueprint a block’s unitsEvery block across its module is blueprinted; every module across its stage has defined outcomes, boundaries and workload.
Blueprint a module’s blocksEvery module across its stage is mapped; the degree’s stage progression and core/specialist boundaries are established.
Blueprint a stage’s modulesEvery stage across the degree has defined purposes, exit expectations, credit budgets and progression requirements.

“Every” refers to the named enclosing scope, not the whole degree. Every lesson in a block means lessons in all its units. Distant stages may retain explicitly provisional maps; their current purposes, boundaries and interfaces must still be clear. We do not need every final-stage lesson before writing an introductory section.

The optional M100 bridge sits outside the degree. Apply its lesson, unit and block conditions normally; use its entry-preparation role and the receiving Stage 1 as the wider context. This does not place M100 inside Stage 1 or count its credits towards the degree.

03 / BLUEPRINT CONTENT

More than a list of titles.

Minimum blueprint information at the relevant resolution
FieldRequired information
Purpose and outcomesWhat this component contributes and what the learner should be able to do.
BoundariesWhat belongs here, what belongs elsewhere, and which repetitions deliberately deepen or retrieve earlier learning.
DependenciesWhat must already be taught or demonstrated, with identifiable source components and ordering.
WorkloadIts share of the parent budget, including explanation, practice, computing and assessment preparation; never add the same hours twice.
EvidenceHow the outcomes will be checked, at the appropriate level of detail.
HandoverWhat subsequent components may assume and what remains for later study.

Use identifiers and explicit links where available. A proposed map, an approved scope and released teaching must remain visibly different states. Unknowns must be recorded; a gap relevant to the proposed work cannot be labelled complete simply to pass a gate.

04 / DEPENDENCIES & CHANGE

Follow the consequences.

Dependencies can require more detail than the minimum. If a physics unit uses mathematics from a concurrent module, identify the teaching component, required outcome and order of availability. A broad module title does not settle the dependency.

Changes trigger a review of affected components. When drafting reveals a missing prerequisite or unrealistic workload, update the blueprint and check its parent, peers, predecessors and successors. Review Section → Lesson → Unit → Block → Module → Stage → Degree, in greater detail close to the work and more broadly farther away. Record the impact on coverage, assessment and hours; do not quietly insert unbudgeted teaching or alter saved timetables.

If a construction condition is not met, return to planning the missing structure. Exploratory notes can inform that plan; they must not be presented as approved or released teaching. Preserve prior versions and record the reason for a policy or scope revision.

05 / RELEASE

Construction readiness is not publication readiness.

A component can pass its construction gate and still need revision before publication. Release review checks scientific and mathematical accuracy, code behaviour, prerequisites, incremental pedagogy, worked examples, exercises and feedback, figures and captions, accessibility, free references and reuse rights, workload and assessment alignment. Apply checks relevant to the piece; document any unresolved issue.

For each construction or material revision, retain a concise review record: component and parent; policy version and applicable gate; links to surrounding blueprints; dependency and budget checks; construction disposition; separate release disposition; evidence, unresolved issues and forward handover. Date the review and identify the actual reviewer without implying independent review that did not occur. Generic records belong with public curriculum sources; personal learning evidence remains private.

06 / CURRENT APPLICATION

A stronger starting condition.

Before the first M100 teaching section, our agreed starting threshold additionally requires Stage 1 block-and-unit blueprints, a review of later-stage progression, every lesson across M100 Block 1, and every section in its opening lesson. Existing orientation tutoring does not replace those blueprints or bypass the condition.

See the current blueprint audit for actual planning status and next decisions, and the coverage register for unresolved subject homes and depth. The existence of this policy does not mean those conditions have already been satisfied.
