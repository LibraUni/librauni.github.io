# Curriculum coherence and authoring

Agreed 24 September 2026. Apply whenever designing, drafting, revising or releasing teaching material.

## Work from the smallest piece outwards

Review Section → Lesson → Unit → Block → Module → Stage → Degree. A section is a subdivision of a lesson, not another enrolment or credit-bearing entity. M100 is an optional bridge outside the degree: review its contribution to Stage 1 readiness without counting it as degree coverage.

Start with the approved parent outline, relevant outcomes, prerequisites, workload allowance and the previous piece's handover. Draft only the currently authorised piece. Review it from inside out before release; repeat the relevant checks after revisions and after learner feedback reveals a problem.

| Scale | Review depth and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Section | Detailed: correctness of every claim, derivation, numerical result and code example; clear notation, units and assumptions; appropriate prerequisites; worked examples, practice and feedback; usable diagrams, captions and alternative text; references, rights and accessible presentation. |
| Lesson | Detailed: a coherent learning objective, gradual progression, independent-study support, sufficient practice and evidence, realistic workload, connection to preceding and following sections. |
| Unit | Detailed: contribution to unit outcomes and assessment, conceptual and computational dependencies, consistency of notation, intentional retrieval versus unnecessary repetition, gaps and cumulative workload. |
| Block | Check how units build towards the block's purpose, integrate skills and prepare for subsequent work. Draft only the next relevant unit's outline, not distant lessons. |
| Module | Check outcomes, assessment evidence, boundaries, prerequisite coverage and the total credit budget. Capture implications for later blocks at outline level. |
| Stage | Check readiness and concurrent-module timing, the balance of theory and practice, and ownership of topics. Capture implications for future modules at role/outcome level. |
| Degree | Check contribution to core breadth, depth, scientific practice and progression; consult the coverage register and specialist-path boundaries. Record broad implications for later stages without inventing detailed content or equivalence. |

High confidence at immediate levels requires evidence, not a declaration that the content fits perfectly. Use worked checks, reproducible runs, source verification and explicit outcome mappings as appropriate. Record uncertainties and unresolved limits honestly. Structural tests cannot certify academic quality.

## Feed the review forward

Each review should inform the next section, lesson, unit, block, module and stage, with progressively less specificity farther ahead. Record what the learner is being prepared to do, the required prior evidence, the conceptual connection, and any new dependency or gap. Keep the agreed top-down architecture as the constraint; detailed teaching experience improves it through visible, justified revisions.

Do not mass-produce distant materials. Do not quietly change agreed scope, credits, module roles or assessment requirements to accommodate a local draft. Where an issue crosses boundaries, identify the affected outlines, coverage rows and timetables, propose the correction at the relevant level, and recheck affected descendants and successors. Keep confirmed decisions distinct from provisional planning. Preserve published identifiers and private progress records when implementing changes.

## A short authoring record

Keep a concise record alongside the source for each authored or materially revised piece:

- **Identity and purpose:** section/lesson ID and parent unit, block and module; applicable outcome IDs; intended contribution.
- **Inputs:** assumed knowledge, where it was taught, and any retrieval/support needed.
- **Immediate review:** mathematical/scientific/code checks; pedagogy and practice; figures/accessibility; free references/licensing; estimated hours and allocation.
- **Wider fit:** block/module role, assessment links, stage readiness and degree coverage; intentional revisits, gaps and exclusions.
- **Forward handover:** next section in detail; next lesson as a plan; next unit as an outline; later block/module/stage implications at progressively broader scope.
- **Disposition:** draft, needs revision, or ready for release; unresolved issues, evidence checked and date. Do not invent a reviewer or claim independent review where none occurred.

Store generic curricular review with public course sources. Store learner-specific difficulties, answers, performance and tutor notes only in private records. Learner feedback can inform public improvements without exposing personal records.
