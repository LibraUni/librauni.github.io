# Academic records that are useful to their recipient

Required tutor review before every future academic archive/journal entry. The category descriptions on /evidence/ are the public contract. Record substantive learning, assessment and evidence, not activity volume. A recipient should understand what happened, what it shows, and its limits without reading the original chat.

## Acceptance questions

1. **Purpose:** What concrete question could this record answer for its recipient? Identify the academic event or change and the appropriate category.
2. **Context:** State the module and task/topic, the relevant question or brief, and the attempt/version or draft/submitted status. Do not export unexplained “exercise 4” or “correct again”. Preserve units, assumptions and marking scales where relevant.
3. **Evidence:** Identify the original work, observed response or source supporting the statement, and link archived originals/related records. Separate what the learner demonstrated, what they reported, and the tutor’s interpretation. State material hints, calculator/code/AI assistance or other conditions. Never infer independent mastery from assisted success.
4. **Time:** Preserve the actual event date when known and the recording date separately. Label unknown or approximate dates; do not manufacture them. Blockchain dates remain distinct.
5. **Usefulness:** Explain the result, change, difficulty, correction or next step in concise concrete language. Include only fields relevant to this event; do not pad each entry with a fixed narrative template.
6. **Sharing:** Read it as a standalone recipient would. Include contextual fields in the record and link evidence in other categories. Full records must be intelligible; a deliberately isolated field may need companion context. Do not silently add unselected private material to an export.

## Category-specific requirements

| Category | Required meaning and recipient use |
| --- | --- |
| Submissions | Actual submitted work and its task/version, attempt, submission date and conditions. Formal receipt when applicable. Shows what was handed in; drafts remain Activities. |
| Assessments | Assessment identity, criteria/scale, actual result and its supporting work. Distinguish diagnostic/formative/summative and provisional/final. Explains assessed performance. |
| Feedback | Specific issue or strength in identified work, the correction/explanation and actionable next step when needed. Links later revision when available. Shows review and response. |
| Skills | A named capability supported by a concrete observed task, conditions and scope. Distinguish emerging, assisted and independently demonstrated performance; use approved standards where applicable. Helps a recipient judge the evidenced capability. |
| Milestones | A specific achieved academic checkpoint, date, criteria and evidence. No milestones for mere clicks, elapsed time or administrative setup. Shows meaningful progression. |
| Certificates | Only actually issued awards with PDF, award ID/version, scope, issuer, dates, applicable evidenced grade and supporting assessment links. Preserve correction/status history. Shows completed study. |
| Activities | Identified practice, investigations, code, drafts or sketches with purpose, version/status and any observed outcome. Shows practical work and development; do not label it formally submitted or graded unless it is. |
| Journal | Substantive dated academic observations integrating what changed, evidence, difficulties and next steps. Shows a learning trajectory, not an administrative diary or a duplicate of all other categories. |
| Tutorials | Academic questions, explanations and learner reasoning with task context and assistance visible. Distinguish concise summary from actual transcript; preserve available source transcripts without inventing missing turns. Shows how understanding developed and what support was provided. |

## What to omit or combine

Omit empty praise, “studied today” without useful substance, repetitive status updates, website/administration discussions and unsupported conclusions. Combine related small exchanges into one coherent academic event. Link a shared source across categories rather than repeating its full text. A mistake, failed attempt or unresolved misconception can be valuable evidence and should be recorded when academically meaningful.

Proactive preservation of supplied academic originals still applies. Archive originals with honest provenance and provisional classification if needed; do not invent an achievement just to accompany a file. This rule governs future entry quality, not retrospective deletion. Correct existing inaccuracies by linked append-only corrections, retaining prior versions and snapshots.

## Review responsibility

Before running either tutor archival uploader, perform this review on the private input. Structural validators only check data shape; they cannot establish recipient usefulness, academic truth or completeness. Before sharing, recommend the contextual fields/files needed for the recipient’s purpose while preserving explicit selective disclosure. Do not log this policy discussion as academic learning.
